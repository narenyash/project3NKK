In my academic pursuits, I am driven by a fervent desire to enhance our capacity to eliminate errors in software programming. To achieve this objective, I aspire to embark on a PhD journey in Computer Science. My research strategy revolves around furnishing static guarantees for programs through a twofold approach: firstly, describing programs via type systems; and secondly, formulating proofs about these programs' properties. In the following narrative, I delve into the experiences that have shaped my research focus and steered me towards Programming Language (PL) theory, Type Theory, and Proof Assistants.

Normalization by Evaluation (Spring 2023)

My fascination with dependently typed programming languages, such as Agda, stems from their ability to provide more robust static guarantees for programs, along with their potential for proof generation. To master Agda as a proof assistant, I immersed myself in "Programming Languages Foundations in Agda" outside of class hours. This textbook, composed entirely within Agda, aims to make it more accessible for students. Inspired by this resource, I authored an online tutorial on Normalization-by-Evaluation (NbE)—an algorithm used to create a type checker for languages with dependent types in Agda. In the tutorial, I demonstrate how to implement NbE and validate its accuracy. This work is based on Andreas Abel’s habilitation thesis, which I extensively studied as part of a graduate-level course. By proving the correctness of NbE, I was introduced to the intriguing topic of Logical Relations, which led to further collaboration with Prof. Stephanie Weirich.

Making Logical Relations More Accessible (Summer 2023)

Logical relations serve as a versatile tool for validating properties about programs, such as the static guarantee that a well-typed program is terminating. Although proof assistants have become increasingly common in teaching Programming Language topics, there remains a dearth of mechanized tutorials on logical relations due to their tedious mechanical implementation. Prof. Weirich and I hypothesize this scarcity is because proofs by Logical Relations are notoriously laborious to mechanize within a proof assistant. To address this gap, we devised a proof that can be employed in a mechanized setting to introduce Logical Relations. Our proof establishes that if a term in the Lambda Calculus possesses a type, its evaluation is well-defined—a well-known result with our proof's notable characteristic of avoiding reasoning about substitutions. This approach facilitates the mechanical implementation without sacrificing emphasis on Logical Relations. As the first author, I spearheaded a paper detailing this proof in Agda, which serves as a mechanized tutorial on Logical Relations and is planned for submission to ICFP 2024 as a Functional Pearl. In the future, I am eager to delve deeper into the study of Logical Relations and their applications in establishing static guarantees for programs. I have already commenced work on an example proof concerning an extension to Call-by-Push-Value (CBPV).

Effects and Coeffects in Call-By-Push-Value (Fall 2023)

Call-By-Push-Value (CBPV) is a computation model that offers insights into the behavior of programs featuring effects (e.g., input/output operations). In collaboration with Prof. Weirich and others, we have observed that CBPV is equally appropriate for investigating coeffects—the environmental demands a program places (e.g., resource utilization). Coeffects and their interactions with CBPV share an inverse relationship, making this the central theme of a paper we submitted to OOPSLA 2024 for review. Our paper encompasses a type-and-effect system for CBPV that dynamically tracks the effects of a computation, accompanied by an operational semantics augmented with effects. Among our findings are effect soundness: the actual effects of a program at runtime are accurately represented by our type-and-effect system. Our effects system serves as a foundation for our approach to coeffects, and we present parallel results for a coeffects system. My principal contribution involved the effects portion of the paper. In addition, I mechanized all of the results for our effect system in Agda.
