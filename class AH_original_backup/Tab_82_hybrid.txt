My academic and professional journey has led me to a profound interest in investigating the principles of machine learning, specifically focusing on probabilistic foundations for deep learning and causal deep learning as applied to natural language processing (NLP) with a social impact. With a robust undergraduate research background in deep learning and NLP, publications in premier NLP conferences, and two years of work experience at Meta in applied research for Bayesian inference and causal inference, I am eager to embark on a Ph.D. journey at Columbia University. I firmly believe that this next step will empower me to contribute significantly to the responsible and equitable development of AI technologies that positively transform human lives.

Intrigued Questions and Areas of Investigation
1. Probabilistic foundations for deep learning: How can we effectively incorporate human knowledge into prior distributions for Bayesian deep models, particularly in areas with vague expert opinions or limited data availability? Can we leverage uncertainty quantification to foster fairness and accountability in deep learning applications?
2. Causal deep learning: Can the integration of causal knowledge make deep models more robust against adversarial attacks or domain shifts? How can we optimally exploit or induce causal structures to create deeper understanding, fairness, and transparency in deep learning models? What principled methods can be developed for learning causal relationships from large volumes of text data with partial understanding or limited causal information?

Background & Motivation
During my undergraduate career, I was fortunate enough to publish several first-author papers in top conferences on deep learning and NLP for social good, under the guidance of Dr. Mohit Bansal at UNC Chapel Hill. These projects included detecting linguistic characteristics of Alzheimer's disease and classifying sexual harassment online, earning me the 2020 CRA Outstanding Undergraduate Research Award as the first runner-up. My skills were further honed through internships at MITRE, Disney, Yelp, and Facebook, where I observed instances of misused or misunderstood deep learning models. For example, while working at Disney, neural network classifiers with high confidence yet incorrect predictions failed to account for uncertainty in demographics with scarce data representation, leading to severe underperformance among marginalized racial groups. At Yelp, a high correlation between an advertiser feature and advertiser survival was mistakenly interpreted as causation, potentially resulting in detrimental changes to marketing practices. These encounters, combined with learning about the importance of fairness and model critique from Dr. Been Kim through the Google AI Research mentorship program, ignited my curiosity about the technical limitations of standard deep learning.

Neural networks inherently lack the ability to produce uncertainty estimates, distinguish between epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, or seamlessly incorporate prior beliefs or expert opinions. As the world increasingly relies on AI to address sensitive and pressing issues, insights into causal structures and counterfactual modes of explanation become indispensable.

At Meta, I worked as a research scientist within the Bayesian Modeling and Probabilistic Programming Languages team, where I approached machine learning through a statistical lens. Leading our team's causal inference research pillar, I emphasized the benefits of probabilistic foundations and causal understanding. For my doctoral training, I aspire to build on this foundation by exploring how best to apply the advantages of Bayesian and causal inference to deep learning and NLP. Text offers a rich medium for offering insights and impact across a wide range of applications with significant societal impact, such as medical sciences, poverty alleviation, sustainability, and community safety—both online and offline. My research interests reflect my enthusiasm to improve deep NLP models by integrating more human knowledge in the form of priors and/or causal structures while enhancing their ability to handle uncertainty, robustness, and explainability.

Probabilistic foundations for deep learning:
My first paper with Dr. Bansal used three neural networks (CNNs, LSTMs, and CNN-RNNs) to aid in the early detection of Alzheimer's disease by classifying and analyzing transcripts of patients' speech. This work was presented at NAACL 2018 and featured in Sebastian Ruder’s Top Paper Picks. The neural networks achieved new independent classification benchmark accuracy, and we conducted interpretability analysis to understand the distinctive language patterns of Alzheimer's patients. During the exploration of misclassified model examples, I found that many required specific prior knowledge about various language customs or an understanding of disease progression stages. Bayesian deep models—such as deep Gaussian Processes, Variational Autoencoders, or Bayesian Neural Networks—already utilize priors while retaining the power and flexibility of deep networks. However, these models traditionally rely on complex or intricately derived priors, such as weight-space priors or function-space priors, making it unclear how to translate expert knowledge like language customs into tractable probability distributions. My experience at Meta in building and deploying an out-of-the-box Bayesian causal inference package has shown me that while Bayesian models offer numerous benefits, their adoption is often hindered due to a lack of familiarity with creating model-understandable priors—a challenge particularly pronounced in high-dimensional feature spaces like deep learning models. I am interested in investigating prior elicitation to develop more principled approaches for constructing human-interpretable priors in deep models for NLP tasks, especially in domains marked by vague or conflicting opinions, such as medically affected speech, or data scarcity, like endangered languages. This could lead to better-specified models and increased accessibility for interdisciplinary communities with domain knowledge.

Causal deep learning:
In subsequent papers with Dr. Bansal, I explored application areas in social media, extending my previous work to the classification of domestic abuse stories from the Reddit Domestic Abuse dataset, presented at the Widening NLP (WiNLP) workshop at NAACL, and accompanied by interpretability analysis. Later, I published a paper with Dr. Bansal that was orally presented at EMNLP 2018, which involved creating a novel task and dataset to automatically classify and analyze various forms of sexual harassment based on stories shared on the online forum SafeCity. Utilizing Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) and embedding visualizations, I demonstrated how this helped extract powerful features that could aid in automating incident reports and identifying unsafe areas in cities. This paper was even featured as reading material in Dan Jurafsky’s CS384 Ethical and Social Issues in NLP seminar!

Individually, these models achieved state-of-the-art results for their respective domains. However, beyond Reddit and SafeCity, there are numerous such forums for sexual harassment and abuse, and even more for related topics like bullying. By relying on domain generalization, we can enable more platforms to efficiently benefit from machine learning models. At Meta, my most recent research project involved constructing Bayesian priors for a sparse NN propensity score model that mapped the distribution of users who opted into tracking to the distribution of users who opted out, following assumptions in a covariate shift setting. I am motivated to explore how these principles can be extended to create more robust deep learning models for various real-world applications like healthcare and education.

Why a Ph.D. at Columbia?
My long-term career objective is to become a professor contributing to a multidisciplinary research lab like the machine learning community at Columbia University. I am confident that a Ph.D. program at Columbia, with access to top minds in many fields and a diverse cohort of students, will foster a vibrant and collaborative environment. Moreover, this degree will allow me to acquire further teaching and mentoring experience. I am particularly interested in working with David Blei, whose work aligns with my interests in both Bayesian and causal inference, and I would like to collaborate with him on Bayesian causal inference for text data and causally-informed fairness. I am also excited to collaborate with Elias Barenboim, notably in his work that focuses on Bayesian nonparametric statistics, causal inference (especially in causal fairness analysis), and causal discovery. Additionally, I am interested in working with Kathleen McKeown, notably in her work that focuses on unsafe text detection, detecting actionable insights from text, and social good applications of NLP. Finally, I would hope to collaborate with Zhou Yu regarding her NLP work and understand how the addition of priors or causal understanding can help make NLP models more robust for various real-world applications like healthcare and education. After following the work of Columbia's Machine Learning, Causal AI, and NLP groups, I am confident that it will be an excellent place for me to pursue a Ph.D. and continue my journey through academia.
