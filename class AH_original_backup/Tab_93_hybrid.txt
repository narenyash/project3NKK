In my academic pursuit, I am driven by a passion for creating Machine Learning (ML) algorithms that optimize efficiency in data, models, and systems, particularly those that address real-world challenges in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV). As AI continues to evolve into a data- and computationally-intensive field, the significance of developing data representations using efficiently optimized methods cannot be overstated. The escalating size and costs of ML models call for a more inclusive development process, which is currently limited to a select few teams within resource-rich institutions. As an independent researcher, my limited resources have necessitated careful planning and strategic experiment design.

During my PhD at Carnegie Mellon University (CMU), I aim to focus on developing methods that enable us to construct efficient models and systems with less computation and data resources, thereby lowering the barrier for shaping the future of ML technologies. In the following sections, I will discuss my research experiences and intended future directions.

Data Efficiency: The scarcity of low-resourced data and computational constraints pose significant challenges in developing and deploying real-world ML systems, especially in regions such as sub-Saharan Africa. My first research project involved the creation of a sign-to-text & speech system aimed at bridging the communication gap between the hearing-impaired community and mainstream society in these regions. At that time, there was no available dataset for sign languages in Africa. I collaborated with a TV sign language broadcaster and two special education schools in Nigeria to generate the first ever large-scale and publicly available sign language dataset in Africa. After developing several baseline models using image classification and object detection architectures such as You-Only-Look-Once (YOLO) and single-shot detector, I deployed the best-performing model (accuracy up to 98%) for real-time conversion of sign words/phrases to text and speech. This work was published at IJCAI22 following a spotlight presentation at the 2021 NeurIPS ML4D workshop, garnering awards and media attention for addressing an underserved societal need. However, the experience of creating a pioneering dataset with limited resources sparked my interest in developing data-efficient algorithms for low-resourced scenarios beyond NLP. In cases where some form of data is available, I am keen to explore optimal transport and geometric data manipulations to address the challenges posed by out-of-domain settings and class imbalance prevalent in many CV tasks.

Efficiency in Algorithms and Methods: My research into federated learning (FL) was driven by a desire to foster collaborative development of updatable ML models. I identified communication and computation efficiency as the most significant bottlenecks in FL, including the heavy degradation of model performance due to the heterogeneous nature of the data on which workers are trained when merging updates using federated averaging. These bottlenecks led me to explore ways to utilize efficient evolutionary algorithms as second-order methods for FL. By leveraging particle swarm optimization, I successfully decreased local computational costs in federated training. I have also investigated several other cost-cutting strategies with FL, resulting in numerous failures and lessons learned. For instance, I attempted implementing decentralized mutable torrents for faster communication among clients in decentralized FL and neural architectural search in a federated setting. The latter presented additional challenges; I had to devise novel methods to combine local architectures globally without resorting to stochastic weight averaging. This experience inspired my interest in researching and designing more efficient optimization methods beyond the traditional gradient descent approach, leading me to investigate methods for speeding up local and global convergence while training neural networks in a computationally cheap manner.

Efficiency in Systems and Applications: In a recent project, I explored ways to modify Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures to make them deployable on mobile and edge devices. These platforms represent the primary deployment locations for large models in real-world settings, typically characterized by significantly less computing power and memory bandwidth. By studying several transformer-based architectures designed for edge devices, I identified common patterns in design choices, including MLP-based or sparse models and combinations of transformer and convolutional neural networks. Quantization, pruning, and other sparsification techniques are often applied to the trained models; however, a tradeoff between compression and model performance always exists. I am interested in designing novel methods that enable us to train, evaluate, and deploy neural networks in resource-constrained environments without compromising model performance. Compression techniques such as the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis have been used to train sparser neural networks with similar test performance as their dense counterparts; however, these techniques are less robust to class imbalance and may result in models that produce less confident predictions. I aim to develop sparser models that are also more resilient to such challenges.
