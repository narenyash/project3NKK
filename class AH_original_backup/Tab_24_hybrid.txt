Title: "Finding Myself Through Hyperfixation: A Journey of Self-Discovery and Passion"

In my childhood, I was often asked, "Where's Parker?" as I would wander off, lost in the labyrinth of my imagination. New York City's bustling streets, school pickups, even airports – all served as backdrops to my perpetual disappearances. My boundless imagination, a double-edged sword, often left me lost not just in space but also in time. As the youngest among siblings, I learned to entertain myself, transforming simple objects like bottle caps and crayons into vessels for my creative pursuits. This innate ability, this superpower – hyperfixation – was my lifeline, my escape from the mundane.

As I grew older, school became a daunting labyrinth of its own. My hyperfixation, once a source of comfort, began to pose challenges. In conversations, I found myself fixating on trivial matters, losing sight of the bigger picture. By the end of middle school, I realized a change was needed – not for bottle caps or crayons, but for something that could help me navigate the complex world beyond my imagination.

In my quest for transformation, I turned to art. The art studio became my sanctuary, a realm where time seemed to stand still. With each stroke of the brush, each sculpture chiseled, I found myself immersed in my creation, losing all sense of reality. Every free moment was spent here, in this world of mine, cutting through the long, overstimulating school days. Art provided me with a mental reset, a breather amidst the chaos.

My confidence as an artist soared when I was accepted into the prestigious Art Scholars program. Here, I created my magnum opus, a triptych titled "Fixations," which explores the intricate relationship between body image and self-perception. Crafted from plaster and showcasing three faces with my own hands strategically placed, it is a testament to my growth as an artist and a person.

Inspired by my journey, I founded the Creative Arts Club, aiming to create a safe, creative space for students to explore themselves beyond academics. Through various mediums such as plaster, printing, charcoal, and thought-provoking prompts, I guide fellow artists on their paths of self-discovery. Art has empowered me to step out of my comfort zone, to express my authentic voice fearlessly.

While art provides me with a mental respite, my experiences with hyperfixation in sports are markedly different. The moment the whistle blows, I am singularly focused – on the game, on my team, on our collective victory. As Captain of both basketball and track, I am responsible for unifying and leading by example. During basketball games, I design strategic plays on the whiteboard and work closely with coaches to execute them. Even when benched, I remain invested in the game, my body language reflecting my unwavering dedication. I follow each play, and upon re-entry, I seamlessly slip back into the rhythm of the game. Hyperfixation, once a source of confusion, has become my strength.

Now, when asked, "Where's Parker?" I proudly respond with a smile. I am in the art studio, guiding students through the intricacies of paper mache. I am on the basketball court, making strategic plays with my team. I am still lost in my imagination at times, but I have learned to cherish that aspect of myself – it is what makes me unique.

In conclusion, my hyperfixation, once a source of confusion and isolation, has evolved into a beacon of creativity and leadership. Through art and sports, I have found my voice and discovered the power of focusing my energy on things I love. My journey continues, but now, when people ask, "Where's Parker?" I am ready to share my story – a testament to the transformative power of passion and perseverance.
