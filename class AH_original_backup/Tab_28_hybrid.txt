Title: The Dichotomy of Symbolism in "Rock, Paper, Scissors": A Reflection on Choices and Narratives

In the realm of the University of Chicago's unique application process, I find myself enmeshed in a conundrum reminiscent of the age-old game of "Rock, Paper, Scissors." The admissions committee finds itself at an impasse, teetering on the brink of decision between my application and that of another equally formidable candidate. As requested, I have chosen to engage in this symbolic game, but not before probing its depths, dissecting the intricate web of meanings that it weaves.

Rock, paper, scissors: a seemingly simple game with a complex tapestry of symbolism. The rock, traditionally emblematic of strength and endurance, finds itself vanquished by the paper, an agent of adaptability and flexibility. This paradox raises questions about the nature of victory, not only in games but also in life's grander endeavors. Is it brute force or subtle cunning that ultimately prevails?

One might argue that this game is a metaphor for the clash between differing ideologies, with paper representing diplomacy and compromise, while rock symbolizes coercion and unyielding resolve. Yet, does compromise always trump force? And where does innovation, symbolized by scissors, fit into this narrative?

Examining this game more closely, it becomes apparent that its logic is deeply rooted in context. To understand the reasons behind its creation, we must imagine scenarios where paper might triumph over rock. Alas, I find myself at a loss, unable to propose a compelling justification for such a victory.

Like the game of "Rock, Paper, Scissors," our lives are often marked by incomplete narratives. We simplify complex situations, disregarding the underlying assumptions that keep them functioning smoothly. Like the game, we accept narratives not because they are true but because they offer a convenient resolution. At times, we overemphasize minor flaws and uncertainties in narratives we find challenging. In a world where truth is elusive, it is all too easy to construct stories that unfairly legitimize or delegitimize our beliefs and actions.

Consider the study conducted by Douglas Walker, the host of the Rock-Paper-Scissors World Championships. His research revealed an intriguing pattern: males were found to favor the rock as their opening move 50% of the time, a tendency attributed to the rock's association with strength and power. This discovery sheds light on gender-related dispositions in our society and raises questions about the influence of social norms on our decision-making processes. Could our choices in this trivial game mirror more significant decisions in our lives? Should important decisions such as going to war or addressing global hunger be influenced by factors like gender, race, or creed?

The narratives I spoke of earlier, the stories I dismissed as mere semantics, may indeed carry more weight than we realize. In Walker's study, men subconsciously constructed a narrative around an abstract rock, mirroring the narratives we construct when considering broader concepts like war or existence. The gaps in these narratives can contribute to many of the societal issues plaguing our world today. To engage in the "life of the mind" meaningfully, we must challenge and refine these unconscious narratives that shape the games we play, the truths we uphold, the lessons we learn, and the people we encounter.

Yet, even after delving into the intricacies of this seemingly insignificant game, I am left with unanswered questions about its origins. Who created this simple yet profound game? Was it a child with a vivid imagination, or perhaps an ancient philosopher pondering the paradoxes of life? Or is it simply another incomplete narrative, like so many others that we accept without question?

In conclusion, "Rock, Paper, Scissors" serves as a microcosm for the complexities and contradictions inherent in our lives. By exploring its symbolism and logic, I have gained a newfound appreciation for the power of narratives in shaping our decisions and experiences. As I stand on the precipice of a new academic journey, I am reminded that life is a game of choices, each decision unfolding like another round of "Rock, Paper, Scissors." I look forward to embracing the challenges ahead, armed with an open mind and a willingness to question the narratives that shape my reality.
