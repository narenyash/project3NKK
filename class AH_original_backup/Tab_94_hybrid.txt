Title: Pioneering the Future of Artificial Intelligence: A Journey Towards Smarter, More Adaptable Models

My academic pursuit is centered on constructing expansive neural networks that can seamlessly adapt to real-world scenarios, addressing the inherent limitations of current models. Specifically, I am drawn to three primary areas: (1) enabling neural networks to learn and evolve throughout their lifespan, akin to humans [5, 3, 1]; (2) ensuring privacy and security in neural networks [6]; and (3) teaching neural networks to comprehend and follow human instructions [4, 8, 7, 2].

**Empowering Neural Networks for Lifelong Learning**
During my academic journey, I am determined to cultivate truly lifelong learners capable of accumulating and retaining world knowledge just as humans do. Large neural networks absorb an extensive amount of information during initial pre-training. However, fully parametric approaches often struggle with managing updates to the stored knowledge, a challenge that becomes critical for models deployed in practical applications (e.g., Q&A about real-world events). I aim to develop strategies that facilitate efficient updates to parametric knowledge within neural networks.

**Navigating Knowledge Updates Efficiently**
Updating implicit parameters poses a complex challenge due to the non-explicit manner in which they are initially acquired. The knowledge is not explicitly amassed or defined, unlike in non-parametric knowledge bases (KBs), but rather is inherently encoded within the parameters through pre-training objectives. To develop truly lifelong learners, I have begun exploring continuing the pre-training objective with continual learning methods and examining factors that aid models in acquiring and retaining new information without losing previously gained knowledge [5]. Initial findings suggest that freezing original parameters while updating new ones through adapters offers a promising balance between acquiring new data and retaining previous knowledge. Furthermore, I have investigated an efficient method of updating Pretrained Language Models (PLMs) by utilizing recent Wikipedia and Wikidata dumps for training and evaluation [3]. It was discovered that utilizing only updated or added data is significantly more efficient than working with entire snapshots, particularly when employing continual learning methods.

**Harnessing External Knowledge Efficiently**
How can we supply external knowledge without constantly incorporating it in context? For instance, models may benefit from informative prompts that provide external information; it may be more efficient to inject the prompt rather than continuously attending to it. One example of this case might involve a chatbot agent's persona, which is continually concatenated with the history of utterances to condition the agent to adopt the given persona [1]. In such situations, we have explored injecting the prompt, which parameters the external knowledge (persona) using a teacher model, thereby allowing more efficient inference during deployment. We demonstrate that this method is highly beneficial in scenarios where the prompt is extremely long and frequently updated.

**Leveraging Reinforcement Learning for Continual Learning**
I am convinced that there exists an efficient method of updating large neural networks, mimicking how humans continually update their accumulated knowledge. More specifically, I believe there are methods capable of acquiring new information while minimizing the forgetting of previously gained data. One intriguing approach I wish to explore is utilizing reinforcement learning (RL) to allow neural networks to selectively update the knowledge they need as the world changes. For example, developing RL agents that can browse the internet and determine whether to incorporate the retrieved information in-context (temporary knowledge) or inject it (long-term knowledge) presents an exciting avenue for future research. This, however, necessitates the development of RL algorithms capable of generalizing to new environments (as the world evolves), a challenge that remains open-ended in the realm of RL [9].

**Memorization and Privacy in Large Neural Networks**
If we view large neural networks as knowledge bases, an intriguing question emerges: how can we execute operations as we would with traditional knowledge bases? For instance, large neural networks may memorize unwanted information during initial pre-training. In such cases, it may be necessary to delete specific stored knowledge within models, such as sensitive personal data [6]. In our recent work, we have demonstrated that simple gradient ascent is effective at forgetting target information without impairing general Language Model (LM) capabilities. Surprisingly, it sometimes results in a boost of LM capabilities, which we are further investigating in ongoing research [9].

**Interacting with Large Neural Networks like Traditional Knowledge Bases**
While we have made some initial steps in performing fine-grained knowledge operations with large neural networks, there is still much to be done to interact with LMs as one would with traditional knowledge bases. One aspect I wish to explore concerning this research direction is determining which part of neural networks contributes to memorization capabilities [1]. For example, I am curious about the role of the training objective (e.g., gradient descent/ascent, causal/masked language modeling) in shaping the granularity of the stored knowledge within the parameters. Next, it would be interesting to utilize these findings in developing LMs capable of controlling their fine-grained knowledge while still being fully parametric.

**Language Models that Understand and Perform Human Instructions**
Another research direction I am passionate about is creating LMs that can perform new tasks based on human instructions. Previous works have explored adapting Pretrained Language Models (PLMs) through multitask training, along with a text description of the task, which demonstrates improved performance on unseen tasks [7]. I aim to develop LMs capable of comprehending and following given textual instructions by approaching the problem from a unique perspective.

**A Novel Approach to Instruction Following**
One interesting approach we have explored is flipping the input and output during multitask training; this method has proven effective at generalizing to novel output options (labels), resulting in significant performance improvements on unseen tasks [7]. We have also delved into a modular approach consisting of two phases: phase 1 involves training experts for each specific task during training, while phase 2 involves searching for the relevant expert to perform the given task. This modular method of training and retrieving multiple experts instead of a single expert offers numerous advantages, such as learning new tasks with minimal additional costs [8, 2]. An intriguing extension of this work is merging the experts to develop efficient meta-experts capable of performing various granularities of given tasks.

**Pushing the Boundaries of Instruction Following and Understanding**
Further extending these capabilities, developing LMs that can truly follow instructions and act in the external world seems like an exciting application of LMs. As large LMs grow more capable, they are increasingly utilized for real-world decision-making, such as planning and reasoning for robots or other agents. To further entrust them with additional responsibilities, it is essential to ensure that they genuinely understand and follow the given instructions [4].

**Cultivating a Community of AI Pioneers**
Driving the community to critically consider the significant limitations of large LMs before delegating major decision-making tasks to them and developing methods to allow them to truly reason and plan when given human instructions is another fascinating research direction I wish to explore further.
