Title: Leveraging Multimodal Learning for Grounded World Knowledge in Large Language Models

Over the past few years, there has been remarkable progress in text-based Large Language Models (LLMs). Yet, the information they provide remains incomplete, ungrounded, and ambiguous without integrating additional modalities such as audio, image, video, and more. In contrast, humans construct a highly grounded world model by utilizing multiple modalities. For instance, a description of a Tarsier feels incomplete without its image, while distinguishing sarcasm from excitement can be challenging without auditory context. Speech can also serve as an alternative source of training data for languages with limited text on the web. This realization led me to pursue research aimed at infusing LLMs with grounded world knowledge by leveraging multiple modalities alongside text, and improving them in sparse text-data domains. In this essay, I discuss my experiences that make me a suitable candidate for this line of research.

**Semantic Parsing and Source Code Understanding**
During my Master’s at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), I delved into semantic parsing under Dr. Shirish Shevade's guidance. My project involved developing explainable and efficient methods for the Natural Language to Bash command translation task, which serves as a pedagogical tool for inexperienced developers and increases productivity by automating repetitive tasks on the Linux command-line. I recognized that previous work in semantic parsing lacked a trustworthy and explainable system and addressed this gap by proposing an explainable model (CoNLL 2021). By grounding generated Bash commands using structured information from Linux manual pages, my work underscored the importance of developing solutions that are both effective and transparent. This experience instilled in me a deep understanding of research methodology, effective communication, and perseverance in tackling silent ML bugs. To make the system more accessible to users with limited computing resources, I used constituency parse trees along with problem structure to create a resource-efficient model (NAACL 2022). Semantic parsing sparked my interest in source code modeling and exploring novel ways of programming. As an initial step towards this objective, I developed a semantic querying benchmark over code (ISEC 2024) and participated in NLP for Software Engineering competitions to gain insights into the challenges within this domain. My Master's thesis focused on making programming more accessible, and as a graduate student, I aim to leverage grounded multimodal LLMs to further lower barriers to programming by building novel interfaces, such as employing UML diagrams, code graphs, and speech for a conversational platform for writing code.

**Large Scale Multimodality Research**
My passion for multimodal modeling led me to transition from text-based NLP to multimodal multilingual modeling as a Pre-Doctoral Researcher at Google Research. My first project was developing an ambitious spoken language identification (LangId) system for video, covering over 500 languages. An accurate LangId system is essential for creating more inclusive multimodal LLMs that cover languages with limited text on the web. I led the multimodal modeling for this project under the guidance of Dr. Partha Talukdar, Dr. Sriram Ganapathy, and Mr. Ankur Bapna (under review at ICASSP 2024). By incorporating multiple modalities such as text (from title and description of a video), speech, and the video upload location, our multimodal LangId system achieved an 8% accuracy boost over the speech-only variant. We are currently pursuing active deployment of this model. This project taught me how to design insightful experiments for gaining a deeper understanding of the model and reinforced the principle of Occam's razor in my thought process - simple multimodal modeling that can be easily scaled with data usually yields better overall performance. I also contributed towards developing a task-aware speech representation learning framework, which leverages language information present in speech datasets to enhance speech representations (INTERSPEECH 2023, ASRU 2023). This project underscored the value of simple techniques like contrastive learning and their ability to learn better representations. Additionally, I contributed towards creating, benchmarking, and auditing multimodal evaluation sets for low resource languages, such as the open-sourced Vaani dataset - a joint effort by Google and IISc. At Google, I learned to work in a highly collaborative environment across multiple geographies and research teams, refining my technical skills and delving into more fundamental research problems.

**Future Interests and Graduate Studies**
My experiences in multilingual and multimodal learning have convinced me that relying solely on text is insufficient for developing LLMs in certain settings. Many of the world’s languages do not have sufficient text on the web for building LLMs. An effective learning method utilizing speech could unlock a larger and widely available mine of data. Additionally, speech, being a more natural medium for communication, can provide a more inclusive interface with LLMs, especially for non-literate individuals. Incorporating speech along with text is essential for developing more accessible LLMs. In general, non-text modalities can help in building LLMs for sparser data settings by cross-modality transfer. During my graduate studies, a fundamental research direction I want to pursue is that of modality alignment, especially modality alignment with limited paired data and via resource-efficient training (in terms of additional parameters and compute). I am interested in exploring how alignment in high-resource domains transfers to low-resource settings. For instance, can we align speech and text for languages that have sufficient paired data, and transfer this alignment to related low-resource languages where one of these modalities is missing? Another intriguing question is whether grounding LLMs can help in interactions with the real world for an intelligent agent. For example, can grounding their semantic representations with images/videos allow us to improve their planning capabilities? Drawing on my semantic parsing work, I am also interested in leveraging multiple modalities for building novel interfaces, aiming to make computing more accessible to everyone.
