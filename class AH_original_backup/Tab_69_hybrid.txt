Title: Unraveling the Potential of General-Purpose AI: A Journey Towards Intelligent Reasoning and Planning

The current state of modern general-purpose AI systems falls short in executing faithful reasoning [Valmeekam et al., 2023, Arora et al., 2023a]. My academic pursuit is centered around crafting AI models that can surpass these limitations and excel in both reasoning and planning.

Learning to reason is a complex endeavor, with numerous factors contributing to the challenge. One such factor is the ability of language models to learn a world model by predicting the next token from a text corpus [Zhang et al., 2022]. However, this projected world model may not represent the true reality; neural networks struggle to discover the genuine Chain-of-Thoughts underlying reasoning.

Another limitation is the restricted capabilities of transformer-based models, which exhibit varying capacities in learning diverse types of algorithms and structures [Dziri et al., 2023, Zhou et al., 2023]. To overcome this, a flexible architecture must be designed that enables true generalization for tasks requiring exact computations.

Moreover, large language models (LLMs) have yet to engage in exploration, which is essential for mathematical creativity and numerous tasks like planning and proof generation [Arora et al., 2023a]. To foster strong, creative reasoning capabilities, LLMs require a feedback mechanism derived from self-verification along with guided exploration.

My passion for this field stems from my diverse research background in various subjects, as detailed in the following sections. I believe that these past experiences will serve as valuable building blocks for my future endeavors.

My journey into Machine Learning Research commenced during my third year at IIT Delhi with a project on Generalized Planning under the guidance of Dr. Mausam and Dr. Parag Singla. Our goal was to create a generalized policy that yielded optimal rewards across all instances of a relational Markov Decision Process (MDP). We developed the SymNet architecture, which learned a Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based policy on an instance graph representing an MDP. This policy's size-invariance allowed it to adapt effectively to diverse MDPs [Sharma et al., 2022].

Later, I faced the challenge of improving the planning capabilities of pre-trained LLMs with my research, "Learning and Leveraging Verifiers" [Arora and Kambhampati, 2023], in collaboration with Dr. Subbarao Kambhampati. This project aimed to enhance the planning abilities of LLMs by creating verifiers that could identify incorrect plan sequences generated by these models.

My most recent exploration delved into the Zero-Shot Information Retrieval domain, where I collaborated on "GAR-meets-RAG Paradigm for Zero-Shot Information Retrieval" [Arora et al., 2023b]. This project combined Generative and Retrieval models to achieve effective zero-shot information retrieval.
