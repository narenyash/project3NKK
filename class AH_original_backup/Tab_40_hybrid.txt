Title: Pioneering Computer Vision and Entrepreneurship at Stanford: A Journey of Passion, Innovation, and Cultural Exchange

My ardent pursuit of computer vision and entrepreneurship has led me to aspire for a Master's in Computer Science at the prestigious Stanford University. This esteemed institution offers an ideal platform to delve deeper into my two passions, enabling me to contribute significantly to groundbreaking research in computer vision and equipping me with the essential tools for founding my own venture post-graduation. I am proud to be one of the 75 recipients of the Fulbright grant awarded by the German Fulbright Commission, a testament to my commitment to fostering cultural exchange between Germany and the US.

As I stand on the precipice of completing my BSc in Electrical Engineering at the Technical University of Munich (TUM), I find myself among the top 5% of my 258 peers. Concurrently, I have been honing my skills in technology management at the Center for Digital Technology and Management (CDTM). This joint initiative between MIT and TUM admits only 25 students out of over 300 applicants each term, a reflection of its rigorous selection process and commitment to academic exchange.

During my enlightening stint at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, I immersed myself in courses and projects spanning robotics, bio-engineering, and multimedia informatics. My fascination with computer vision was sparked when I encountered its intricate challenges firsthand. This newfound passion guided me to a project at the Fraunhofer Institute, where I developed a prototype of a customer analytics tool using OpenCV on Fraunhofer’s “Intelligent Camera.” The tool utilized computer vision to count incoming customers and track their paths through the store, further fueling my interest in this captivating field.

My current project at TUM's Computer Vision Group, under the guidance of Prof. Daniel Cremers, has allowed me to delve deeper into the world of computer vision. My focus is on the recently developed Large-Scale Direct Monocular Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (LSD-SLAM) algorithm, which enables real-time odometry and SLAM from a monocular RGB video stream. I am developing an algorithm that estimates the dominant plane in the SLAM point cloud by first employing the RANSAC algorithm to identify the most likely plane model and subsequently refining it using Principal Component Analysis with subsequent frames. In the near future, I aim to expand this algorithm to detect multiple planes within a scene, facilitating simple object detection and scene segmentation.

The fusion of color and 3D information holds immense potential for overcoming challenges in computer vision, as evidenced by Stanford's publications such as "Understanding the 3D Layout of a Cluttered Room From Multiple Images" (Bao S.Y., Furlan A., Fei-Fei L., & Savarese S., 2014) and "Convolutional-Recursive Deep Learning for 3D Object Classification" (Socher R., Huval B., Bath B., Manning C.D., & Ng A., 2012). In my graduate studies, I aim to leverage my expertise in point-cloud, image and video processing, and machine learning to address the question of how depth and color information can be effectively processed for scene segmentation and object recognition purposes. I am particularly interested in exploring the application of deep-learning neural networks to RGB-D datasets, a research direction spearheaded by Prof. Ng's group. Courses such as "Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition" and "Computer vision: from 3D reconstruction to recognition" will fortify my theoretical foundations, while ongoing projects at laboratories such as the Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and the Computational Vision and Geometry Laboratory will provide ample opportunities to contribute to cutting-edge research.

Algorithms like LSD-SLAM promise to make 3D information readily accessible on mobile consumer devices in the near future, making it imperative to develop algorithms capable of utilizing this data from a business perspective. Stanford's robust entrepreneurial ecosystem, which has given birth to numerous successful startups through programs such as StartX and the DFJ Entrepreneurial Leaders Fellowship, is uniquely positioned to nurture my aspirations of founding a computer-vision driven venture. My project with the Fraunhofer Institute and other projects at the CDTM have instilled in me the spirit of entrepreneurship, further cementing my desire to establish my own company. Participating in Stanford's esteemed entrepreneurship programs will provide me with invaluable insights into what makes a successful entrepreneur and arm me with the necessary means to found my own computer-vision driven venture in the future.

My experiences working with international students and colleagues, during my studies at the CDTM, in Hong Kong, and as an intern in a Chinese company, have instilled in me an appreciation for diverse cultures and their unique problem-solving approaches. I firmly believe that fostering intercultural collaboration can significantly impact a project's success. To this end, I have been diligently studying Chinese, French, and English, and I recently began learning Spanish. I aspire to leverage my cultural awareness and language skills to enhance the intercultural collaboration of students at Stanford.

A Master of Science in Computer Science from Stanford will empower me to contribute significantly to cutting-edge research in computer vision and propel me towards realizing my dream of founding my own company. I am humbled by this opportunity to represent Germany and promote cultural exchange between Europe and the US. I eagerly await a positive response to my application.
