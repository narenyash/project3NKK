In the dynamic, ever-evolving realm of computer vision, my passion lies in the cutting-edge area of visual content synthesis, specifically image and video generation. The emergence of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) marked a significant milestone in creating new content, yet recent advancements in Diffusion Models have demonstrated an impressive capacity to generate a broader spectrum and higher quality images. As we transition into an age characterized by AI-driven content creation, the demand for controllable and personalizable visual content synthesis is becoming increasingly crucial. My research interests gravitate towards constructing visual content synthesis models that are both controllable and adaptable, with a focus on developing robust features that encapsulate multimodal constraints.

My journey into the field of visual content synthesis began during a summer internship at Adobe Research. Under the guidance of esteemed researchers Dr. Balaji Vasan Srinivasan and Dr. Stefano Petrangeli, I embarked on an endeavor to generate illustrative videos from procedural documents. We developed a methodology that enabled us to understand the textual content, retrieve relevant visuals (images and videos), and seamlessly assemble them into cohesive videos. Through innovative re-ranking approaches and a Viterbi-inspired optimization framework, we optimally selected combinations of visuals within each frame and across multiple frames in a video. This work was published in WACV 2023 [1] and was also filed as a patent in the USPTO.

My interactions with senior researchers during the internship significantly expanded my understanding and perspectives on research. I was particularly captivated by their ability to comprehend new projects, even those outside their area of expertise, and offer thoughtful insights and recommendations. These encounters broadened my outlook on research, inspiring me to pursue a Ph.D. In pursuit of more extensive research experience and the exploration of different aspects of computer vision, I joined Adobe Research as a full-time Research Associate post-graduation. Building upon my internship experience in image and video retrievals and re-ranking methodologies, I focused on developing deep-learning retrieval models capable of learning constraints during training, thereby eliminating the need for post-retrieval re-ranking. This led me to the challenge of composed image retrieval – performing image retrieval conditioned on multimodal inputs (text and image). Existing techniques that addressed this problem relied on global features for retrieval, resulting in inaccurate localization of regions of interest. To address this, I proposed a multi-modal gradient attention computation mechanism [2] to help the model learn local features and enable better localization and retrieval. This methodology has been filed as a patent in the USPTO.

Our team observed similar issues in vision-language models that employed trainable prompts to improve generalizability – the absence of flow of contextual local information from input images to prompt vectors. To remedy this, we introduced Contextual Prompt Learning (CoPL) [3], a technique that learns prompt weights dynamically and aligns the resulting prompt vectors with local image features. This work has been accepted at AAAI 2024 and is also filed as a patent in the USPTO. This experience expanded my understanding of text-image alignment, enabling me to explore this concept in various vision tasks beyond retrieval.

Retrieval is effective in generating content aligned with user intent; however, its effectiveness relies on the richness of the underlying corpus and the quality of learned representations. A superior creation should iterate between generation and retrieval to overcome these shortcomings, leading me to subsequent explorations in image editing with multi-granular control. Building on my expertise from work on retrievals and stable diffusion models, we pursued a training-free approach that involved manipulating and iterating within the latent space of pre-trained diffusion models to reduce noise accumulations and control the spatial extent of edits. This work has been accepted in WACV 2024 [4].

Expanding on this, I recognized the potential to apply constrained and multi-granular image editing concepts to generate and edit graphic designs. Generating graphic designs is a complex process that requires simultaneous optimization across three dimensions – content, layout, and color. I am currently engaged in instruction-based graphic design editing, where we leverage large language models to comprehend the instruction and diffusion models to produce new design elements. We utilize FlexDM, a transformer-based model, to predict optimal positions and colors for the new elements. To enhance the aesthetics of the updated designs, we train the model with additional loss functions to enforce layout constraints.

My experiences and explorations in image generation sparked my interest in another facet of visual content synthesis – video generation. When I joined Adobe in 2022, a newly released feature called Moving Elements captivated my attention. This feature facilitated the creation of cinemagraphs from static images featuring fluid elements. Building on my learnings from image generation works and drawn by my intrigue for this project, I am currently collaborating with Dr. Kuldeep Kulkarni to explore and animate other types of motions beyond fluids. After immersing myself in the current model, I discovered that most research focuses on animating fluid elements by assuming a constant flow model. However, this approach fails for motions involving larger displacements, like pendulums and swings. To tackle this, I am developing methodologies to formulate the motion model for periodic motions with large displacements. This project has solidified my interest in tackling exciting challenges in the area of visual content synthesis.
