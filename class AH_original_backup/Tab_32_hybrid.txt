Title: Strutting Towards the Stage: Embracing Identity, Culture, and Politics in the World of Drag

In the mesmerizing realm of RuPaul's Drag Race, I found myself captivated by the allure of glittering stilettos, opulent gowns, and voluptuous lashes on the illustrious main stage. After ten seasons of dreaming about my moment under the spotlight, adorned with a crown and scepter, I have crafted a strategy that would make me an unstoppable force in the competition. Although my acting skills might not measure up to the caliber needed to ace the Snatch Game, my cultural pride and political involvement promise to elevate me to new heights as RuPaul's Drag Race's most illustrious queen.

My city, a bustling melting pot of cultures, has been my crucible, shaping me into a unique amalgamation of identities. Growing up in a predominantly Latinx community, I have always worn my Latinx heritage with pride. Yet, the intricacies of my Cuban and Honduran roots often confound me, leaving me yearning for a deeper understanding of my heritage. My estrangement from my Cuban family leaves me feeling disconnected from that part of my identity, while living in a Cuban-dominated city has limited my exposure to Honduran culture. But these complexities are not obstacles; they are the rich tapestry that I would weave into a triumphant masterpiece on the runway.

I envision myself donning an elegant bata cubana, adorned with the striking colors of the Honduran flag. This harmonious fusion of cultures would be a testament to my heritage and serve as a powerful statement of identity. In a lip sync battle, I would not shy away from showcasing the rhythmic beats of my roots—a medley of punta, mambo, and hip-hop that would leave the audience in awe. I would captivate the judges with my impression of the iconic Gloria Estefan during the Snatch Game, a celebrity impersonation challenge, and mesmerize them with my vocal dexterity on write-your-own-song verse challenges by trilling my Rs with precision. The intricacies of my vibrant culture would undoubtedly be the key to unlocking my victory.

Drag is more than just a form of entertainment; it is a powerful platform for self-expression and social change. In our current political climate, dominated by straight white men, it is essential that queer voices are heard. As a queer person of color, I have no choice but to engage in politics. Unfortunately, in my hometown—affectionately known as "mini Cuba"—politics are often shrouded in secrecy, and young people like myself are discouraged from seeking political education. However, the legacies of trailblazing queer activists like Marsha P. Johnson and Stormé DeLarverie have served as my guiding light, illuminating the path to becoming a staunch advocate for civil rights. RuPaul once said, "Every time I bat my false eyelashes, it's a political statement." In the unlikely event that I found myself lipsyncing for my life, I would transform any song into a call to action, advocating for freedom, equality, and justice for people of color, immigrants, and those trapped in the cycle of incarceration.

Although I am still many seasons away from competing on RuPaul's Drag Race, the show continues to inspire me to be outspoken about my culture, beliefs, and identity. For now, I will continue to lip sync to Britney Spears' anthems in my bedroom, dreaming of the day when I might join the queen of queens on the judge's table.

As I prepare for my journey towards the stage, I am reminded that drag is not just about donning sparkly stilettos and grandiose gowns; it's about embracing one's identity, celebrating diversity, and using art as a vehicle for change. And as I strut confidently onto the main stage, I will embody the spirit of resilience, passion, and determination that has defined me throughout my life—a testament to the transformative power of drag and the indomitable human spirit.
