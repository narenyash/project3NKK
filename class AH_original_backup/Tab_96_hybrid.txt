Statement of Purpose

The modern landscape of artificial intelligence (AI) has witnessed a surge in advancements, particularly in recognizing patterns and extracting computational representations from diverse data modalities. While these achievements are noteworthy, AI systems remain limited in their ability to perform complex reasoning and planning. Essentially, they struggle to bridge the gap between basic tabletop settings and simulated tasks, a challenge rooted in their inability to make inductive leaps from data and learn causal relationships between concepts—a skill humans master naturally. Driven by this intrigue, my research pursuits aim to unravel the computational foundations that could usher in such cognitive abilities within AI systems.

Throughout my undergraduate research journey, I have collaborated with esteemed colleagues to tackle the challenge of inferring a manipulation program—a sequence of actions grounded in the scene—from natural language instructions. Human cognition adopts a hierarchical approach to this task: first, it utilizes its understanding of visuo-spatial concepts to identify objects alluded to by the instruction within the environment; subsequently, it proposes tentative new locations for these objects based on an understanding of action semantics. Inspired by this hierarchical process, we conjectured that scene understanding and manipulation could be realized as the execution of a symbolic, hierarchical program extracted from language instructions. Building upon the work of Mao et al. [1], my significant contribution involved formulating a generalized, hierarchical symbolic-program space for action manipulation, coding up the hierarchical parser for natural language instruction, and data generation using PyBullet. Our model showcases end-to-end learning and impressive generalization to novel scene compositions and extended instructions. A related full paper [2] is currently under review at the International Conference of Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2023, while it was previously featured at the Workshop for Neuro Causal and Symbolic AI at NeurIPS 2022.

Complicated planning for embodied agents can be attributed to the intricate nature of satisfying spatial and kinematic constraints. This predicament fuels my current research endeavors, which revolve around enhancing the aforementioned architecture with an action representation that encapsulates this critical property. My strategy involves learning action effects as a probability distribution over the task space conditioned upon scene constraints. Preliminary experiments have employed a conditional Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to parameterize the action space, resulting in promising outcomes. Garrett et al. [3] introduce streams as a black box interface for incorporating this property. I discovered that my action representation is well-suited for instantiation as a stream, and planning heuristics such as those proposed in [3], can be implemented upon it. This system could pave the way for deeper reasoning pertaining to more complex goals. I eagerly anticipate further progress in this direction.

In actual-world settings, motion plans often fail due to external disturbances. In collaboration with two of my colleagues, I am investigating approaches to fortify our model with resilience against such failure modes of action execution. My proposed solution is to learn action effects as the transformation of an object-centric scene graph representation of the environment parametrized by a Graph Neural Network (GNN), much like [4]. By learning to predict deviations in the environment state from the scene graph representation, we can identify errors in execution. Subsequently, recovery from the failure state can be achieved by computing an adjusted action sequence to reach the expected final state from the current failure state using forward planning. Preliminary experiments have displayed promising results in this regard.

Beyond my work in embodied AI, I have also ventured into exploring how imposing symbolic structures upon dense representations can foster more generalizable models within various domains of machine learning. In collaboration with Prof. Manik Verma, we are investigating graph-based approaches to learning superior representations of the label space for the problem of multi-label extreme classification. Traditionally, approximate nearest neighbor search graphs, used as efficient inference engines for extreme classification, have been constructed upon pre-trained label embeddings. We are examining strategies to construct nearest-neighbor search graphs conditioned on the query-label distribution, which can yield better hard negatives for training the language encoder.

My research aspirations align with Prof. Anca Dragan, Prof. Pieter Abbeel, and Prof. Sergey Levine in their pursuit of building AI systems capable of long-horizon reasoning in real-world scenarios. (i) What are the cognitive biases that empower intelligent human behavior and learning? Can these be translated into inductive biases for learning algorithms? (ii) How can we develop embodied-AI agents adept at learning novel concepts from sparse data points gathered through real-world exploration? (iii) Can we achieve large pre-trained models capable of reasoning and planning to long horizons for novel tasks? These questions serve as the cornerstones of my long-term research objectives. In addition, I am eager to delve into the areas of causality, reinforcement learning, and out-of-distribution generalization, alongside task and motion planning, and visual reasoning in my future research.

In a developing country like India, the AI research ecosystem is still in its nascent stages. My mentors have been an invaluable source of inspiration throughout my academic journey, sharing insightful perspectives about research in general and ensuring that resource constraints do not impede progress as much as possible. I firmly believe that being part of academia provides an individual with the opportunity to foster progress further into the future through interactions with future researchers. In the future, I aspire to be a valuable contributor to this community.

To undertake these professional and personal endeavors, I am seeking a Ph.D. at UCB, which offers a solid technical foundation and the chance to learn from pioneers in their respective fields—an opportunity that will greatly contribute to my lifelong career.
