January 9th, 2007 was a day that marked a turning point in my life, as I eagerly anticipated Steve Jobs' MacWorld keynote, which was scheduled for 11 a.m. Central Standard Time. With the fervor of an Apple enthusiast, I knew I had to be present to witness the unveiling of Apple's next groundbreaking innovation. That night, my mother, recognizing the depth of my passion for all things techy, agreed to let me skip school the following day. This decision served as a testament to the profound impact Apple's innovative spirit has had on my identity - shaping me into an adventurous innovator, budding entrepreneur, and technology enthusiast since I could first grasp a mouse.

From infancy, cords, gadgets, and screens have been my hallmark. My baby book reveals that my first real word was "hose," a misinterpretation of the long green things in people's yards that I believed were outdoor electrical cords. At age six, my only Christmas wish was for an overhead projector, a desire that Santa miraculously fulfilled by sourcing one on eBay. At eight, I embarked on a self-driven installation of a dazzling holiday light display that illuminated the entire neighborhood, along with our electricity bill. This year also marked the launch of my first successful business: Tony's Tech Help. Two years later, after diligently saving my earnings, I invested in my first laptop - a decision that would kindle my curiosity for computer science.

My newfound companion was a sleek MacBook Pro. I spent countless hours learning about its intricacies while my parents spent equally as many hours protesting my excessive screen time. By age 11, I was penning clumsy programs and writing fragments of code. A year later, I rented my first server and became fluent in multiple programming languages. In eighth grade, I didn't merely attend school; I dedicated each day to managing my servers and maintaining more than a dozen websites for local businesses and nonprofits. At 15, an idea struck me - one that would become the foundation of my next venture.

The year was Fall 2012, and Minecraft had captivated my generation. I found myself experimenting with coding Minecraft server "plug-ins." Word of my work spread quickly, and I soon became a recognized expert within the global server community. A brilliant idea then emerged: to create team combat matches on unique Minecraft maps organized by server plug-ins - a concept that no one else had yet attempted. The prototype was soon followed by growth - from a dozen players on my development server to over a hundred on my first 24/7 server. People couldn't get enough of what I offered! Within just a few months, my infrastructure, built upon Java, Ruby, MongoDB, and Nginx, was overflowing with new subscribers and became known as "The Overcast Network." By 2013, I was receiving nearly 100,000 unique users and over one million page views per week. Competitors soon arose, attempting to replicate my concept, but watching my idea flourish into a successful business was a truly exhilarating experience. In the last ten months, unique users and page views per week have doubled, and I am now preparing to present at the international Minecraft convention, Minecon, on the panel "Growing Server Communities."

As I continue to design the gameplay, website, and user experience of Overcast, Apple's legacy and Steve Jobs' vision remain my steadfast inspiration. Their relentless pursuit of innovation through design, simplicity, and audacious risk-taking serves as a constant reminder that the most transformative ideas often arise from a commitment to stay hungry, to stay foolish. As I embark on the next phase of both my computer science education and my life as an entrepreneur, I will carry Jobs' words close to heart: "Stay hungry, stay foolish."
