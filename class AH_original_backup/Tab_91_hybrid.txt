In our current era of information abundance, the proliferation of long-form documents, articles, and other textual data offering high-quality information is soaring at an unprecedented pace. Navigating this vast sea of content and extracting relevant insights can require a substantial investment of time and cognitive energy. Moreover, some resources encompass domain-specific knowledge semantics and technical concepts that may prove challenging for the general public to grasp.

My journey working on several Natural Language Processing (NLP) systems to enhance document accessibility has significantly shaped my research interests and ignited a passion within me to develop advanced text processing systems for long documents. These systems aim to dissolve knowledge barriers while facilitating swift consumption. To further this pursuit, I am eager to embark upon a Ph.D. in NLP to deepen my comprehension, fortify my foundation, and acquire the skills necessary to spearhead focused research in this field.

My foray into the legal domain unveiled a realm where the intricate linguistic structure and semantics of documents like terms and conditions often render them opaque even for legal practitioners. Performing legal tasks such as risk analysis, contract review, or summarization can be time-consuming and laborious. At Adobe Research, I was granted the unique opportunity to devise an NLP system that processes a multi-page input contract to identify rights and responsibilities, link them with concerned parties, and flag potentially risky clauses. This project not only alleviated the burden of contract review but also made legal documents more accessible to laypeople by presenting structured, multidimensional information. This venture into legal AI sparked my interest in the power of language models to drive the components of a legal system.

Traditionally, these language models were pre-trained over legal corpora using Masked Language Modeling (MLM) or Auto-regression without explicitly harnessing domain-specific characteristics. However, I believe that incorporating domain knowledge and semantics during pre-training could engender a superior language model with improved downstream performance. To test this theory, I proposed a novel legal language model framework that employed multi-task learning to comprehend various legal facets while pretraining. This strategy yielded state-of-the-art (SoTA) performance across several downstream tasks, paving the way for its utilization to power key features in Adobe Sign.

During this research exploration, I gained insight into the life cycle of a project, learned how to formulate sound inductive hypotheses, and honed the necessary soft skills to articulate my research ideas effectively and collaborate productively. My research endeavors have led to the filing of three patents in this domain. In the future, I aspire to extend this approach to other domains, such as healthcare, finance, etc., to expedite the development of resources that make specialized domain literature more accessible.

While my legal AI efforts focused on removing knowledge barriers to accessing specialized resources, I simultaneously tackled the problem of improving the consumption experience for long documents. These texts often prove time-consuming and tedious to read, regardless of their straightforward content. A key observation reveals that they provide extensive information on various topics that may not be relevant to a specific user. Developing technologies that present pertinent content based on a user's topical interests can pave the way for an efficient and personalized consumption experience. To achieve this objective, I worked on automatically extracting the latent topical structure of a document text. While most previous approaches modeled this as a linear segmentation task, I developed a supervised method for recovering its topical organization as a hierarchy by utilizing tree-structure conditional random field. Being better aligned with the widely accepted theory of discourse segmentation, our approach outperformed prior techniques for several linear segmentation datasets. This marked the first time I witnessed the success of combining linguistic theory with the solution method, inspiring me to seek intriguing possibilities where NLP can be connected with other disciplines to construct better computational models.

At Adobe, I leveraged this technology to create the feature of topic-based summarization, personalizing input documents based on a user's topical interests, and intelligent navigation experiences to enhance document consumption experiences. Currently, I am developing a text segmentation framework that involves an auxiliary objective to infer the aspect associated with the identified segments. This modeling technique would provide complementary information to improve segmentation and help unveil the topical themes in the document.

My initiative in making information accessible to all predates these experiences. During my tenure as a research intern at Adobe, I developed a corpus-level system that established various semantic relations between documents in a user-uploaded directory to provide users with a holistic understanding of their collection. Here, we addressed the problem of detecting version relationships between documents to minimize the cognitive effort required to locate the most recent document version and determine how a specific copy has evolved over time. As the naive pairwise computation of version relations would be computationally demanding, we employed Minhash-LSH to approximately identify version sets and devised a sophisticated post-processing algorithm to rectify any potentially noisy results. This work earned the best-paper runners-up award at WISE 2021. To reduce knowledge barriers to reading a constituent document, I also tackled the problem of determining the prerequisite documents from the corpus while developing this system. My proposed solution presented the prerequisite relations as an interactive dependency graph, offering insightful information like the best reading order for comprehension. These projects not only introduced me to NLP but also demonstrated its potential in achieving accessibility and reducing cognitive load.
