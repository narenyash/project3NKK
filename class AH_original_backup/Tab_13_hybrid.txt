Title: Embracing the Epistemological Stream: A Journey Through Behavioral Economics

Nestled beneath the two colossal branches of a venerable Newton Pippin Tree, I find solace amidst the verdant Green Mountains of Vermont. The vast landscape unfolds before me like an endless sea, with the distant peaks and valleys mirroring my own inner journey. Perched high above, I feel a strange kinship with these ancient mountains, both motionless and ageless in our shared stillness. Once, I would have scoffed at such introspection as a waste of time; now, I find myself drifting through the epistemological currents, seeking answers to life's most profound questions.

My educational journey, prior to my transformation at Mountain School, was a testosterone-infused whirlwind that steered me towards psychology and neuroscience. The promise of academic success beckoned from the NIH, just 2.11 miles away, like a guiding light in the darkness. I was taught to strive for specialization above all else, but deep down, I knew this path did not align with my true self.

Upon arriving at Mountain School, I felt an overwhelming sense of alienation. The warm embrace of acceptance that pervaded the campus was strange and disconcerting to me. Instead of embracing this new environment, I retreated to familiar territory – sports and labor. It wasn't long before a Broomball tournament captured my attention, and I seized the opportunity to organize it, even though I had never played the game before.

That fateful night, under the ethereal glow of the ice rink, I found myself locked in combat with an opponent. Our brooms clashed, and I fell, my head colliding with the unforgiving surface. Despite the pain, my stubborn spirit refused to accept defeat – even as I was diagnosed with a concussion. I yearned to return to my classes and maintain the same pace as my peers, but my injured brain had other plans.

My teachers struggled to accommodate me during my recovery period, unsure of how best to care for a student who no longer fit neatly into their structured curriculum. In this limbo, I began wandering the campus, finding solace in the silent companionship of Zora, my English teacher's dog, and in the meditative tasks of pruning the orchard, feeding the wood furnaces, and splitting wood. In those quiet moments, I discovered a newfound sense of belonging within myself.

However, introspection alone was not enough to satisfy my intellectual hunger. I craved diverse perspectives, so I organized late-night debates on topics ranging from medieval war machines to political theory. I even challenged my friends to defend outrageous ideas, eager to engage in the art of discourse. As Thoreau once wrote, "Let the daily tide leave some deposit on these pages, as it leaves, the waves may cast up pearls." Ideas have always captivated me, but now I understand the joy of riding their waves, letting them breathe and evolve into something more profound than mere solutions to immediate problems.

My passion lies in ideas that lead to innovative and practical improvements for humanity. Whether it's a large-scale challenge or a seemingly insignificant conundrum, I revel in the process of dissecting and solving these puzzles. It was during a cross-country meet that my friend and I serendipitously conceived a concept for an eco-friendly disposable water bottle – a project we are now eager to bring to fruition.

My fascination with psychology and neuroscience remains, but I have come to realize that these fields alone do not allow me to expand human consciousness in the way I desire. In search of a solution, I found myself drawn to behavioral economics, where I believe I can synthesize my love for both academic rigor and practical innovation. It was a concussion, in more ways than one, that led me to this revelation.

I am still on a journey, navigating the complex currents of knowledge and self-discovery. But I am no longer content to simply drift along – I am eager to dive deeper, to plumb the depths of human behavior and unlock its secrets. The mountains may be vast, but with each step, I feel myself growing stronger, more resilient, and more determined than ever before to make a difference in this world.
