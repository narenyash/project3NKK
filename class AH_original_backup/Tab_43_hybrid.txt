Hybrid Essay:

My passion for delving deeper into complex adaptive systems was ignited during an undergraduate research project in crowd simulation at the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur. Working alongside faculty from the Industrial and Chemical Engineering departments, I calibrated popular crowd models against Indian data to evaluate their resilience amidst diverse customs and cultures. The intriguing realization that simple rules can generate complex behavior, coupled with profound discussions with my mentor about the work of the Santa Fe Institute, kindled my fascination with complexity science.

Fast forward a few years, after graduating and securing a job as a site-reliability engineer at a leading tech company, I attended a complexity conference in San Francisco. This event was instrumental in forming an independent working group consisting of PhDs and industry professionals. Together, we successfully applied for a military grant to explore the application of complexity science techniques on physiological data collected from wearable devices. Our multidisciplinary team, comprised of individuals from diverse backgrounds, fostered stimulating conversations that further fueled my curiosity in the field.

As I delved deeper into this captivating world, I found courses like operations research, systems simulation, and machine learning particularly enticing within my industrial engineering curriculum. This sparked a collaboration with Dr. Sergiy Butenko of Texas A&M to investigate the relationship between the ratings of 700 popular movies and the global structural properties of their character graphs. The challenge of starting with initial conditions and working towards desired outcomes not only stretched my creativity but also provided an avenue to apply the probabilistic, computational, and optimization techniques I learned in my courses to real-world problems.

During my B.S., one of my favorite experiences was helping a professor teach his course. I cherished being an accessible aid during office hours or when holding exam review sessions. This inspired me to volunteer for Big Brothers Big Sisters, a program that pairs underprivileged high school students with college-educated mentors in the U.S. As a master’s student at Chalmers, I aspire to continue serving as a teacher or research assistant, dedicating myself to education and service.

In my current role at Apple, I have cultivated a reputation for proactivity by creating numerous automation scripts that minimize labor and enhance the insight into the health of our clusters and technologies. Despite having no formal training on these technologies, I honed these skills through nurturing relationships with subject matter experts and hard work. Both in industry and academia, statistics, insight, prediction, and control remain my primary areas of interest. By consistently seeking out opportunities for advancement, I am confident that I will be well-prepared to handle the rigorous academic and research demands of Chalmers' CAS program.

When exploring graduate studies, one of my key criteria was a formal complex adaptive systems program. I envisioned myself surrounded by complexity enthusiasts from various disciplines rather than a handful of faculty with only general interest in the area. Chalmers stands out as an ideal fit for this criterion, boasting a wide array of active researchers and faculty members dedicated to complex adaptive systems. Furthermore, collaborations across the Atlantic offer invaluable academic connections and potential research partners.

By studying and conducting research at Chalmers, I aspire to lay the groundwork for a fruitful future in academia through publishing multiple peer-reviewed papers. Post-graduation, I aim to embark on the journey towards tenure as a professor, remaining open to working in any culture or society that offers an environment conducive to my ambitions. Pursuing studies and research at Chalmers would undoubtedly present an opportunity to thrive in Sweden after graduation.

Although I am open to various research topics, several professors in the CAS program have projects that resonate deeply with me: Kristian Lindgren (information theory and agent-based economics), Kolbjörn Tunström (swarming and nonlinear dynamics), and Claes Andersson (wicked societal systems). Reading through their papers has further solidified my belief that Chalmers' MSc CAS program aligns perfectly with my interests and future aspirations.
