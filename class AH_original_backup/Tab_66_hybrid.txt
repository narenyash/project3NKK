In Pursuit of a Symbiotic Future: My Journey as an HCI Researcher in Human-AI Interaction

My transition from industrial software development to Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) research was catalyzed by the visionary works of Dr. Michel Beaudouin-Lafon. His scholarly papers illuminated the persistent challenge in visual interfaces: prioritizing features over usability, a dilemma that continues to shape my work [1, 5]. Today, my research focus lies squarely on human/community-AI interaction, with an emphasis on two key areas:

1. **Designing Interactive Visual Interfaces**: I am particularly drawn to creating interfaces that enhance AI's transparency and controllability for a diverse audience, including programmers, data workers, and data scientists. My interest extends to using Natural Language Processing (NLP)-driven visual interfaces to support multi-verse analysis. This involves reifying and reusing information silos gathered from interactions with AI-driven agents or web resources [3].

2. **Understanding User Behavior**: I am intrigued by investigating how users operationalize everyday life information into concrete intentions and actions when utilizing AI. For example, I aim to delve deeper into the foraging and sensemaking loop that translates web resources into inputs for AI models, leading to more aligned outcomes [6].

With my independent research experiences and programming skills, I aspire to join MIT's HCI and collaborate with experts from the Visualization Group, Haystack Group, and Usable Programming Group. My long-term goal is a career in academia or industry research after receiving my Ph.D.

My foray into HCI was marked by two pioneering projects supervised by Dr. Can Liu. The first project, RE1, was the development of a cooperative Wizard of Oz Platform for simulating future speech interfaces with multiple wizards (conference publication at CSCW 2023) [3]. In my second project, RE2, I was involved in creating interaction frameworks tailored to speech input, focusing on understanding speech to inform new interfaces for verbal text composition (conference publication at CUI 2023) [6]. These projects honed my foundational skills in HCI research, encompassing prototyping and the ability to conduct studies and analyze both quantitative and qualitative results.

My undergraduate thesis with Dr. Zhicong Lu, RE3, provided a pivotal turning point. I sought to explore interventions in live streaming that encouraged prosocial community interactions without compromising viewer engagement [10]. My innovative approach leveraged the visual narrative to proactively moderate the live streaming community, dynamically altering the storyline based on the sentiment of live chats. This approach is built upon the foundation of bystander intervention and persuasive design principles, meticulously crafting the visual narrative’s design and applying it to the realm of live streaming moderation [8].

My passion for Human-AI Interaction was ignited further when I joined a thesis-based master's program at the University of Waterloo, under the guidance of Dr. Jian Zhao. During my studies, I focused on bridging the gap between users’ goals and AI-generated contents [7, 9]. My two first-author conference submissions to CHI 2024 reflect these efforts:

RE4: Enhancing the controllability and transparency of AI for programmers [11]. We introduced the concept of hierarchical generation, utilizing decomposition techniques in NLP to externalize programmers’ mental models. The system further leverages a modular block design that directly links NL prompts to corresponding code segments, enabling precise modification of specific code segments through direct manipulation.

RE5: Design mechanisms for future collaborative Natural Language-based programming scenarios [2]. Collaborative Natural Language programming presents challenges, as participants need to stay informed about their collaborators’ progress and intentions while simultaneously building upon each other's work. To address this, we developed a prototype that supports collaborative prompt engineering through referring, requesting, sharing, and linking mechanisms.

My research philosophy echoes Picasso's words, "Learn the rules like a pro so you can break them like an artist." This is evident in my propensity to leverage foundational theories and introduce innovative perspectives [10]. I am adept at thriving in this dynamically evolving era, continually improving my mindset through self-reflection. These skills enabled me to successfully submit two CHI papers within eight months while managing a demanding academic load, including four courses (GPA 4/4) and three TA duties. My dedication to research has also resulted in me receiving scholarships, including the International Master Award of Excellence and the Graduate Student Scholarship, in addition to my full entrance scholarship.

Driven by my passion for contributing and sharing my skillsets, I have actively participated in and led various programmer communities, such as the Google Developer Student Group, and served as an AWS and Microsoft Student Ambassador. In these roles, my leadership and collaboration skills have proven invaluable. These experiences have provided me with firsthand insights into the challenges and practical needs of the programmer community. I also co-founded a company, providing me with real-world insights into innovation and entrepreneurship. Besides, I was a full-stack developer and data analyst at Hong Kong’s largest social media company and an AI research intern at Huawei Research Lab, where I cultivated strong development and prototyping abilities [10]. I then applied these skills to lead the design and development of prototypes for my research projects.

My diverse background and experiences converge to shape my research with a steadfast commitment to user-centered design that ultimately contributes to the well-being of communities. My future research aims to explore further facilitating users' interaction with generative AI, focusing on two potential areas:

FR1: Reifying Data Scientist-AI Multi-Verse Interactions. With Large Language Models (LLMs) serving as an alternative to conventional Question-and-Answer sites, I envision a future where interactions between data scientists and LLMs are not ephemeral but instead sharable and reusable. Just as multiple universes in science fiction, every exchange between a data scientist and an LLM can birth a new 'universe' of understanding and solutions [12]. The embedded memories of multiverse interactions enable programmers to reuse previous LLM-driven solutions, cultivating a dynamic knowledge base that evolves with each interaction, where programmers disseminate and benefit from LLM-augmented insights.

FR2: From Web Resources to Actions. LLMs offer an alternative avenue for users to find answers. Yet, how can users effectively transform the “cues” they previously relied on into actionable prompts for LLMs? I aim to investigate the synergy between these two resources and explore the design and interaction techniques that can help users translate their information-seeking and intention-formulation processes into actionable steps [12].

Why MIT HCI? MIT HCI's labs at CSAIL are renowned for their innovative advancements and dedication to creating solutions that prioritize human needs. I am particularly drawn to three distinct yet complementary research directions within the institute:

1. **Interactive Visual Interfaces**: I am captivated by Dr. Arvind Satyanarayan's papers, which cleverly incorporate theories derived from interaction and interface design [8]. For instance, his concept of malleable interfaces that leverage the idea of tool re-purposing aligns closely with my interests (FR1). I would relish collaborating with their research group to explore novel ways of interacting with AI.

2. **Empowering Users**: Dr. David Karger's research focuses on empowering users to effectively manage information [13]. I believe that his research group's expertise could offer me valuable insights into concretizing web resources and transforming them into actionable steps for future reuse (FR2).

3. **Software Design**: The work of Dr. Daniel Jackson, especially his recent contributions to developing the reactive relational model and its implementation in software design, is compelling [4]. I see the MIT EECS Ph.D. program not just as a graduate school but as a collaborative ecosystem where my research visions can truly take shape, supported by the unparalleled expertise and resources available. The alignment of our shared goals not only benefits my academic pursuits but also creates opportunities for us to take the lead in future design initiatives, enriching the community and improving accessibility to AI models.

In pursuit of a symbiotic future where human ingenuity and artificial intelligence coalesce, I am excited about the potential that MIT HCI offers to shape the course of this transformative era.
