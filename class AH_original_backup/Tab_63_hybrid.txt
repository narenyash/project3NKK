Title: Navigating Algorithmic Odysseys: Empowering Platform Workers Through Collaborative Research

My academic aspirations revolve around two interconnected goals: first, to explore how platform workers construct robust algorithmic knowledge systems; second, to create accessible algorithm intervention toolkits by building upon this research. These tools aim to foster a grassroots approach, empowering platform workers to navigate algorithmic systems autonomously, promoting inclusivity and enhancing well-being within the platform workforce.

The digital world of platform work is intrinsically governed by algorithms, regulating service demand, shaping evaluation systems, and often remaining hidden from view. This invisibility does not erase their impact; instead, it sets the stage for a challenging journey—a modern-day Odyssey—where workers must decipher these 'spectral' algorithmic entities they encounter on platforms. To survive this digital landscape, workers develop a kind of folk wisdom, relying on it to improve work conditions. While academic research has delved into various aspects of this relationship (Bucher, 2016; Huang et al., 2022; Karizat et al., 2021), there is still a need for deeper theoretical exploration.

I argue that this area calls for intense scholarly scrutiny. We must delve into the origins, evolution, and validation of the algorithmic knowledge developed by platform workers, understanding how it influences their practical application in the workplace. My preference for "algorithmic knowledge" over "folk theories" or "algorithmic imaginary" stems from the fact that platform workers, unlike casual users, must critically assess the reliability of this knowledge, given that their livelihoods depend on these algorithms. When they find a strategy effective, they incorporate it into their routines; conversely, they abandon ineffective strategies. This approach transcends mere understanding of algorithms; it alters the workers' actual interaction with the platform and significantly impacts job performance.

In my current research, I have examined how dedicated fans manipulate social media algorithms, a topic I presented at the Fan Studies Network North America (FSNNA) Annual conference. Through interviews with 43 Chinese fan opinion leaders, I uncovered how key fans decode algorithms and influence other fans to participate in algorithmic labor, significantly disrupting the recommendation algorithm in China. In response to these manipulations, Weibo launched a separate entertainment trending section in December 2020. However, this measure had minimal impact, demonstrating the potency of fan-driven algorithm manipulation. I describe this phenomenon as an "empirical dance" between understanding and manipulating algorithms, where key fans must continually observe, verify, and refine their strategies to maintain influence. This project showcases how a few users can decode algorithms, mobilize other users, and ultimately shape the direction of algorithmic trends.

Bringing this project into focus underscores the potential for ordinary users to disseminate reliable algorithmic knowledge, foster collective action within digital communities, and intervene in recommendation algorithms. My experience as a qualitative researcher has equipped me with fundamental computational skills, having completed the 'Introduction to Python' course at the Oxford Internet Institute, University of Oxford, and attending the Oxford Machine Learning Summer School. However, my primary interest lies in social science methodologies, such as interviews, experiments, and surveys.

Embarking on a Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) Ph.D. at Carnegie Mellon University, I am well-positioned to advance my expertise as a platform and algorithms culture scholar. Post-Ph.D., I aspire to become a professor while maintaining close collaboration with platform companies to advocate for improved working conditions of platform laborers. During my doctoral studies, I am particularly drawn to investigating the complex interplay between algorithmic knowledge and the broader technological frameworks of the platform economy, focusing on how workers navigate these systems and potentially subvert them.

My ambition is to collaborate with esteemed scholars like Dr. Hong Shen, Dr. Motahhare Eslami, Dr. Laura Dabbish, and Dr. Sarah Fox to unravel the opaque aspects of platform algorithms, examine their impact on digital laborers, and explore strategies for enhancing workers' autonomy through the acquisition of robust algorithmic knowledge. Through these collaborations, I hope to shed light on the complexities of platform work, empower workers, and contribute to creating a more equitable digital landscape.
