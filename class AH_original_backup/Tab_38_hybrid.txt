Title: From Self-Taught to College-Bound: My Evolution as a Visionary Entrepreneur

In January 2019, I texted my mother with a declaration that seemed final: "I will never go to college." For years, this sentiment echoed within me, a steadfast rejection of the conventional path. But like the stones in Kyoto's Ryoan-ji rock garden, my journey has been a delicate balance of order and chaos—a testament to life's intricate tapestry of contradictions.

From a tender age, I witnessed a homogenized path being carved out for my peers: excel academically, secure a prestigious college education, pursue a stable career, and live a predictable life. This script, devoid of individuality, felt unpalatable to me. I sought solace in self-learning, carving my own niche within the digital realm.

By the age of seven, I was dabbling in code. By ten, I was imparting my knowledge for $30 an hour. At twelve, I proudly launched my first app on the App Store. My online gaming platform, by the time I turned fourteen, was generating an impressive annual revenue of $60,000. A six-figure exit followed suit by sixteen, with YouTube acting as my personal guide through the labyrinth of programming and finance.

As I neared the end of my junior year, while my peers were preparing for exams that supposedly mapped their futures, I found myself returning from a startup accelerator—an ambassador of the unconventional path. My latest creation, Cal AI, had just hit the market, promising to revolutionize calorie tracking by simply photographing food.

Invigorated by initial positive feedback, my co-founder and I made the bold move to San Francisco for the summer. Within a week, we found ourselves interviewing our first employee—a man with 24 more years of experience and a family to support. Despite my imposter syndrome, I was driven to prove myself worthy, often leaving the office last each night.

The chorus of approval from VCs, founders, and mentors echoed a resounding theme: I didn't need college. Cal AI had swiftly become the fastest-growing app in its category. Our team grew to 15 members, our users collectively shed tens of thousands of pounds, and investors clamored to invest—offers we declined.

However, success brought with it a nagging question: what's next? As I reveled in the euphoria of surpassing the million-dollar revenue mark, I felt an emptiness—a yearning for purpose beyond capitalist pursuits. Yes, my app was helping countless individuals lose weight. But would this fleeting sense of triumph forever define my destiny? Was my compass set on wealth, acclaim, and recognition, or did it point towards a deeper calling?

In search of answers, I stumbled upon the Ryoan-ji rock garden in Kyoto—a sanctuary that once harbored Steve Jobs' introspection. While I didn't experience an epiphany like him, the paradoxical beauty of the asymmetrical stones left an indelible impression. Perhaps life is a dance between chaos and order, meaning not residing in resolution but in the pursuit of understanding this delicate interplay.

My rejection of conventional education had unwittingly led me down another path with its own set of expectations—the archetypal dropout founder. Once again, I found myself guided by those who held sway over the world of startups and venture capital. Yet, as my quest for purpose continued, I began to understand that college was more than a rite of passage. It represented an opportunity to broaden my horizons, learning from fellow students and professors alike—not just from computers or textbooks.

Individuality and connection are not mutually exclusive, but rather complementary forces that coexist within us all. Through college, I aim to contribute to this larger community, growing alongside it while honing my skills to leave an even greater positive impact on the world.

Now, almost five years after my initial declaration, I am ready to send a new text: "I'm going to college." The journey that began with self-taught determination now embraces connection and collaboration—a testament to the evolving nature of individuality.
