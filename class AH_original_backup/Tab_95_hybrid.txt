Title: Overcoming Real-World Challenges in Machine Learning: A Journey Towards Transforming ML Landscape

Machine learning (ML) has witnessed remarkable progress, yet its application in the real world remains fraught with challenges that surpass mere statistical methods. These hurdles necessitate robust system and infrastructure support to ensure optimal performance and user-friendly functionality across every stage of the ML pipeline. For example, training systems must address scalability for large models, while also catering to heterogeneity in in-house clusters. Serving systems should minimize latency and prioritize privacy protection for users. As we navigate these challenges over the coming years, I am driven to pursue a Ph.D. to confront them head-on.

In my pursuit of large-scale ML, I find myself at the intersection of ML and distributed systems. My vision is to design ML models, algorithms, and systems that not only deliver outstanding performance but also are user-friendly, enabling widespread adoption in the real world. During my pre-doctoral research, I have delved into democratizing distributed ML training, privacy-preserving ML serving, and large-scale ML algorithms. My work has been recognized at prominent conferences such as ICLR’23 (spotlight), NeurIPS’22, CVPR’21, and MLSys’23, with awards from Amazon Research Awards to boot. In my Ph.D. study, I aim to expand my perspective and focus on the most impactful issues at hand. Below, I summarize my research development.

Large-scale Machine Learning algorithms: My ML journey commenced during my undergraduate studies under the guidance of Prof. Zhuowen Tu. Our initial findings highlighted that existing generative models were limited in their application scope, hindering broader usage. Incorporating an instance-level contrastive loss showed promise for well-rounded performance. However, its effective utilization required a substantial number of negative samples, far beyond the storage capacity of a single GPU. To tackle this scalability issue, I drew from relevant literature and employed data-parallel distributed training. The resulting model was the first generative model capable of simultaneous image synthesis at the level of Generative Adversarial Nets (GAN) and image reconstruction and representation learnability at the Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE) level. This work was accepted at CVPR 2021 as a co-first author, underscoring the potential for system support to unlock new opportunities for improvement beyond model designs alone.

Democratizing distributed ML training: Curiosity piqued, I joined Prof. Eric Xing’s group during my master's at CMU to explore ML Systems. Through investigation of contemporary ML training systems like DeepSpeed, I observed that they catered primarily to experts in designing model-parallel strategies while addressing limited industry scenarios. In practice, however, many individuals are system outsiders, yet they are still engaged in training beyond these confined setups. To create a general solution, I proposed a framework capable of identifying effective model-parallel strategies for various models and clusters.

An automatic approach necessitates thorough evaluation of model-parallel strategies; however, this is time-consuming due to the exponential number of possibilities. To enable automation, I devised a two-step method. First, I created a cost model as a fast proxy for real trials. In this process, I dissect model parallelism into distinct components and analyze each against wall clock execution time. For example, I discovered that communication in tensor model parallelism is asymptotically constant to the number of workers. Second, I developed a dynamic programming-based optimization procedure to reduce the number of evaluations to a manageable polynomial count. The resulting framework, AMP, marks the first automatic approach in the complex and expressive space of data parallelism, tensor model parallelism, and pipeline model parallelism. It returns strategies that outperform expert-designed ones by 1.54x and 1.77x on heterogeneous clusters and models setup, respectively, while matching them on industrial setups. This work was accepted at NeurIPS 2022 as the first author, under the supervision of Prof. Eric Xing and Prof. Hao Zhang.

Model-parallel communication compression: While validating the cost model in AMP, I identified that communication is a significant bottleneck in model parallelism. I collaborated with Prof. Shivaram Venkataraman to explore model-parallel compression as a potential solution for various real-world setups, including different families of compression methods, hyper-parameters, and hardware. Our study revealed that only learning-based compressors demonstrated an acceptable tradeoff between ML performance and system throughput. Based on the gathered throughput data, I further developed a cost model to help users predict the speedup and decide whether to employ learning-based compressors for their training needs. This work is currently under submission at MLSys 2023 as a co-first author.

Privacy-preserving ML model serving: Positive feedback from earlier projects convinced me of the importance of both performance and usability in ML systems. This insight led me to delve into enhancing usability in another type of ML system – serving systems. In collaboration with my supervisors, I discovered that many users were hesitant about using services like GitHub Copilot due to privacy concerns stemming from the requirement to send code to the cloud. To address this demand for privacy, I proposed an inference framework based on Secure Multi-party computation (MPC).

However, MPC raised new real-world concerns, as many functions in Transformers, such as Softmax, require substantial communication, resulting in unacceptably slow inferences. Moreover, different service providers adopt various serving systems, necessitating a general and effective solution for multiple MPC systems. To tackle this, I formulated an approximation abstraction that replaces slow functions with their MPC-friendly counterparts. This abstraction allows for a 12.5x speedup for Softmax while introducing a minimal drop in ML performance. I further introduced a Knowledge Distillation (KD)-based solution, which positions the original Transformer as the teacher. The resulting framework, MPCFormer, co-designs the ML model, the training algorithm, and the MPC system to deliver well-rounded performance in terms of privacy, latency, and ML performance. In particular, it demonstrates a 5.93x speedup compared to contemporary BERT models serving in MPC with comparable ML performance. This work is currently under submission at ICLR 2023 as the first author, under the supervision of Prof. Eric Xing and Prof. Hao Zhang. This research has been recognized by Amazon Research Awards ($80,000 funding) and accepted at ICLR 2023 as a spotlight (top 25% in accepted papers).

Engineering Efforts: In addition to research, I derive immense satisfaction from engineering efforts that contribute to impactful ML systems. Highlights include co-developing the NCCL backend for the Ray system, designing several resource scheduling policies for AdaptDL, and building the recommendation system for UniLink, an AI startup that attracted over 200 users. These experiences have significantly influenced my research problem choice, as I found iterating with real users, addressing problems they care about, and witnessing my codes in actual use to be incredibly rewarding.

Future Plans and Fit: A Ph.D. represents a stepping stone on my path towards enhancing ML system support for broader real-world application, ultimately aiming to enrich the lives of many. Beyond generic ML systems, I am also intrigued by specific application domains such as ML for science (e.g., biology) and ML for augmenting human abilities (e.g., through program synthesis). My long-term career goal encompasses both professorship and entrepreneurship, as I believe that academia and industrial environments offer unique opportunities for gaining insight into real-world needs and developing effective system support.
