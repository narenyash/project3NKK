Revamped Essay:

From my formative years, I have been driven by a fervent passion for fostering a harmonious and equitable society – one that cherishes freedom, nurtures individuals of diverse backgrounds, and offers them an opportunity to flourish. This dream was fortified when I encountered cryptography, a tool that promises privacy and security in the digital realm. The emergence of blockchain technology, with its potential to redistribute control from central authorities to users while enhancing system trust, served as a catalyst for my curiosity.

This fascination led me down a path toward distributed systems and algorithms. I am convinced that applying computer science principles to develop protocols that fortify the security and privacy of our systems can lead to groundbreaking advancements – a pursuit I aim to persue as a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Southern California, collaborating with leading experts in this domain.

To satiate my initial curiosity about blockchain and cryptocurrencies, I embarked on an enlightening journey during the summer of 2022 as a research assistant at the Data Analytic lab, University of Tehran, under the guidance of Prof. Behnam Bahrak. My mission was to design a novel blockchain consensus protocol, focusing on addressing high energy consumption – a significant issue plaguing Bitcoin and many other cryptocurrencies. Through an in-depth review of state-of-the-art literature, we discovered that Proof of Activity (PoA), first introduced in 2014, could provide promising enhancements. We devised a strategy to incorporate user activities within the blockchain mining process, ultimately rewarding active users with a greater chance of mining the next block. My key contribution was designing the activity system mathematically, ensuring both the resilience and security of the system against malicious actors, while maximizing honest users' profits. Furthermore, I proposed an evaluation framework based on game-theory concepts for our protocol – similar to the Prisoner’s Dilemma problem. This innovative approach incentivizes users to increase their contributions to the system, thereby enhancing security, stability, and dynamism. Our research culminated in the creation of Crystalline, a distributed file-sharing system powered by this protocol, serving as a proof of concept. The manuscript detailing our work is currently under review at the IET Blockchain journal.

My passion for privacy preservation techniques in cryptocurrencies led me to undertake a research internship at the ACIS lab, University of Victoria, under the tutelage of Prof. Homayoun Najjaran. My focus was on exploring mixing and obfuscation methods within cryptocurrencies – a domain heavily reliant on cryptography and graph theory. While there have been numerous proposals for mixing services in both academia and industry, I recognized a lack of comprehensive analysis and evaluation. To address this void, we embarked on writing a survey paper that would provide an exhaustive exploration of mixing solutions. My role as the principal author allowed me to shape the structure of our research methodology, identify and define essential mixing functionalities crucial for anonymity, analyze over 32 different mixing services, and develop an evaluation framework designed to assess their effectiveness while highlighting open problems and challenges in this domain. These experiences have equipped me with the ability to tackle complex issues systematically – a valuable asset for my future research endeavors as a Ph.D. student at the University of Southern California. My first-author manuscript resulting from this research is currently under review by the Journal of Network and Computer Applications.

In an era where digital systems play an increasingly integral role in our daily lives, safeguarding personal information online has become paramount. With artificial intelligence (AI) driving a wave of revolutionary innovations, the realms of security and privacy are poised to become central concerns for the future. I believe that cryptography presents a promising avenue to address these challenges, offering fertile ground for fruitful research. Additionally, my interest in distributed systems and algorithms stems from their ability to provide scalable, consistent, fault-tolerant solutions for modern computing challenges. With my sights set on becoming a leading expert in this field, I am committed to dedicating the prime years of my life to conducting groundbreaking research – a journey that will lead me toward discovering uncharted research territories and contributing meaningfully to society.

The University of Southern California's Viterbi School of Engineering stands as my top choice for my Ph.D. studies, given its renowned research groups in security, privacy, and distributed system domains. My research interests align well with Prof. Sirvastan Ravi’s work on distributed algorithms, particularly his project on concurrency and security of smart contracts. I am also intrigued by Prof. Seo Jin Park's research on distributed systems and flash burst computing, as well as Prof. Cyrus Shahabi's work on fairness and differential privacy. In the long term, I aspire to continue my path as a researcher, leveraging the expertise I will acquire from USC Viterbi and its brilliant faculty/students to secure a position in top-tier research institutes and industries.
