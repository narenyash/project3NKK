Title: The Symphony of Potatoes: A Journey of Identity and Growth

Growing up amidst the aromatic haze of our family's Chinese restaurant, I developed a peculiar distaste for the common potato skin. As the restaurant's stove cooled down, I would find myself by a bucket of water, meticulously peeling each tater with an apple peeler, the device soon becoming clogged with the bruised epidermis of the vegetable. Yet beneath this unassuming exterior lies the marvelous, golden-hued potato that my family and I hold in high regard.

In many American households, dessert is a sweet symphony of pastries and chilled confections. However, in our home, the evening's sweet finale revolves around the humble baked potato, a tradition that has been passed down through generations. On slumberous Monday evenings, we would pile into our family's Nissan Pathfinder, navigating through the undulating hills of middle Tennessee to reach the nearest O’Charley’s, renowned for their delectable baked potatoes.

The beauty of the baked potato lies in its versatility. The range of toppings is vast: from a pat of butter to a sprinkle of chives to even a dusting of dried pork floss, each ingredient plays its own melodic part in this culinary symphony. With the generous portion of chives I add, I can confidently say that I am consuming my daily serving of vegetables, a fact that brings me both delight and surprise.

Yet, as I continued to savor these humble tubers, I found myself yearning for more than just flavor. Perhaps it was the relentless pull of social media, urging comparisons with others to unhealthy extremes. Or perhaps it was a subconscious longing to compensate for the limitations of our family's financial situation and the frequent moves we had endured during my formative years. This hunger led to a decline in academic performance, oversleeping, and a general lack of motivation.

In the midst of this tumultuous period, I discovered an unexpected source of growth. Each potato that I placed against my peeler was marred with gnarly blemishes and mole-like protrusions on its skin. Yet, on rare occasions, I would come across one that bore sprouts - these "potato eyes" captivated me. Each potato could spawn another, a reminder of the countless potatoes I could consume in my lifetime, and that I didn't need to overload my potato as if it was my last. This realization served as a stark contrast to the global history of the potato, a New World commodity that was spread across continents through the Columbian Exchange, gradually adapting to European conditions.

Born feet-first and reluctant to speak until three, I learned the importance of perseverance at an early age. I still find solace in flipping through weathered pages of National Geographics, enchanted by the ancient artifacts and colossal temples of civilizations long past. I have traversed a diverse array of landscapes, from the bustling streets of Coney Island to the palm-fringed shores of the Carolinas, even braving the potholes along the way.

Each plate of orange chicken and white rice consumed after a long day at the family restaurant serves as a poignant reminder of what truly matters - the love and support that has sustained me through thick and thin. People may joke about those who order the same meal off the menu, or those who choose the least spicy option at the local Thai restaurant. But I have learned to appreciate the beauty in simplicity, finding comfort in the familiar warmth of a baked Russet potato. After all, potatoes come from humble beginnings, and it is essential to remember my own roots as I continue on this journey of life.
