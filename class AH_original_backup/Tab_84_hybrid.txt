Title: Transforming Communication and Society Through Intelligent Systems: My Journey Toward a PhD at Cornell University's Information Science Program

The Information Science (IS) PhD program at Cornell University presents a singular opportunity for me to merge my background in human-computer interaction (HCI), social science, and humanities with the computational expertise required to innovate AI-mediated communication systems. In an era where interpersonal interactions are increasingly mediated by algorithms, I envision a future where community-driven intelligent systems comprehend human intent, adapt to feedback, and foster personalized digital exchanges that augment human-human communication and collaboration online. My primary interests lie in (1) investigating informal learning and community development on online platforms, and (2) designing and developing human-in-the-loop AI systems that support these processes. Upon graduation, I aim to secure tenure-track professorships specializing in human-AI interaction, computational social science, and social computing.

Online platforms such as Reddit and YouTube have emerged as fertile ground for distributed collaborative initiatives across diverse sectors like education, healthcare, and work. Yet, significant hurdles persist in the integration of large-scale machine learning (ML) methodologies with human-centered perspectives in social computing to foster seamless, value-sensitive, labor-efficient social interaction. For instance, classification algorithms for hate speech, trained without contextual awareness, can unintentionally exacerbate preexisting biases within smaller online communities [1]. These predicaments motivate my desire to delve into human-AI interaction theory and sociotechnical systems to scrutinize AI's impact on society.

My journey in social computing, computational social science, and technical HCI has equipped me with the necessary tools to navigate this research path. Under the mentorship of Dr. Geoff Kaufman and Dr. Joseph Seering during my Master's in Human-Computer Interaction at Carnegie Mellon University (CMU), I conducted semi-structured interviews, employing grounded theory, to develop a cross-platform theory on the influence of human moderation on online community development. Our findings underscored the vital role moderators play in the growth and sustainability of online communities by curbing antisocial behavior and facilitating constructive discussions [2]. However, their complex decision-making process for handling violations presents a significant challenge that requires further exploration to ensure automated governance effectively upholds community norms.

Post-graduation from CMU, I joined Google as a User Experience Researcher, where I leveraged techniques such as diary studies, usability testing, and large-scale surveys to inform interface design for content on Google Maps. This experience enriched my understanding of mixed-methods research, honing my skills in data collection, analysis, and interpretation.

To broaden my expertise in ML algorithms, I pursued an MS in CS at the Georgia Institute of Technology. Guided by Dr. Diyi Yang at Stanford University, my recent research delved into the impact of conversations with non-professional volunteer counselors on mental health support seekers on an online therapy platform. In collaboration with two other Master’s students, we devised a data science pipeline that encompassed data collection from a SQL database, feature engineering using computational linguistics techniques in Python, and regression modeling in R [3]. Our findings suggested that while individual support may not significantly influence seeker mood, it could potentially impact the platform's overall retention of counselors as integral members of the community. As first author on a paper in submission to CHI 2023, I propose that tailored metrics and tools for counseling impact can help platforms maintain a stable community of counselors.

To further hone my engineering skills for sociotechnical systems, I collaborated with Dr. Haijun Xia at the University of California San Diego’s Design Lab to develop a videoconferencing system that discerned intent from real-time speech, retrieved information relevant to ongoing discussions, and intelligently suggested interface actions [4]. My principal contribution was the design of novel language-oriented interactions. By analyzing recorded meetings through a videography study, I identified both implicit and explicit cues people use in remote meetings to direct others' attention. To materialize the prototype, I also designed interface mockups in Figma, implemented a semantic search engine in Python using Transformers, and led user testing with 17 participants. Although language model capabilities have evolved significantly in recent years, designing real-time interactions for mixed-initiative language user interfaces remains a challenge that I eagerly aim to address through future research in technical HCI.

Cornell offers an unparalleled platform for integrating social and computational perspectives in AI-mediated communication and its impact on society. I am particularly interested in collaborating with Dr. Mor Naaman to develop systems that uphold human values in online governance and discourse. My academic journey would be enriched through further exploration of human-AI interaction design theory under the guidance of Dr. Qian Yang. Studying computational social science methods with Dr. Cristian Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil will enable me to deepen my understanding of analyzing conversational behavior at scale for moderation and counseling purposes. Lastly, learning from Dr. Malte Jung's work with social robots aligns with my interests in human-in-the-loop AI, as I look forward to gaining insights into the application of intelligent agents in facilitating human-human collaboration within distributed teams.

My non-traditional path to computing has instilled an interdisciplinary, human-centered approach not only in scholarly pursuits but also in pedagogy. Currently, I teach a masters-level Introduction to HCI course as an Adjunct Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Pace University, using user-centered design methods to gather feedback from students and continually improve the course syllabus. Upon earning my PhD, I aspire to nurture a new generation of human-centered scientists, designers, and engineers who can address the challenges and opportunities presented by an increasingly AI-mediated future.
