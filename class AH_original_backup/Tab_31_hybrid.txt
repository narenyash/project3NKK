Tab 31 Hybrid Version:

The sun's radiant beams illuminated my windshield one fateful morning, as I journeyed towards the confines of a corporate office. However, this routine commute was abruptly disrupted by the soothing cadence of Billy Collins, former U.S. Poet Laureate, reciting his poem "Introduction to Poetry" on the radio. As he finished, I felt an inexplicable compulsion to pull over and sit on the curb, lost in contemplation. This moment marked the beginning of my life-changing pursuit to conquer a longstanding personal challenge – my stutter – a transformation that was deeply intertwined with poetry.

That morning, I was no stranger to the agony that accompanied public speaking. My school days were filled with a cacophony of unintelligible syllables during roll calls: "...Roll number 21?" "Present!" "22?" "Present, miss!" "23?" "P...p...p..." "...24?" I was roll number 23, and my speech impediment loomed as a colossal obstacle in everyday life. Simple tasks like answering questions in class or engaging in conversations felt like insurmountable hurdles.

My odyssey to conquer stuttering commenced when poetry was introduced as part of the school curriculum. In this literary realm, I discovered courage and inspiration that forever altered my existence. P.K. Page's poem "This Heavy Craft" still resonates within me, mirroring my early struggles: "I, Icarus, though grounded / in my flesh / have one bright section in me / where a bird / night after starry night / while I'm asleep / unfolds its phantom wings / and practices." In the story of Icarus, I recognized my own battle against adversity.

Drawn by poetry's fluidity, I began reciting verses to myself in front of a mirror. The measured rhythm of verse coerced me to articulate clearly and helped me identify the letters that triggered my stutter – "p," "d," "n," and "s." Through diligent practice, I identified patterns in my speech, such as the need for controlled breathing and reduced anxiety when confronted with challenging words.

As my confidence blossomed, I stepped out of my solitary comfort zone to engage with others. Initially, these encounters were fraught with humiliation, but even the smallest victories – a five-minute conversation in a small group, or a stanza recited before an audience of fifty – sustained my resolve.

Embracing the art of composition was the next logical step in my journey. Determined not to retreat into solitude again, I sought out local writing communities wherever I resided. For the past eight years, I've nurtured and managed an online poetry club that originated at IIT-Bombay. In Austin, I established the city's first poetry club, expanded it from a nucleus of five members to over one hundred in just one year, and organized monthly readings at coffee shops and book clubs. Today, my flourishing club continues to inspire others.

Reading poetry aloud in public not only improved my speech but also fueled the creative evolution of my own verses. In 2009, I was awarded a National Poetry Award in India – a testament not only to my literary skills but also to my relentless pursuit of self-improvement and my unyielding spirit in the face of years spent grappling with self-doubt.

As I ascended the stage to read my poem, my hands trembled with excitement. The audience of 500 fell silent, transporting me back to my bedroom – a child facing the mirror. Once again, I was Icarus: wings unfurled, prepared for flight. With a deep breath, I commenced.
