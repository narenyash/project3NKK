Like many European nations, most witnessed a decline in individuals at risk of poverty or social exclusion by late 2017. However, my home country bucked this trend [1], with an alarming statistic revealing that approximately one out of five Maltese citizens fell into such a category. This revelation struck me during a weekend-long hackathon, spurring me to ponder ways in which loneliness among the elderly could be alleviated through the strategic use of social media and language. This experience served as the catalyst for my burgeoning interests at the intersection of NLP and computational social science. As a result, I am driven to explore the following research questions and career aspirations, aiming to harness language's power to bring about positive societal change.

1. How can we employ language to comprehend human behavior and guide policy addressing societal issues? My studies in NLP at the University of Malta deepened my understanding of language, particularly its ability to unintentionally reveal our thoughts and shape our self-presentation [2]. Inspired by this, I collaborated with Dr. Charlie Abela on a research project that aimed to explore the connection between users' personalities and their social networks within a real-world Twitter community. By utilizing a broad range of personality-annotated datasets and psycholinguistic lexicons, such as LIWC and Sensicon, we trained machine learning models to identify users' personality traits from their own tweets and compared them against an open-vocabulary alternative [3]. We subsequently incorporated users' personality preferences for the accounts they followed into topological link predictors and demonstrated improved accuracy. This project not only provided me with valuable experience in initiating and leading a research project concerning NLP and network science but also resulted in a first-author publication at the ACM Symposium on Applied Computing [4] and culminated in my graduation with the highest honors. I was particularly proud of these achievements, considering my personal background and the limited opportunities available at my university.

In future research, I hope to delve deeper into how individual differences manifest themselves in linguistic expression and their impact on broader social dynamics, such as group cohesion, misinformation spread, bias perpetuation, and others. By doing so, I aim to shed light on collective behaviors and understand why individuals behave the way they do when confronted with these phenomena. My ultimate objective is to inform social science research and contribute to healthier, more inclusive societies. However, achieving this goal necessitates ethically grounded NLP models.

2. How can we develop NLP models and pipelines that effectively mitigate potential societal risks and maintain their ethical integrity? During my coursework in 'LIN3012 Data-driven NLP,' I investigated the consequences of overexposing NLP models to real-world data, specifically gender bias within Maltese word embeddings [5]. I demonstrated how certain occupational roles were biased towards men, with few corresponding biases present for women. To mitigate this effect, I removed gender inflections and applied the 'hard-debias' method [6]. The limited prior efforts addressing bias in my native language made this project challenging, but it highlighted the difficulties surrounding gender bias mitigation techniques in gender-marked languages. I am now drawn to research focused on uncovering potential risks associated with NLP technologies, especially those that manifest themselves in various forms and across different languages. Additionally, I also hope to contextualize such a pursuit within an intersectional perspective, considering social dimensions such as race and class.

Unfortunately, the societal impact of NLP technologies extends beyond the propagation of biases. During my master's at Stockholm University, I collaborated with Prof. Panagiotis Papapetrou to investigate how podcast comprehension could be improved using a pipeline combining topic segmentation and text summarization methods [7]. The short time frame allocated to this project presented a significant challenge, prompting me to reflect on the broader ethical implications of such work. For instance, text summarization models trained on human-generated text or evaluated by annotators with skewed demographics are prone to producing inaccurate or even harmful perceptions of the original content [8]. I aspire to delve deeper into how ethical frameworks could be established for similar NLP pipelines, aiming to ensure their desirable outputs when deployed.
