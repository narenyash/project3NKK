Passion for Mathematics and Machine Learning: As a mathematics major, I find immense joy in unraveling the mysteries behind specific mathematical methods, particularly within Machine Learning (ML). My primary interests lie in Computational Learning Theory (CoLT) and the Foundations of Deep Learning (DL). I am captivated by the intricate process of developing and optimizing bounds on relevant values, as these bounds offer assurances and provide deeper insights into the enigmatic black-box machine learning systems. The paradoxical nature of DL models, despite their high capacity and inherent instability, generalizes well in practice, a phenomenon that I find intriguing. My ambition is to delve deeper into this field to understand the paradoxical behavior of DL models and explore the realm of Deep Reinforcement Learning, Meta-Learning, Neural Operators, and contribute to AI4Science projects. Among all institutions, Caltech stands out as my top choice for graduate research due to its renowned Ph.D. program, under the esteemed supervision of Professor Anima Anandkumar.

Academic Journey: My academic journey commenced during my sophomore year when I embarked on an internship with Microsoft as a Data Science Intern, collaborating with Microsoft Research Redmond and China. There, I laid the groundwork for my team by investigating underutilized Azure clusters, which were costing the company significant money and resources. Through data mining and developing interpretable classifiers, I aimed to solve the bin packing problem more efficiently. My industry experience offered autonomy over my work, a collaborative environment, and a supportive system that left a lasting impression. However, I harbored questions about my research direction moving forward.

Further Explorations: Subsequently, I joined the Reinforcement Learning and Artificial Intelligence lab at the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute under professors Martha White and Adam White (DeepMind). There, I worked on two projects: one investigating the differences between Kernelizing Bayesian Linear Regression (BLR) versus Gaussian Processes Regression (GPR), and another optimizing the MinAtar codebase. The former project helped me understand the different representability powers of these methods through functional analysis, leading to a deeper appreciation for machine learning theory. The latter project saw me implement various theoretical properties, reduce training times by almost 50%, and increase the efficiency of algorithms and PyTorch optimizations. I also joined Professor Anima Anandkumar’s (Nvidia) lab at Caltech as a research intern, where we aimed to tackle the fundamental question: "Why are Neural Networks so good at generalizing data?" from the perspective of the Fourier space, investigating the relationship between the Implicit Bias of gradient descent and the Spectral Bias that Neural Networks exhibit. Our work on Incremental Fourier Neural Operators has been accepted in the NeurIPS 2022 AI for Science Workshop.

Beyond Academia: Beyond my academic pursuits, I am passionate about projects that make a positive impact on society and where Neural Networks play a significant role. I have had opportunities to work on climate change, healthcare, and PDEs projects. Last winter semester, I joined the Artificial Intelligence Computational Team, developing algorithms to predict weather patterns in Western Canada. Although my work was implemented as part of a $3 million project, it highlighted the challenge of applying deep learning for applications requiring dependability due to DL models' lack of performance guarantees.

Research Aspirations: My research goals encompass contributing to a better understanding of ML models, providing mathematical frameworks for designing new robust and stable ML algorithms, bridging the gap between theory and practice, and proposing improvements to DNN-based systems inspired by recent theoretical advances such as Neural Collapse, Double Descent, Grokking, Spectral Bias, and Benign Overfitting. I am also intrigued by the topic of Geometric Complexity, a recent study that I wish to explore further to understand better the performance and generalization capabilities of Deep Learning models. Furthermore, I am curious about understanding representational learning, the internal structure of DL models, and their ability for abstract thinking.
