Title: Unraveling the Intricacies of Language Model Behaviors: A Holistic Approach to Evaluation and Capability Induction

My primary research focus lies at the nexus of natural language generation (NLG) and understanding the underlying mechanisms that govern language model behaviors. I am captivated by the versatility of general-purpose language models, their ability to tackle a diverse range of generation tasks. Yet, it remains unclear how these models acquire high-level generation capabilities through various training data and dominant learning paradigms. To delve deeper into this intriguing conundrum, I aim to construct an analysis model that elucidates the factors enabling language models to learn specific capabilities—like a sturdy tree with deep roots, it will not be swayed by the winds of uncertainty.

Concretely, my research interests converge around two primary areas: (i) developing fine-grained evaluation frameworks that meticulously identify and assess what specific capabilities language models lack and (ii) exploring the role of synthetic data in inducing desired abilities into language models for further enhancement. By establishing a solid foundation for understanding language model behaviors, we can glean insights to enhance their ability to tackle more complex tasks—akin to a complex systems scientist peeling back layers of an intricate machine.

1. A HOLISTIC APPROACH TO EVALUATION WITH FINE-GRAINED CRITERIA

In the realm of AI, evaluating language models poses unique challenges, particularly when it comes to assessing long-form responses. Traditional evaluation methods, such as employing reference-based metrics or using coarse-grained criteria like Helpfulness and Harmlessness often fail to capture the depth and granularity that human evaluation offers. To address this, I believe a fine-grained evaluation framework is necessary—one that includes explicit criteria for each instance, enabling language models to serve as judges with discerning eyes.

My colleagues and I have developed such a framework in two of my publications [1, 2]. This approach proved effective at pinpointing specific areas where language models fall short. For example, when evaluating a response for a FinTech startup’s strategy, it is more instructive to assess the omitted details about crucial aspects like regulations and compliance, rather than assigning a simplistic Harmlessness score.

However, scaling up fine-grained evaluation poses a significant challenge, as it demands extensive effort to tailor criteria to specific instances. One promising direction is creating a benchmark that includes a compositional hierarchy of language model capabilities—a structured framework that categorizes each evaluation instance by the abilities it requires, enabling community contributions and streamlined testing.

Moreover, this fine-grained approach can be expanded into different modalities, such as vision-language models assessing text outputs given an image input [3]. By evaluating AI-generated outputs using interpretable, detailed criteria, we can gain a nuanced understanding of the model’s behaviors and its ability to generalize.

2. INDUCING DESIRED CAPABILITIES WITH SYNTHETIC DATA

Another captivating direction in my research is the use of generative models to produce high-quality data, thereby inducing specific generative behaviors in language models. The recent success in augmenting synthetic data to improve instruction-following abilities in language models has been a promising advancement. This progress excites me, as it hints at the potential for synthetic data to unlock even more capabilities. I am keen to explore how synthetic data can be utilized to specifically address the deficiencies of language models that are identified through fine-grained evaluation—akin to humans learning from their mistakes, where the language model is iteratively improved by being exposed to data that targets its specific shortcomings.

One particular area I have delved into is inducing step-by-step reasoning abilities in language models. In my prior publications [4, 5, 6], I have demonstrated that incorporating synthetic rationales and commonsense inferences significantly enhance a model’s reasoning abilities in various settings. However, an intriguing finding was that despite the trained model excelled at constructing logical flows, it often hallucinated and made simple calculation mistakes. Therefore, during my doctoral studies, I am interested in identifying which capabilities can be acquired through high-quality, machine-generated data and which skills are inherently unattainable, possibly due to constraints such as insufficient parameter size or lack of essential knowledge.

Additionally, a key question in my research is how we can efficiently induce multiple capabilities in a language model without it losing its pre-existing abilities. In some of my recent works [7, 8], I have introduced a modular approach that utilizes specialized checkpoints for each task and user-defined criteria. By employing post-hoc weight merging, this method not only showed improved performance on new, unseen tasks but also effectively aligned with diverse user preferences. This system's efficiency in adding new expert models without the risk of catastrophic forgetting—achieved by maintaining the differential (‘diff’) weights compared to the original model’s weights—suggests that it can successfully integrate new experts and adapt to evolving user interests.
