Title: "A Dog Named Finn: Navigating Life's Tides"

Each nightfall, my rescue Plott Hound, Finn, makes a habit of nudging open the door to my room every five minutes, his eager eyes reflecting the dim light. "Finn," I call out, "I haven't forgotten about you, buddy." With the dexterity of a seasoned acrobat, he dashes down the stairs, snatches the leash, and awaits my appearance by the door. Our nightly walks are a ritual I cherish. As I step out into the cool evening air, it envelopes me in a sense of tranquility that soothes the mental and physical strain of my other pursuits: school, filmmaking, and ice hockey.

The stillness of our sleeping town serves as my personal meditation chamber. These walks offer a respite amidst the relentless whirlwind of academics, video editing deadlines, and exhausting hockey practices. Whether I'm grappling with Econ homework, taking a breather from a demanding deadline, or returning from a grueling practice session, Finn's companionship provides a much-needed mental respite. The stark contrast between the frenetic pace of ice hockey and the serenity of our walks helps me find equilibrium in life's chaotic rhythm.

During our walks, I occasionally pause to allow Finn to sniff his surroundings. His nose, being that of a hound, is always on the hunt for new scents or familiar ones. As he delves into the world through his olfactory sensors, I find myself observing details visually that I had previously overlooked: the synchronized march of ants or the swift flash of a skunk's black and white tail. I find myself spinning intricate tales about these creatures and the circumstances that led to our encounters. In much the same way that Finn seeks new smells, I seek new perspectives.

Over the summer, I collaborated with members of the Mira Costa Cinema Club to craft a narrative short film titled "12:48." This poignant tale revolves around grief, misconception, and the main character's struggle with his perceived role in his friend's fatal car accident. As the lead writer for the film, I facilitated story meetings where we discussed dialogue flow, scene transitions, and script continuity. When I believed my writing was nearing completion, the members of our club shared their unique interpretations of the story, often differing from my own. A member who had recently lost her grandmother pointed out a missing element: the denial aspect of grief. In response to her insights, I incorporated a scene in which the main character, Mac, attempts to resume his daily routine before succumbing to the guilt that has been gnawing at him. My most impactful work stems from collaborations with individuals who bring diverse viewpoints to the scriptwriting process, resulting in a more layered and nuanced narrative.

If Finn had his way, we'd explore every blade of grass under the moonlight. I can hardly blame him for his insatiable curiosity. I share this trait with him. Like Finn, I am constantly sniffing out my next business venture. Much like following a scent, good ideas demand that I stay grounded and persistent. One evening, after a day spent at Disneyland, an idea struck me as we strolled along. I reflected on the long lines I had waited in, for an authentic, screen-accurate lightsaber - an item exclusive to Disney properties. People were willing to pay generously for these collectibles, myself included. The allure was undeniable. I recognized a niche market and began selling them worldwide. Although this business proved profitable, it had a limited shelf life. I remind myself to approach each success like a surfer riding the crest of a wave - savoring the ride, knowing that the wave will eventually crash. Yet, after every wave comes another opportunity to catch another one. I've experienced several triumphs and numerous failed startups, but my determination to find the next wave remains undeterred.

As Finn leads us on our nightly excursions, his curiosity serves as a guiding light for me. Although Finn won't be by my side forever, I hope to embody his spirit of discovery and devote time to contemplating new ideas and reflecting on my own decisions.
