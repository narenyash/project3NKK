THE EVOLVING "PARENTS' RELATIONSHIP" COLLEGE ESSAY EXAMPLE
Narrative Essay, “Challenge” Type

The year 2013 marked the establishment of Kanishka’s Gastropub, a venture my mother embarked upon with unbridled enthusiasm. This bold step made us the pioneering Mother-Son Indian duo gracing the Food Network, where we shared our beloved Bengali recipes and honed our skills in peeling potatoes, skinning chicken, and grinding spices. However, as the restaurant blossomed, it became an unwitting catalyst for the disintegration of my parents' bond.

Two years into the culinary journey, my father's late-night appearances, slurred from "happy hour with work colleagues," began to strain our family dynamic. My mother, juggling her day job at Kaiser and the demands of the restaurant, funneled her burgeoning stress towards me, voicing her frustration, "What on earth is wrong with you? Always engrossed in YouTube videos, never a word spoken!"

The most tumultuous period occurred when my parents attempted to mend their relationship. Frequent date nights only escalated the disputes. Plagued by the pressures of her restaurant, my father's indiscretions, and her own self-reproach, my mother contemplated ending her life. Fortunately, I intervened just in time.

Over the following two years, our family navigated through challenging times, albeit with gradual improvement. My parents made a conscious effort to restart their relationship, taking breaks and eventually reuniting. My mother started picking me up from activities on schedule, while my father and I bonded over watching Warriors and 49ers games. However, I still found myself stepping in to emotionally support my mother during unexpected trips to India or assisting with putting the younger children to bed if my parents were absent at night. The role of family mediator became increasingly taxing.

In search of solace, I sought refuge in creation, dedicating more time to crafting aircraft from foam sheets in our garage. Finding satisfaction in aligning fuselages or leveling ailerons with meticulous precision, I discovered a haven where I could correct all errors. Here, I found purpose and balance.

As I progressed through high school, I began forging connections with like-minded creators among my peers. In sophomore year, I established an engineering club, showcasing my ability to manage people and motivate them to bring ideas to life, even when faced with failure. I also developed resilience by learning how to accept feedback constructively. This environment allowed me to indulge in endless discussions about warp drives and the potential of anti-matter without being dismissed. Weekly reports on new technology were met with engaging conversations about the diverse applications of a hypothetical blacker material.

While nurturing a creative community at school helped rekindle my self-confidence, I still cherished moments of solitude. Driving in my car, my thoughts often wandered to visions of movies like Big Hero Six, contemplating the feasibility of a zero-friction bike or designing an AI highway system that intelligently suggested lane changes based on traffic patterns and mathematical calculations to minimize braking incidents. I even envisioned a novel classroom concept featuring interactive desks immersing students in historical events as if they were playing a VR game. These intricate ideas sometimes offered valuable insights into my ongoing research or provided seeds for potential future projects.

Reflecting on the turmoil from our restaurant days, I recognize the lessons that have shaped me. Guiding my mother through her emotional struggles instilled in me an awareness to watch out for those who are vulnerable. Spending sleepless nights fostered independence, as it was then that I enrolled in advanced math and programming courses and applied for software internships. Most importantly, witnessing my mother take the leap of faith in starting a restaurant without any food-industry experience ignited a spark within me to establish two clubs and a Hydrogen Car Team.

Although we now enjoy Luchi Mongsho once a month, I acknowledge that our family will never resemble its former state. We may not become the Food Network's mother-son duo or repair all the fractures, but I can utilize these experiences to enrich the present.
