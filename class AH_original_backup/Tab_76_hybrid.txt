In the vast landscape of artificial intelligence, several domains pique my curiosity; however, for my doctoral studies, I am drawn to delving deeply into natural language processing (NLP) and the interwoven questions at the junction of machine learning and public policy. Post-graduation, my aspirations lie in a research career spanning academia or industry.

My initial taste of research was during my sophomore year at undergraduate studies (February 2021), when I embarked on a project alongside Dr. Amitava Mukherjee (Adjunct Professor, SUNY Polytechnic Institute) and Dr. Arnab Raha (Staff Research Scientist, Advanced Architecture Research, Intel AI). This project centered around the development and distribution of deep learning models and layers on low-computing edge devices. Our unique graph-based, greedy solution, different from existing methods, offered comparable performance in some cases, even improving by 3-5%. Engaging directly with this project, observing its evolution from problem formulation to solution exploration, sparked my desire to traverse adjacent machine learning subfields.

Simultaneously, I was fortunate enough to work on an NLP project under Dr. Asif Ekbal (Department of CSE, Indian Institute of Technology Patna). In this venture, I focused on enhancing persona and generating dialogues aware of perception using NLP procedures and knowledge infusion through Knowledge-Graphs. Alongside reinforcing my understanding in Recurrent Neural Networks and Transformers, I absorbed advanced concepts such as Knowledge Graph, Graph Transformer, and Graph Neural Networks. This journey deepened my fascination with NLP methodologies, leading me to embark on another NLP project with Prof. Utpal Garain from Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, concerning hate speech recognition and phrase extraction in Bengali, a low-resource language, utilizing the HASOC dataset.

Having garnered substantial experience through these two projects, I reached out to Prof. KhudaBukhsh (Rochester Institute of Technology) for mentorship. His groundbreaking research paper, "We Don't Speak the Same Language: Interpreting Polarization through Machine Translation," served as a catalyst for my transformation. Intrigued by his innovative approach and general theme of AI for Social Good, I wrote to him and was granted the opportunity to work with him on a project that introduced the novel concept of web archeology. Dr. KhudaBukhsh provided me access to a distinctive dataset consisting of comments on YouTube channels deleted after the Capitol riot. Leveraging cutting-edge NLP techniques, we unearthed insights from these erased comments and observed disconcerting discourse surrounding election fraud, vaccine misinformation, and incitements to violence. Our work is currently slated for submission to either SIGIR 2023 (Perspective Track) or IJCAI 2023 (AI for Good Track), jointly authored with Adel Khorramrouz and Sujan Dutta, Ph.D. students of Dr. KhudaBukhsh.

My involvement in a seminar-style course led by Dr. KhudaBukhsh and Prof. Mark Kamlet at RIT-CMU on machine learning and political data provided an enthralling opportunity to acquaint myself with foundational papers at the intersection of machine learning, political science, and natural language processing. It was equally exhilarating to witness my project partner Adel Khorramrouz present our preliminary findings to Prof. Kamlet, a renowned political scientist and economist, during the final class project presentation.

A doctoral program in Computing and Information Sciences at the Rochester Institute of Technology under the tutelage of Dr. KhudaBukhsh offers an ideal platform for my research career progression for several reasons. First and foremost, our research interests resonate harmoniously. Second, I have already collaborated with his students, forming a community of highly talented researchers who share enthusiasm for AI for Good problems. Third, I am aware of Dr. KhudaBukhsh's ongoing collaborations with Prof. Christopher Homan. The synergy between their research programs presents an enticing opportunity to participate in joint projects. Lastly, RIT stands out as a champion of diversity and inclusion, while boasting a thriving computer science community. I am confident that this dynamic environment will foster my growth both academically and personally.
