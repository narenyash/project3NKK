Title: A Seattle Teenager Chasing Innovation: From Coding a Coronavirus Dashboard to Dreaming of a Sustainable Future

In the words of a dismayed CDC staffer, echoed in The New York Times, a Seattle teenager managed to compile coronavirus data faster than the agency itself - a testament to the power of youthful determination and technological ingenuity. If you had told me a year ago that Dr. Fauci would be presenting me with a Webby Award for coding one of the most significant websites of the year, I would have dismissed it as a far-fetched dream. At that time, I was still unfamiliar with the esteemed physician's name.

My journey into the realm of digital innovation began when I received my first computer at the tender age of six. Within a year, I found myself typing faster than my father, a skill that would serve me well in the years to come. By middle school, I had progressed from creating simple video games for personal enjoyment to selling them online, fueled by the exhilaration of sharing my creations with the world. As early high school approached, my ventures expanded to include the development of mobile apps, websites, and computer software - all of which earned me a lucrative income. As a 10th grader and the youngest competitor, I walked away from a major hackathon with $30,000 in prize money, having developed software for the US government.

This brings us to January 2020, when I received a text from a friend living in China. He spoke of his city going into lockdown due to an exceedingly contagious virus. As the child of a physician and a biochemist, this news piqued my curiosity. As I questioned my parents about it, their concerns began to mount, despite the fact that few around me were discussing the matter. When I sought more information, I found myself navigating Chinese government websites or outdated news articles riddled with advertisements.

It was clear that there had to be a better way, so I set out to create one. I developed a website that would automatically gather the most current data from Chinese health departments and present it in an easy-to-use dashboard. As the virus continued its relentless spread, my web scrapers expanded their reach, combing through every public health department across the globe. What started as a fascinating project transformed into a round-the-clock commitment, requiring me to work swiftly to code new web scrapers and keep my site up-to-date - eventually leading me to forgo formal education.

For many, COVID-19 has brought about ennui and stasis; but for me, the past year has been a whirlwind of events, sleepless nights, international speaking engagements, and early morning video calls with the United Nations. Within a few months of launching the site, the virus had escalated into a global pandemic, and I found myself on CNN (dressed in my pajamas) conversing with millions of people. My site now attracted more than 30 million visitors daily, largely from those unaware that it was being managed single-handedly by a teenager from Seattle.

Reflecting on the trials and tribulations of the past year, I can't help but feel that all the stress and lack of sleep were worth it. The significant impact I have made as an individual has instilled in me the motivation and self-assurance needed to tackle even more ambitious projects. Every day, I find myself pondering potential applications for cybernetics that could enable paralyzed individuals to walk again, or exploring the possibility of vertical farming to mitigate our planet's escalating climate crisis.

In college, I aim to acquire the skills necessary to transform these theoretical ideas into reality. I envision myself as an active member of the university community, eager to contribute and collaborate. Immersing myself in a diverse and intellectually stimulating environment will broaden my perspective and encourage me to seek more innovative solutions. The future promises to be both captivating and daunting, but it will be shaped by individuals like me and my peers, pushing the boundaries of what's possible.

As Steve Jobs once said, "The people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world, are the ones who do." I hope that my high school transcripts don't overshadow my track record, but instead serve as a testament to my potential - a potential that I believe is worth your university taking a chance on, as we embark on a joint mission to reshape the world together.
