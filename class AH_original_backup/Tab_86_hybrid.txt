Title: Pioneering Autonomous Computing Systems: A Journey Towards Self-Healing, Reliable, and Intelligent Infrastructure

In today's digital era, the relentless surge in computing demands has led to an unprecedented dependency on intricate cloud-based systems. However, these complex networks can succumb to high operational costs and disruptive hold-ups due to inefficient use (1). My research passions revolve around optimizing such systems through advancements in machine learning, bolstering resilience, and creating components that counteract disruptions. Primarily, I aspire to spearhead the development of autonomous and intelligent distributed computing systems capable of self-healing from degradation. This ambitious pursuit drives my decision to embark on a Ph.D. journey in Computer Science at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC).

Crafting autonomous systems necessitates tackling multiple subtasks, ranging from intelligent data ingestion and scheduling within distributed clusters to workload-specific optimizations, constructing efficient storage systems, analyzing and recovering from faults, and ensuring end-to-end reliability. During my college years, I honed my skills in designing distributed systems and system optimizations for federated learning and streaming applications. Presently, as a member of Adobe Research, I am contributing to enhancing the dependability of cloud microservices by scrutinizing system data, with a focus on automatically locating faults and forecasting outages within extensive microservice deployments (2). Unraveling the root cause of a fault can be formidable due to the dynamic interplay between modules. A burgeoning body of work aims to detect causal relationships among multiple performance metrics of entire microservices, reconstruct the underlying system architecture using graph-based algorithms, and pinpoint the root causes (3).

However, prior methodologies for unearthing these causal dependencies often overlook the deployment strategy of microservices, which spawns multiple instances/pods for reliability and load balancing purposes, or average metric data monitored for each instance, resulting in a loss of valuable information. We addressed this predicament through "CausIL," an innovative solution that utilizes (i) metrics variations for each instance deployed per service by considering these instances as conditionally independent of other instances and (ii) generic domain knowledge common to any microservice system to identify the causal relationships. The adaptability of CausIL to cope with instances' dynamic nature, typically observed in a microservice deployment due to autoscaler configuration, renders it more effective than existing approaches in identifying the causal structure of systems (4).

Even though CausIL surpasses previous methodologies, limitations may arise when domain knowledge is unavailable. Additionally, conventional causal structure detection algorithms grapple with scalability and often rely on parametric assumptions. In collaboration with Prof. Saurabh Bagchi and Prof. Murat Kocaoglu from Purdue University, we devised a hierarchical and localized causal algorithm to detect root causes for a fault in the absence of domain knowledge, which was accepted at NeurIPS 2022 (5). This cutting-edge solution leverages causal techniques to pinpoint interventional targets and hence the root causes by incorporating an additional proxy node in the causal graph. The scalability of this algorithm stems from its ability to identify interventional targets without requiring a comprehensive causal identification of the entire system architecture. These research experiences have shed light on issues within deployed systems that warrant attention (6).

Beyond these research endeavors, I have been fortunate enough to bring some ideas and prototypes to life in practical settings at Adobe Research. Overcoming obstacles inherent in engineering domain-specific features, handling distributional shifts in data, managing misleading information, and facilitating continuous learning calls for significant effort (7). In the process, I worked on explaining potential outages for a production service from service alerts and predicting time-to-live for streaming jobs without knowledge of the system configuration running them. Integrating these research projects into the engineering stack has significantly influenced my research perspective when working with practical systems.

My fascination with enhancing system performance predates Adobe Research. My undergraduate thesis at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kharagpur, under the guidance of Prof. Sandip Chakraborty, focused on constructing an adaptive 360-degree video streaming system to minimize bandwidth consumption for streaming (8). We discovered that streaming the entire frame of a 360-degree video unnecessarily consumes bandwidth since the user's gaze (viewport) is confined to specific regions. This observation led us to develop PARIMA, an intelligent, low-latency streaming system that predicts viewports in advance via online regression on the viewer's localized region of interest and video characteristics. Additionally, we allocated higher bandwidth solely to predicted viewport regions without compromising user quality of experience (QoE). The success of this project boosted my confidence and fueled my passion for further research on designing intelligent systems that tackle real-world challenges.

My master's thesis project, advised by Prof. Sandip Chakraborty on the utilization of smart contracts to enable Federated Learning (FL) and its application to ensure data privacy and accountability, solidified my interest in system design (9). We developed a system optimized for ML model training across silos using federated networks by employing blockchain interoperability. This solution was motivated by the idea that cross-silo federated networks can empower enterprises to train superior models without sharing data. Our work, published as a full paper in ICBC 2022, proposed a relay mechanism native to individual federated networks that mediates the process of model exchange following appropriate authentications. In the process, we created a scalable and adaptable federated system allowing heterogeneous clients, with Kafka serving as the communication network between the server and clients. The experience of designing an end-to-end system ignited my curiosity in dedicated research on optimized system design.

A multitude of research engagements coupled with a practical perspective towards problems instills confidence in me to pursue graduate studies to achieve my research goals. I firmly believe that these unique experiences qualify me to tackle intricate system design research problems and realize a self-healing, autonomous, reliable, and intelligent system. During my tenure at UIUC, I aim to make large distributed systems more efficient and robust under dynamic workloads by incorporating learnable components to drive their performance and intelligence. In this pursuit, I am particularly keen on collaborating with Prof. Aishwarya Ganesan, Prof. Indranil Gupta, Prof. Ramnatthan Alagappan, and Prof. Yongjoo Park. The researchers' interests in creating more reliable and efficient storage systems resonate with my research objectives (10). Their work on utilizing learned indexes for storage systems, such as Bourbon, intrigues me, as these techniques can be adapted to optimize reads and writes for range queries in distributed systems. Concurrently, Prof. Gupta's team's work on building efficient cloud computing and distributed systems aligns with my aspirations (11). Their research on resource utilization and performance isolation for streaming systems and graph processing systems sparked my interest. Query time prediction and scheduling hot over cold clusters for dynamic graph queries can be a possible research avenue, crucial to maintaining Service Level Agreements (SLAs). I also believe I can contribute to Prof. Park's vision of building an adaptive and intelligent data-driven computing system, with AirDB projects targeting efficient storage for cloud systems seem appealing. Additional capabilities to make the cloud systems more reliable upon failure can be incorporated.

The graduate program at UIUC will offer me ample exposure to the multifaceted requirements essential to design a dependable and intelligent system that can help me achieve my research objectives. A focused research environment compounded by the coursework provided by the Ph.D. program will be vital for me to evolve into an independent researcher, generate visionary ideas with abstractness and practicality, and construct a potentially beneficial system. In conclusion, I am excited about embarking on the next phase of my academic journey as a graduate student at UIUC.
