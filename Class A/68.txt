
I am a student who enjoys participating in technical competitions because they give me an opportunity to apply what I learn in a practical environment. My interest in computer science has grown through academics and personal projects, but hackathons have provided a different type of learning experience. They have taught me how to work under time constraints, collaborate with others, learn new technologies quickly, and turn an idea into a working prototype.

My first experiences with technical competitions made me realise that they are different from regular academic assignments. In academics, the problem and the expected method are often clearly defined. In a hackathon, the problem may be open-ended, and the team has to decide what solution to develop.

This gives the participants more freedom, but it also creates more responsibility.

The first step is usually understanding the problem statement. Before deciding which technology to use, I try to understand what the actual problem is, who is affected by it, and what kind of solution could be useful. This has helped me realise that a technically advanced project is not necessarily a good project if it does not address the problem properly.

Once the problem is understood, the team needs to decide what can realistically be built within the available time.

This is one of the biggest lessons I have learned from hackathons.

There can be many ideas for a project, but it is not possible to implement everything within a limited period. The team needs to identify the most important features and focus on building them first.

I have learned to think about the core functionality before adding additional features.

For example, if we are building an AI-based application, the first priority should be to make sure that the main AI functionality works and can be connected to a basic interface. Additional features can be added later if time is available.

This approach helps prevent a project from becoming too complicated.

Hackathons have also improved my ability to learn new technologies quickly.

Sometimes a project requires a framework, library, API, or cloud service that I have not used before. There may not be enough time to learn everything in depth before starting the implementation.

In such situations, I focus on understanding the parts that are directly required for the project.

I use documentation, examples, tutorials, and experimentation to learn the basics and then apply them.

This has made me more comfortable with unfamiliar technologies.

It has also taught me that I do not need to know everything before starting a project.

I can learn while building.

This is an important skill in computer science because technologies change frequently.

Hackathons have also given me experience working in teams.

A team may include people with different technical skills. Some may be stronger in frontend development, while others may have experience with backend development, AI, databases, or design.

Dividing responsibilities based on these strengths can help the team work more efficiently.

However, I have learned that team members still need to understand the overall project.

If I am responsible for the frontend, I need to understand how the backend works well enough to integrate the APIs.

If another member is working on an AI model, they need to understand how the model will be used by the application.

This shared understanding makes collaboration easier.

Communication is therefore an important part of hackathons.

Team members need to provide updates, discuss technical decisions, and communicate problems quickly.

If someone is stuck for several hours without informing the team, the entire project can be affected.

I have learned that it is better to communicate a problem early.

Another team member may already know the solution, or the team may be able to change the plan.

This can save valuable time.

Hackathons have also taught me how to work under pressure.

When the deadline is approaching, there may be several things that still need to be completed.

The team may need to fix bugs, integrate different components, prepare the presentation, and test the application.

It becomes important to decide what needs to be done immediately and what can be left for later.

This has improved my ability to prioritise tasks.

I have also learned that a working prototype is more important than a large number of unfinished features.

A project that demonstrates its main idea clearly is stronger than a project with many features that do not work properly.

This has changed the way I approach development outside hackathons as well.

Another useful part of technical competitions is receiving feedback.

During presentations, judges may ask questions about the problem, technology, implementation, limitations, or future possibilities.

These questions can reveal areas that the team had not considered.

For example, a judge may ask how the system would work with a larger number of users or what would happen if the AI model produced an incorrect result.

Such questions encourage us to think beyond the prototype.

I find this useful because it helps me understand the difference between demonstrating an idea and developing a complete product.

A prototype may show that something is possible.

A production system needs to consider reliability, scalability, security, usability, and maintenance.

Hackathons have given me an introduction to these considerations.

My participation in technical competitions has also helped me become more confident in presenting technical ideas.

A team may develop a good project, but it still needs to explain the project clearly.

The presentation should describe the problem, the proposed solution, the technology used, and the expected impact.

It should also demonstrate the working system.

This has helped me understand that technical knowledge needs to be combined with communication skills.

I have also learned to explain technical concepts in a simpler way.

Not everyone in the audience will have the same technical background.

A good presentation should therefore focus on the important idea rather than using technical terms unnecessarily.

This is a skill I want to improve further.

Another important lesson from hackathons is that ideas often change during development.

The original approach may not work as expected.

A particular technology may create problems.

The team may discover that a feature is not necessary.

The problem statement may turn out to require a different solution.

Instead of continuing with an approach simply because it was the original plan, I have learned to consider alternatives.

This flexibility is important when working with technology.

I believe that good engineers should be comfortable changing their approach when evidence shows that another method is better.

Hackathons have also taught me the importance of planning.

It may seem that starting development immediately is the fastest approach, especially when time is limited.

However, spending some time planning the architecture, features, and responsibilities can save time later.

A simple plan can help the team avoid duplicate work and integration problems.

I now try to think about the structure of a project before beginning implementation.

This has also improved my understanding of software architecture.

When building a project quickly, it is still important to keep the code organised enough to make changes later.

A poorly structured project can become difficult to debug as more features are added.

I want to continue improving my software engineering practices so that I can build projects efficiently without sacrificing maintainability.

Hackathons have also helped me understand the importance of time management.

A limited deadline means that every hour matters.

The team needs to divide time between development, testing, debugging, and presentation preparation.

Leaving the presentation or testing until the last few minutes can create unnecessary problems.

I have learned to keep some time available for testing and fixing unexpected issues.

This is especially important because a project that works on one computer may behave differently when deployed or demonstrated in another environment.

Testing is therefore an important part of the process.

I have also learned that technical problems are often unavoidable.

During a competition, there may be dependency issues, deployment problems, API errors, or unexpected behaviour.

The important thing is not to panic.

The problem needs to be isolated and investigated.

Sometimes a quick solution is available.

Sometimes the team needs to change its approach.

This has improved my debugging skills and my ability to remain focused when something goes wrong.

I believe these skills will be useful in my future career.

Software development and AI engineering involve many situations where things do not work as expected.

Being able to investigate problems calmly is important.
