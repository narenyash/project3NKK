One of the areas I find particularly interesting is computer vision.

Cameras and satellite images can provide information about changes in land, forests, water bodies, agricultural areas, and urban regions. Computer vision models can potentially analyse these images and identify patterns that may be difficult to monitor manually.

For example, image-based systems can be used to identify changes in vegetation or detect certain forms of land-use change.

I find this interesting because it connects my interest in AI with a practical environmental problem.

Learning about computer vision has also taught me that the quality of the data is extremely important.

Images taken from different satellites or cameras may have different resolutions, lighting conditions, and characteristics. A model trained using one type of image may not perform equally well on another type.

Therefore, an environmental AI system needs to be trained and tested using appropriate data.

This has helped me understand that building an AI solution is not only about choosing a model.

The data, problem definition, evaluation method, and real-world environment all matter.
