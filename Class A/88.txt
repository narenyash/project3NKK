
I am a student with a strong interest in computer science, artificial intelligence, and software development. The important part is understanding that data and using it to solve practical problems.

My interest in data science developed through my experience with machine learning. When I started learning about AI, I initially focused mainly on models and algorithms. I wanted to understand how a model could learn patterns and make predictions.

As I worked on different projects, I realised that the model is only one part of the process.

Before training a model, data needs to be collected.

The data needs to be cleaned.

Missing values may need to be handled.

Different types of information may need to be converted into suitable formats.

The data needs to be analysed.

Only after understanding the data can an appropriate model be selected.

This made me more interested in the complete data science process.

One of the first concepts I learned was data cleaning.

Real-world data is rarely perfect.

It can contain missing values, duplicate records, incorrect entries, inconsistent formats, and other problems.

If these issues are ignored, they can affect the results of an analysis or machine learning model.

I found this interesting because data cleaning may not appear as exciting as training an AI model, but it can have a major effect on the final result.

This taught me that good data is an important foundation for good technology.

Another area that interests me is exploratory data analysis.

Before building a model, it is useful to understand what the data contains.

Statistics and visualisations can reveal patterns, relationships, and unusual values.

For example, a dataset may contain a strong relationship between two variables.

There may also be groups of records that behave differently.

Visualising the information can make these patterns easier to understand.

I enjoy this process because it involves asking questions rather than immediately looking for a final answer.

A dataset can often provide information that is not obvious at first.

Another concept I find important is correlation.

Two variables may change together, but this does not necessarily mean that one causes the other.

Understanding this distinction is important when interpreting data.

For example, two trends may appear related simply because another factor influences both.

This has taught me to be careful when drawing conclusions from data.

I want to develop stronger statistical knowledge so that I can interpret data more accurately.

Statistics is an important part of data science.

Concepts such as probability, distributions, averages, variance, and sampling help us understand data.

Another important concept is model evaluation.

A machine learning model should be tested using data that was not used for training.

This helps determine whether the model can generalise to new examples.

Different problems require different evaluation metrics.

Accuracy may be useful for some classification problems.

Precision and recall may be more appropriate in others.

Regression problems may require different measures.

I want to understand how these choices affect the interpretation of a model.

Another issue I have learned about is overfitting.

A model can perform extremely well on training data while performing poorly on new data.

This means that the model may have learned details specific to the training examples rather than general patterns.

Techniques such as regularisation, cross-validation, and appropriate model selection can help.

Understanding overfitting has helped me become more careful when evaluating AI projects.

I no longer consider a high training score enough evidence that a model is useful.
