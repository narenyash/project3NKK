
I am a student who believes that education is not limited to what is taught in the classroom. Academic subjects provide an important foundation, but I have also learned a lot through online resources, personal projects, technical events, teamwork, and independent exploration. These experiences have helped me understand my interests better and have influenced the way I approach my education and future career.

As a computer science student, I have access to a large amount of information outside my academic syllabus. There are online courses, documentation, research papers, tutorials, coding platforms, developer communities, and technical discussions. I started using these resources because I wanted to understand topics that were not always covered in detail in my regular coursework.

One of the main areas I explored outside the classroom was programming. I started learning different programming concepts through online tutorials and practical examples. Initially, I focused on understanding basic programming and web development. As I became more comfortable, I started exploring frameworks, databases, APIs, and other areas of software development.

I found that independent learning was different from classroom learning. In a classroom, the syllabus and learning sequence are usually already defined. When learning independently, I have to decide what I want to learn, find suitable resources, and determine how to practise the concept.

This gave me more responsibility for my own learning.

I also realised that independent learning requires consistency. It is easy to start a new course or watch a tutorial, but understanding a subject properly requires practice. I usually try to apply what I learn by building a small project or experimenting with the concept. This helps me identify what I actually understand and what I need to study further.

This approach became particularly useful when I started exploring artificial intelligence.

AI was not an area I understood deeply when I first became interested in it. I started with basic concepts and gradually explored machine learning, computer vision, and AI-based applications. I used online resources to understand concepts such as datasets, model training, classification, evaluation, and neural networks.

As I worked on projects, I realised that AI involves both theory and experimentation. Reading about a model can explain how it works, but implementing it gives a different understanding. Sometimes the model does not perform as expected, and I need to investigate the reason.

This encouraged me to learn more about the mathematics and statistics behind machine learning.

I began to understand that using an AI library is only one part of the process. To build reliable systems, it is important to understand how the data is prepared, how models are evaluated, and what the limitations of the results are.

This is one of the areas I want to explore more deeply in higher education.

Apart from AI, I have also learned full-stack development outside the classroom. I have worked with technologies such as React, Node.js, Express, MongoDB, and SQL. Working with these technologies helped me understand how different parts of a software application connect.

When building a complete application, I need to think about the frontend, backend, database, authentication, APIs, error handling, and deployment. Learning these areas independently helped me understand how theoretical programming concepts are used in actual software systems.

I also enjoy learning by solving problems that come up during development.

For example, when an application does not behave as expected, I usually start by checking the error message, understanding which component is causing the issue, and testing possible solutions. Sometimes the answer can be found in documentation, while other times I need to experiment with different approaches.

These experiences have improved my ability to learn independently.

Another important part of my learning outside the classroom has been participating in hackathons and technical events. These events gave me opportunities to work on problems within a limited amount of time and collaborate with other students.

Hackathons are different from normal academic assignments because the problem is often open-ended. The team has to decide what solution to build and how to implement it. There may also be technologies that some team members have never used before.

This taught me to learn quickly.

If a project required a technology I had not worked with before, I had to understand the basics and start using it instead of waiting until I became an expert. This helped me become more comfortable with unfamiliar tools.

Hackathons also taught me the importance of teamwork.

A project can have several technical components, and different team members may take responsibility for different parts. Communication becomes important because everyone's work needs to fit together.

These experiences helped me understand that learning does not always happen individually. Sometimes I learn faster by discussing a problem with another person.

I have also learned from other students.

When working on a technical project, someone else may have a different approach to the same problem. Discussing these approaches can help me understand alternative ways of solving it.

This has made me more open to feedback.

I do not expect my first solution to always be the best one. If someone suggests a better approach, I want to understand why it works and consider whether I can improve my own solution.

This is an important part of continuous learning.

Another way I learn outside the classroom is by reading technical documentation. Documentation is often more useful than short tutorials when I need to understand how a technology actually works.

It also teaches me to solve problems independently.

Instead of looking for a complete solution every time, I can understand the available options and decide how to use them in my project.

I want to become increasingly comfortable with documentation because professional software development requires it.

I want to gain more experience in this area during college.
