One of the applications I find particularly interesting is crop disease detection.

Plant diseases can affect crop production and may spread quickly if they are not identified early. Farmers often rely on visual inspection to identify problems. Computer vision models can potentially analyse images of leaves or plants and identify patterns associated with certain diseases.

I find this application interesting because it connects AI with a practical problem.

However, I also understand that building such a system is not as simple as training a model on a collection of images.

Images can be taken in different lighting conditions.

Leaves may have different sizes and colours.

Backgrounds may be different.

The same disease may appear differently at different stages.

A model trained on controlled images may therefore perform poorly when used in a real field.

This taught me an important lesson about machine learning.

A model needs to be tested in the environment where it is actually expected to work.

This is one reason I want to learn more about data collection and model evaluation.

Another area that interests me is crop monitoring.

Large farms can cover significant areas, making it difficult to inspect every plant manually.

Computer vision models can potentially analyse these images and identify changes in crop growth or areas that may require attention.

This could help farmers focus their time and resources more effectively.

I find the combination of drones, remote sensing, and AI particularly interesting.

Agricultural fields can also be monitored using sensors.

My experience with machine learning has also taught me the importance of model evaluation.

If an agricultural model claims to identify diseases, it needs to be tested using data that represents real field conditions.

Metrics such as precision, recall, and accuracy may be useful, but the appropriate metric depends on the problem.

For example, missing a serious crop disease may have different consequences from incorrectly identifying a healthy plant as diseased.

Therefore, the evaluation should consider the real-world impact of different types of errors.

I want to understand this type of problem-specific evaluation better.
