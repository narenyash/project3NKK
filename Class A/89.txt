This made me interested in an important question: how can we meet increasing energy requirements while using resources responsibly?

Renewable energy is one part of the answer.

Sources such as solar, wind, hydroelectric, and other forms of renewable energy can produce electricity without relying on the continuous consumption of fossil fuels.

I became particularly interested in solar energy because it is accessible in many parts of the world and can be combined with modern technology.

Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity.

However, generating electricity is only one part of the energy problem.

The amount of solar energy available changes throughout the day.

It also depends on weather conditions.

Electricity demand can change at different times.

Therefore, energy systems need to manage the difference between generation and consumption.

This is where technology becomes particularly interesting to me.

I learned that an electricity grid needs to maintain a balance between the amount of electricity being generated and the amount being consumed.

If renewable generation is high but demand is low, there may be more electricity available than the system can use at that moment.

If generation is low and demand is high, additional sources may be required.

This creates a problem of planning and coordination.

I find this problem interesting because it can involve data science, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, optimisation, and software engineering.

One of the areas that interests me is energy forecasting.

Solar and wind generation can be affected by weather.

Solar generation depends on factors such as sunlight and cloud cover.

Wind generation depends on wind conditions.

Energy demand also changes depending on factors such as temperature, time of day, season, and human activity.

Machine learning models can potentially use historical and real-time data to predict future generation and demand.

I find forecasting interesting because it connects my interest in artificial intelligence with an important real-world problem.

A forecast is not a guarantee.

There will always be uncertainty.

However, a good forecast can help energy operators make better decisions.

For example, if a system expects high solar generation during the afternoon, it can plan how the electricity should be used or stored.

If lower renewable generation is expected later, storage or other generation resources can be prepared.

This shows how prediction can support planning.

Another area I find interesting is battery storage.

Renewable energy sources such as solar and wind do not always produce electricity when it is most needed.

Battery storage can help by storing electricity when generation is high and making it available later.

This can help reduce the mismatch between generation and demand.

However, batteries have limitations.

They have finite capacity.

They have charging and discharging limits.

They also lose some energy during the process.

Battery systems can also become less efficient over time.

These factors create optimisation problems.

An intelligent energy management system could consider electricity prices, predicted generation, demand, and battery state when deciding how to operate a storage system.

I find this type of problem particularly interesting because it combines several areas I enjoy studying.
