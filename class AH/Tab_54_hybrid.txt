My foray into the realm of visual content synthesis commenced during a summer internship at Adobe Research, where I worked alongside esteemed researchers Dr. Balaji Vasan Srinivasan and Dr. Stefano Petrangeli. Our mission was to create illustrative videos from procedural documents, a task that involved comprehending the textual content, retrieving relevant visuals (both images and videos), and meticulously stitching them together to form a coherent video narrative.

To optimize this process, we devised innovative methods for re-ranking visual selections and introduced a Viterbi-inspired optimization framework that intelligently selected the most suitable combination of visuals within each frame and across the entirety of the video. Our groundbreaking work was published in WACV 2023 [1] and was subsequently patented by the USPTO, reflecting its significant potential for transforming the field of visual content synthesis.

In my quest for deeper research experience and an exploration of various aspects of computer vision, I accepted a full-time Research Associate position at Adobe Research upon graduation. Leveraging the knowledge I acquired during my internship in image and video retrievals and re-ranking methodologies, I dedicated myself to creating deep-learning retrieval models capable of learning constraints during training, thereby obviating the need for post-retrieval re-ranking. This innovative endeavor led me to the complex challenge of composed image retrieval - a task that involves image retrieval based on multimodal inputs (text and image).

Existing approaches aimed at solving this problem primarily utilized global features for retrieval, which often resulted in erroneous localization of regions of interest. Recognizing the need for improved localization, we proposed a cutting-edge multi-modal gradient attention computation mechanism [2] to enable the model to learn local features and thereby enhance both localization and retrieval accuracy. Our groundbreaking methodology has been patented by the USPTO.

We observed a similar issue in vision-language models that use trainable prompts to improve generalizability - lack of flow of contextual local information from input images to the prompt vectors. To address this, we proposed Contextual Prompt Learning (CoPL) [3], which learns prompt weights dynamically and aligns the resulting prompt vectors with local image features. This work has been accepted at AAAI 2024 and is filed as a patent in the USPTO. This experience deepened my understanding of text-image alignment, enabling me to explore this concept in various vision tasks, beyond retrieval.

While retrieval is effective in creating content aligned with user intent, its effectiveness relies on the richness of the underlying corpus and the learned representations. A good creation should iterate between generation and retrieval to plug this shortcoming, which led to my subsequent explorations on image editing with multi-granular control. Building on the knowledge from my work on retrievals and stable diffusion models, we worked towards a training-free approach that involves manipulating and iterating in the latent space of pre-trained diffusion models to reduce noise accumulations and control the spatial extent of the edits. This work has been accepted in WACV 2024 [4].

In my quest for innovation, I unearthed the profound potential of harnessing intricate image editing principles, particularly those involving constraints and multiple granularities, to revolutionize graphic design creation and modification. This intricate process necessitates a harmonious optimization across three critical dimensions: content, layout, and color. My current focus is on instruction-driven graphic design editing, an exciting endeavor that employs cutting-edge large language models to decipher instructions and diffusion models to birth new design elements. Our tool of choice, FlexDM – a transformer-based model – meticulously predicts the optimal positions and colors for these novel creations. To ensure the updated designs maintain an aesthetically pleasing layout, I'm training the model with additional loss functions that strictly enforce layout constraints. This exploration promises to redefine the future of graphic design, merging technology and creativity in a way that enhances both.

My experiences and explorations in image generation sparked my interest in another realm of visual content synthesis – video generation. When I joined Adobe in 2022, a newly released feature called Moving Elements caught my eye. This feature enabled the creation of cinemagraphs from static images for fluid elements. Building on my learnings from image generation works and driven by my innate interest in this project, I am currently collaborating with Dr. Kuldeep Kulkarni to explore and animate other types of motions beyond fluids. After familiarizing myself with the current model, I found that most works focus on animating fluid elements by assuming a constant flow model. However, this approach falls short for motions involving larger displacements, like pendulums and swings. To tackle this, I am working on methodologies to formulate the motion model for periodic motions with large displacements. This project has solidified my continued interest in tackling exciting challenges in the area of visual content synthesis.
