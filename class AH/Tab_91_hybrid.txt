Immersion in the Labyrinthine World of Legal Documentation: The landscape of legal papers, brimming with terms and conditions, agreements, and more, is a familiar one. Yet, their intricate linguistic architecture and esoteric semantics often render them impenetrable to the uninitiated. Even for seasoned legal professionals, understanding these labyrinthine documents requires considerable time, patience, and intellectual rigor, as they navigate tasks such as risk analysis, contract review, answering legal queries, summarizing, and more. At Adobe Research, I seized a singular opportunity to engineer an NLP system capable of deciphering the nuances of multi-page contracts, discerning rights and obligations, matching them with relevant parties, and flagging potentially perilous provisions. Not only would this innovation significantly alleviate the arduous process of contract review, but it would also democratize legal knowledge by transforming complex, inaccessible documents into structured, comprehensive information. This project served as a catalyst, immersing me in various information extraction techniques, honing my ability to critically appraise the efficacy of solutions, and charting a course for my ongoing research journey.

Working on this project made me realize the importance of legal language models and how they can be used to power the components of a legal system. Traditionally, these language models were pre-trained over legal corpora using MLM/Auto-regression without explicitly utilizing domain-specific characteristics. However, in my opinion, leveraging the domain knowledge and semantics during pre-training may furnish a superior language model with better downstream performance. To validate this hypothesis, I proposed a novel legal language model framework that employed multi-task learning to understand different legal facets while pretraining. This approach yielded SoTA performance across several downstream tasks, which initiated plans to utilize it to power key features in Adobe Sign. While leading this research exploration, I understood the various stages of a project’s life cycle, learned how to make a sound inductive hypothesis, and sharpened the necessary soft skills to articulate my research ideas and collaborate productively. My research endeavors have led to filing of three patents in this domain. In the future, I would like to extend this approach to other domains, such as health care, finance, etc., to expedite the development of resources that make specialty domain literature more accessible.

Engaging with Efficiency in Long-Form Text: In parallel with my immersion in legal AI, I found myself drawn to the challenge of enhancing the reading experience for lengthy documents - texts that, despite their straightforward content, often prove time-consuming and tedious due to their sheer volume. A salient observation is that these documents offer a wealth of information spanning various topics, much of which may be irrelevant or unnecessary for individual users. To address this issue, I focused on creating technologies that deliver relevant content tailored to each user's specific interests, aiming to streamline and personalize the consumption process. In pursuit of this goal, I developed a novel supervised method for automatically extracting the underlying topical structure of a document's text. Traditional approaches treat this as a linear segmentation task; however, my innovation leverages a tree-structured conditional random field to recover the document's topical organization as a hierarchical network. This approach, grounded in linguistic theory and demonstrably superior to previous techniques for several linear segmentation datasets, represents the first instance I've encountered where such theory seamlessly integrates with solution methodology. This experience serves as a catalyst for my curiosity regarding the fascinating intersections between Natural Language Processing and other disciplines, where new computational models can be crafted for optimal efficiency and user satisfaction.

At Adobe, I leveraged this technology to develop the feature of topic-based summarization, personalizing input documents based on a user’s topical interests, and intelligent navigation experiences to enhance document consumption experiences. Currently, I am developing a text segmentation framework that involves an auxiliary objective to infer the aspect associated with the identified segments. This modeling technique would provide complementary information to improve segmentation and help unveil the topical themes in the document.

Research Journeys: My passion for democratizing knowledge predates these transformative experiences. At Adobe's esteemed research internship, I engineered a corpus-level system that forged semantic connections between documents within a user-uploaded directory, providing users with a comprehensive overview of their collection. This project sought to address the challenge of identifying version relationships between documents, thereby streamlining the process of locating the most current document version and tracing its evolutionary trajectory. In lieu of a labor-intensive, pairwise computation of version relationships, we employed Minhash-LSH to approximate version sets and devised an intricate post-processing algorithm to refine any potentially erroneous results. This project earned us the coveted runner-up position for the best paper at WISE 2021. To further eradicate barriers to understanding, I also delved into resolving the problem of determining prerequisite documents within the corpus during this development process. My innovative solution presented prerequisite relationships as an interactive dependency graph, offering illuminating insights such as optimal reading sequences for comprehension. These projects not only ignited my fascination with Natural Language Processing but also underscored its potential in fostering accessibility and reducing cognitive burden.
