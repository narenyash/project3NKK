Nestled within the cultural tapestry of Basque Country, I was raised bilingual in the rich, yet often overlooked language of Basque and Spanish. This dual linguistic immersion sparked an early awareness of the intricate challenges and boundless opportunities inherent in low-resource languages (LRLs). As these languages intersect my fervor for both linguistics and technology, they have become the compass guiding my academic pursuits.

During my undergraduate journey, I stumbled upon computational linguistics (CL) and natural language processing (NLP), realms that resonated deeply with my passions. My fascination with the field intensified as I delved into NLP seminars hosted by the University of the Basque Country, where I dedicated myself to a variety of downstream tasks. Through this immersive experience, I witnessed firsthand how the study of language could transcend its traditional boundaries and transform into a vibrant, interdisciplinary platform for tackling complex issues.

With a Ph.D., I aspire to dismantle the linguistic barriers that obstruct the technological representation of minority languages. By delving deeper into this interdisciplinary realm, I aim to uncover my full potential as a researcher and contribute to a more inclusive digital landscape where every language finds its voice.

A Multidisciplinary Approach. My initial interests revolved around semantics, pragmatics, and syntax. Yet, technology was always present, leading me to computational linguistics. As I observed the poor performance of language technologies for Basque, I was absorbed by the challenge of crafting methodologies that could uplift LRLs. I realized that developing an effective model training strategy for an isolated language such as Basque would be a personal victory and a new paradigm for many other LRLs. To do this, incorporating cognitive science can unveil more efficient solutions that increase the effectiveness of smaller datasets, a common limitation for LRLs. A Ph.D. integrating linguistics, CL, and cognitive science will help me acquire multidisciplinary skills that are critical for achieving my objectives.

My Research. Thanks to working on projects that involved LRLs, I learned valuable techniques to address my original goal: give those languages a voice. In my first study, I used language models’ (LM) code-switching reproduction to assess their low-resource multilingual capabilities using Basque-English as a benchmark [4]. I evaluated models focusing on output naturalness, code-switching aptitude, and hallucination frequency. This study, which I will present at EMNLP WiNLP 2023, showed that some LMs coherently mixed Basque and English syntax and morphology. They also code-switched with Spanish and French when asked to reproduce conversations between native Basque speakers, languages not mentioned in the prompts. This reflected their cross-lingual capabilities and knowledge about language location in the real world.

That sparked in me a fundamental curiosity about the hidden linguistic competencies of LMs. My discovery that models have accurate information on language in contact led me to work on how prompting can influence model responses. To do so, I compared the perplexity, ROGUE scores, length, lexical diversity (MTLD), human evaluations, and self-attention maps of role-playing and standard prompts [5]. My results showed that role-playing enhances the quality of generated text across several dimensions. With this project, (i) I got deep technical insights analyzing attention distributions of LMs and (ii) I reminded the importance of creativity to overcome dead-ends such as the ones faced by underrepresented languages.

My journey has steered me towards intriguing questions that delve into the intersection of Language Reference Libraries (LRLs) and Artificial Intelligence safety: what is the nexus between LRLs and prompting strategies? Preliminary findings suggest that integrating role-play prompts with diverse LRLs, such as Haitian Creole or Quechua, can potentially breach the confines of cutting-edge Language Models (LM), even to the extent of producing hate speech. Additionally, my investigation revealed a correlation between the level of representation and the success rates of attacks. With high hopes, I am preparing to submit this research for consideration at NAACL 2024.

In parallel to my academic path, I am working with Cohere for AI, a non-profit research lab led by Dr. Sara Hooker [3]. Here, I am the leader of the Basque group in a project aiming to build a state-of-the-art, open-source, multilingual dataset. At Cohere, I have had the opportunity to engage with NLP experts, sharpening my skills. This experience has enabled me to pursue my objective of LRL representativeness while expanding my network, allowing exchanges of ideas and collaboration opportunities. Furthermore, I have become adept at data curation and realized the importance of data quality. A fruit of my time at the lab has been the Visibility Project [6], a site I have founded designed to spotlight underrepresented languages by offering insights into their linguistic features and presence in NLP research.

Collaborating with native LRL speakers in my research has deepened my understanding of linguistic diversity and its importance. Each of my experiences has contributed to my maturation as a researcher and improved my understanding of the research process. Collectively, these projects represent my commitment to advancing the field of CL, particularly for languages marginalized in technology.

Aspiring Horizons and Significant Influence: My passion lies in exploring the intricate workings of language processing within the human brain, with a particular focus on developing efficient modeling strategies for underrepresented languages. Embracing the innovative potential of generative adversarial networks (GANs), I believe this technology can revolutionize our understanding and approach to language models, especially in resource-limited contexts. My academic journey leads me towards collaboration with esteemed researcher Dr. Gašper Beguš, whose groundbreaking work serves as a beacon for my aspirations. Together, we aim to delve deeper into the application of GAN architectures, assessing their viability and potential in low-resource language (LRL) contexts.

I am eager to explore the intriguing connection between predictive coding in speech perception [8] and the functioning of GANs. Utilizing an expanded version of the method proposed by Beguš and Zhao (2022) [2], I envision a comparative study that correlates Electroencephalography (EEG) patterns with convolutional neural network feature maps. This research could shed light on the degree to which GANs emulate human speech processing and offer insights into enhancing these models based on the brain's predictive coding approach, potentially paving the way for more effective language models in the future.

I am also interested in exploring how neural models can help us learn more about language. As a linguist, I have always been fascinated with Universal Grammar and the Poverty of the Stimulus (PoS) argument. With Dr. Terry Regier [7], I want to investigate how LMs learn as linguistic inductive biases are increased. This could offer new perspectives on the PoS argument and inform us whether some grammatical features are more readily learned. In this framework, I am excited about experimenting with artificial languages [9].

Nurturing a deep-rooted passion for unraveling the intricate tapestry where language, cognition, and computation intersect, I am eager to cultivate innovative projects at UC Berkeley that strive to advance the realm of technological justice. My research aspirations are a mosaic of interconnected pieces, each meticulously crafted to champion equitable linguistic representation within the burgeoning landscape of language technologies.

This multidisciplinary approach mirrors my academic goals and promises a comprehensive education, crucial for addressing the multifaceted challenges I aspire to conquer. Engaging in research across distinct subfields not only provides rigorous training but also allows me to investigate the far-reaching implications of my work across various disciplines, thereby positioning me for a thriving career in academia.

My work has amply prepared me for doctoral studies, not only in my research abilities but also in my excitement to contribute to UC Berkeley’s forward-thinking and innovative academic environment.

Grounded on my connection to low-resource languages, I aim to make a lasting contribution to computational linguistics, a field that has essential links to my lifelong goals and is strongly tied to my journey as a native Basque-Spanish bilingual.
