Neural networks lack the ability to generate uncertainty estimates, differentiate between epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, or incorporate prior beliefs or expert opinions unambiguously.

As society increasingly relies on AI to address more delicate and urgent issues, understanding causal structure and counterfactual modes of explanation becomes paramount. The stakes are high, and I am eager to contribute to a future where AI systems are not only effective but also responsible and equitable.

While I remain open to exploring this nexus via various paradigms and project areas, I offer below specific examples of research domains inspired by past scholarly works and practical experiences.

In the realm of deep learning, my fascination lies in probabilistic frameworks that hold immense potential for applications focused on social welfare. My inaugural research collaboration with Dr. Bansal saw us employing three neural architectures—CNNs, LSTMs, and CNN-RNNs—to expedite the early identification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) by examining patterns in transcripts of patients' speech. This groundbreaking work was presented at NAACL 2018 and was lauded by Sebastian Ruder as one of his Top Paper Picks. Our research not only achieved unprecedented independent classification benchmark accuracy but also conducted an interpretability analysis to discern distinctive language patterns exhibited by AD patients. However, our investigation revealed that many misclassified instances demanded a comprehensive understanding of diverse linguistic customs or a profound grasp of disease progression stages.

Bayesian deep models, such as deep Gaussian Processes, Variational Autoencoders, and Bayesian Neural Networks, are renowned for their ability to merge priors with the robustness and versatility of deep learning models. Nevertheless, these models typically rely on intricate or convoluted priors—like weight-space priors or function-space priors—which leaves it unclear as to how intricate language customs can be transformed into manageable probability distributions. My tenure at Meta developing and deploying a Bayesian causal inference package has underscored the fact that despite the numerous advantages of Bayesian models, they often face resistance due to consumers' lack of expertise in translating their subjective beliefs into model-compatible priors. This challenge is exacerbated within high-dimensional deep learning models. Even seasoned Bayesian deep learning practitioners often resort to isotropic Gaussian or similarly "uninformative" priors, which can result in prior misinterpretation and unintended consequences during inference.

My interest lies in exploring prior elicitation strategies to foster more principled methods for creating human-comprehensible priors within deep learning models for NLP tasks—particularly in domains where opinions are ambiguous or conflicting, such as medically-affected speech, or where data is scarce, such as endangered languages. I am hopeful that this research will lead to better-defined models and increased accessibility for interdisciplinary communities with domain knowledge, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of our collective efforts in utilizing deep learning for social good.

Causal deep learning. In later papers with Dr. Bansal, I explored application areas in social media, extending my previous work to the classification of domestic abuse stories in the Reddit Domestic Abuse dataset. This was presented to the Widening NLP (WiNLP) workshop at NAACL, accompanied by interpretability analysis. In the same vein, I later published a paper with Dr. Bansal which was presented orally at EMNLP 2018 and involved creating a novel task and dataset to automatically classify and analyze various forms of sexual harassment, based on stories shared on the online forum SafeCity. I used Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) and embedding visualizations and demonstrated how this helps extract powerful features that can help automatically fill out incident reports and identify unsafe areas in cities. In fact, this paper was included as reading material in Dan Jurafsky’s CS384 Ethical and Social Issues in NLP seminar!

Individually, these models achieved state-of-the-art for their given domains. However, beyond Reddit and SafeCity, there are many such forums for sexual harassment and abuse, and even more for related topics such as bullying. One could learn from each environment individually, but since they contain similar latent structures, relying on domain generalization could allow for even more platforms to efficiently benefit from the use of ML models. At Meta, my most recent research project involved building Bayesian priors for a sparse NN propensity score model which mapped the distribution of users who opted into tracking to the distribution of users who opted out, following the assumptions of a covariate shift setting. While that model used low-dimensional advertiser and user features, I am motivated to investigate how we can best use domain generalization and infer causal structures within the high-dimensional feature-space of text data.

While learning causal structure in neural networks can lead to increased robustness in unseen domains, spurious correlations in the data can lead to negative effects in downstream tasks. This misspecification of the causal structure can have especially harmful effects in sensitive applications like harassment and bullying. I’m interested in understanding how we can work to induce and incorporate causal-awareness in deep learning to take advantage of explainability and robustness, while also investigating how to best account for uncertainty based on the possibility of misspecification of the causal structure—especially by marrying causally-aware deep learning with Bayesian inference methods.
