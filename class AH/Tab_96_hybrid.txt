Statement of Purpose

In the realm of modern artificial intelligence, there has been a striking advancement in the ability to discern patterns and create computational representations from data across diverse mediums. Yet, the capacity for sophisticated reasoning and strategic planning remains rudimentary, confined largely to simple tabletop scenarios and simulated tasks. This limitation can be traced back to AI's inability to make inductive leaps from data and understand causal connections between concepts – a feat that humans achieve effortlessly. Inspired by these obstacles, my academic pursuits are centered around investigating the computational foundations required for AI systems to emulate such sophisticated cognitive abilities.

During my undergraduate research, in collaboration with my colleagues, I have worked on the problem to infer a manipulation program: a sequence of actions grounded in the scene, from natural language instruction. Human cognition adopts a hierarchical approach to this task. First, it uses the acquired understanding of visuo-spatial concepts to ground objects referred to by the instruction in the environment. Then, it produces tentative new locations for the objects based on the acquired understanding of the action semantics. Inspired by this, we hypothesize scene understanding and manipulation as the execution of a symbolic, hierarchical program, extracted from the language instruction. We build on the work of Mao et al [1]. My main contribution was towards formulating a generalized, hierarchical symbolic-program space for action manipulation, coding up the hierarchical parser for natural language instruction, and data generation using PyBullet. Our model demonstrates end-to-end learning and strong generalization to novel scene compositions and longer instructions. Related full paper [2] is under review at International Conference of Robotics and Automation, 2023 and appeared at the Workshop for Neuro Causal and Symbolic AI at NeurIPS 2022.

Navigating the intricate landscape of developing embodied agents is a daunting task, primarily due to the labyrinthine challenge of reconciling spatial and kinematic restrictions. This complex maze is the driving force behind my current research, which aims to enhance this architecture by incorporating an action representation that encapsulates these very intricacies. My innovative strategy involves learning action effects as a probability distribution within the task space, contingent upon scene constraints. To validate this approach, I've initiated experimental trials using a conditional Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to define the action space, yielding encouraging preliminary results. Garrett et al.'s [3] proposition of 'streams' as a versatile, black-box interface for integrating such properties sparked my interest. I discovered that my action representation can be effectively materialized as a stream, and planning heuristics, such as those proposed in [3], can be applied in layers on top of it. Such a system could pave the way for more profound reasoning capabilities, particularly for intricate objectives. I am eager to delve deeper into this methodology in the ensuing months.

Motion plans in real-world settings often fail due to extraneous disturbances. Along with two of my colleagues, I am looking at approaches to augment the above model with robustness to such failure modes of action execution. I proposed to learn action effect as the transformation of an object-centric scene graph representation of the environment parametrized by a GNN, as in [4]. Thereafter, errors in execution can be identified by learning to predict differences in environment state from the scene graph representation. Recovery from the failure state can be achieved by computing an adjusted action sequence to reach the expected final state from the current failure state using forward planning. Initial experiments have shown promising results in this direction.

Amidst my ongoing endeavors in the realm of embodied AI, I have ventured into uncharted territories, examining the potential of layering symbolic structures onto dense representations to construct more universally applicable models within machine learning. In a collaborative effort with esteemed professor Manik Verma, we are delving into graph-centric methodologies for enhancing the quality of label space representations in the complex domain of multi-label extreme classification.

Traditionally, approximate nearest neighbor search graphs serve as swift inference engines for extreme classification problems, being constructed upon pre-existing label embeddings. Our focus now lies in developing approaches to create conditionally-structured nearest-neighbor search graphs, tailored to the query-label distribution, thereby offering superior hard negatives for fine-tuning the language encoder.

In the pursuit of my doctoral studies, I am drawn towards collaborating with esteemed researchers such as Professors Anca Dragan, Pieter Abbeel, and Sergey Levine. My current research endeavors revolve around developing sophisticated AI systems capable of disentangling representations of both state and action spaces, and creatively combining them to further our understanding. Yet, this is merely the tip of the iceberg in our quest for AI systems that exhibit complex long-term reasoning abilities in real-world scenarios.

Intriguingly, I am fascinated by exploring the cognitive biases that drive intelligent human behavior and learning, and investigating methods to transpose these into inductive biases for learning algorithms. A second area of interest is engineering embodied AI agents capable of mastering novel concepts from scant data points obtained through real-world exploration. Lastly, I am intrigued by the potential of creating pre-trained models that can reason and plan ahead for extended periods, even when tackling unforeseen tasks.

These research questions serve as the foundation for my long-term aspirations, which I eagerly aim to delve deeper into during my PhD journey. Beyond these core topics, I am keen on expanding my expertise in causality, reinforcement learning, out-of-distribution generalization, and task/motion planning, as well as visual reasoning. Through collaboration with these renowned researchers, I am confident that I will be able to advance the frontiers of AI and contribute meaningful insights to the field.

In a developing country like India, the research ecosystem in AI is still in its infancy. My advisors are a source of inspiration for me as they guided me all through my work, shared invaluable insight about research in general, and ensured that resource constraints do not hinder progress as much as possible. I believe that being a part of academia gives an individual the opportunity to foster progress further away in the future through interactions with future researchers. In the future, I would love to be a part of this community.

In order to undertake these professional and personal endeavors, I seek a Ph.D. from UCB which will give me a solid technical background and the chance to learn from pioneers in their fields, all of which will be immensely valuable to my life-long career.
