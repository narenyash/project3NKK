John Muir once wrote, ‘In every walk with nature, one receives more than he seeks.’ Well, the first time I visited Yosemite National Park, all I could think about at six years old was the cold. Sure, the view was wider and deeper and brighter than any I had beheld through my binoculars, but, in the end, the waterfalls, the sea of pines, and the granite palace were beyond my enjoyment. Family trips like those made only a fleeting impact on me, an Asian girl with tunnel vision but not approaching Tunnel View. For the first fourteen years of my life, I was raised by tiger parents. Like the stereotypical Asian immigrant child, I was drilled to shoot for the moon and achieve nothing less than a landing. It’s hard to say whether I was stretched thin, but I rarely complained: I was made to excel in school, sports, music, and daughterhood. Driving to a place where all my achievements fell away felt oddly hypocritical. I questioned my parents’ motives for bringing me to a paradise just to admire it from my car seat. I couldn’t have cared less about the glacial history of Half Dome, the number of gallons of water spewed from Vernal Falls each year, and other meaningless information about which my parents pretended to care. Back then, we knew we didn’t belong in that crowd of nature lovers.

Nestled amidst the monolithic grandeur of Yosemite, my heart has always found solace and inspiration in the lush, vibrant landscapes of my parents' homeland, the Philippines. Even for a girl who spent her entire life in America, these visits have resonated with a depth that no American vista could ever match. Moments such as curling up with a book in my grandfather's ancestral home during a thunderstorm or tending to my great-grandmother's verdant tropical garden under the relentless sun have nourished my introverted soul.

One unforgettable morning during my trip in 2018, I rose at dawn, tip-toed through the peaceful stillness of our home, and found the dining table laden with steaming rice, sizzling bacon and eggs, and, as expected, ripe mangoes. In a whirlwind of emotion reminiscent of Miyazaki's "Spirited Away," I envisioned spirits presiding over the household, cooking, cleaning, whispering secrets behind closed doors. My mind drifted to my grandmother, who had woken before the rest of us each day to breathe life into our home - a home she no longer inhabits, having passed away three years earlier.

As the reality of her absence washed over me like a tidal wave, I was struck by an overwhelming sense of connection and purpose. 'Remember who you are and where you come from,' I heard my grandmother's voice echo in my mind. My grandmother, my parents, my indomitable ancestors separated by generations - they all dwell within me. If they could overcome adversity as doctors, lawyers, immigrants, women, what challenges can I not conquer?

Amidst the cusp of high school's final frontier, college, my parents made a decisive move. Eagerly anticipating our impending exodus from the familiar, they orchestrated an unprecedented series of family expeditions to national parks across the country. My father, driven by an insatiable thirst for adventure and wilderness knowledge, transformed into a seasoned explorer, leading us on treks that redefined our bond as a family.

Initially, I begrudgingly tagged along, my thoughts filled with images of monotonous car rides and the oppressive quietude of untamed wilderness. However, as the sun dipped behind Half Dome's majestic silhouette, my perspective began to shift. Perched precariously on a wooden plank nestled into the mountain's incline, midway through a punishing ascent, I found myself gazing down at my parents with awe and a sense of profound connection. Surrounded by an infinite sea of towering pines and rugged granite cliffs, I felt a fatigue that was both physical and spiritual, yet the serenity I experienced was unparalleled.

This arduous journey up Half Dome served as both a dismantling and a rebuilding process for me, stripping away layers of complacency and revealing my true grit and determination. I now view this achievement as one of many that have propelled me toward new horizons—moments where I've stood on the precipice of accomplishment, looking out at the vast expanse before me, ready to conquer whatever lay ahead.

Whether it's medicine, journalism, or music, this enlightening experience has underscored the indispensable value of my support systems. Embracing this newfound wisdom, I am now poised to ascend and overcome whatever challenges come my way—to explore, learn, and achieve with unwavering determination.
