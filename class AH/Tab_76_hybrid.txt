Though many areas in artificial intelligence interest me, for my doctoral studies, I am interested in gaining a thorough understanding of natural language processing and artificial intelligence research questions at the intersection of machine learning and public policy. After my doctoral studies, I intend to pursue a research career in academia or industry.

My first brush with research happened during my sophomore year at my undergraduate program (February, 2021) when I started working on a project focused on developing algorithms and deploying and distributing Deep Learning Models and layers on low computing edge devices with Dr. Amitava Mukherjee (Adjunct Professor, SUNY Polytechnic Institute, College of Nano Science and Engineering, Albany, NY) and Dr. Arnab Raha (Staff Research Scientist, Advanced Architecture Research, Intel AI). In this project, we proposed a greedy, graph based solution different from existing existing methods with comparable (in a few cases 3-5% better) performance. Working first hand on this project and witnessing different stages of research right from the very beginning of figuring out the problem statement to investigating viable solution paths and exploring related literature in parallel, inspired me to broaden my horizon by trying adjacent machine learning sub-fields.

Under the guidance of esteemed professors Prof. Mukherjee and Dr. Raha, I found myself immersed in an exhilarating opportunity: collaborating with Dr. Asif Ekbal from the Department of CSE at the Indian Institute of Technology Patna on a cutting-edge natural language processing (NLP) project. My task involved improving persona development and generating perception-sensitive dialogues using sophisticated NLP techniques, while integrating knowledge through Knowledge-Graphs. This experience expanded my understanding beyond Recurrent Neural Networks and Transformers, introducing me to intricate concepts such as Knowledge Graph, Graph Transformer, and Graph Neural Networks. My fascination with NLP was ignited, leading me to embark on another captivating NLP project with Prof. Utpal Garain from the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata. This project focused on recognizing hate speech and extracting hate phrases in my native language of Bengali, a resource-limited language, using the HASOC dataset.

Amidst my immersion in two captivating NLP projects, I seized the opportunity to forge a mentorship with esteemed Professor KhudaBukhsh at the Rochester Institute of Technology. My fascination with the practical application of Natural Language Processing was sparked and further fueled by Dr. Ashiqur Khudabukhsh's groundbreaking paper, "We Don't Speak the Same Language: Interpreting Polarization through Machine Translation." His pioneering research approach, infused with a commitment to AI for Social Good, left an indelible impression upon me.

Inspired by his work, I reached out and was granted the chance to collaborate with him on a groundbreaking project exploring the nascent field of web archeology. Dr. KhudaBukhsh entrusted me access to an exclusive dataset — comments from YouTube channels that were deleted following the Capitol riot. Utilizing a diverse set of advanced NLP techniques, we delved into these deleted comments and uncovered alarming discourses surrounding election fraud, vaccine misinformation, and calls for violence. Our findings are poised for submission to either SIGIR 2023 (Perspective Track) or IJCAI 2023 (AI for Good Track), alongside my esteemed colleagues, Adel Khorramrouz (Dr. KhudaBukhsh's MS student) and Sujan Dutta (his Ph.D. student).

I informally audited Dr. KhudaBukhsh and Prof. Mark Kamlet’s RIT-CMU course on machine learning on political data. This seminar-style course gave me an exciting opportunity to familiarize myself with seminal papers at the intersection of machine learning, political science, and natural language processing. It was also a thrilling experience to see my project partner Adel Khorramrouz present our preliminary findings to Prof. Kamlet, a noted political scientist and economist during the final class project presentation.

Embarking on a doctoral journey in Computing and Information Sciences at the Rochester Institute of Technology, under the guidance of Dr. KhudaBukhsh, presents an exceptional platform for cultivating my research aspirations. This program offers numerous compelling reasons that resonate deeply with me:

Firstly, I have had the privilege of working under Dr. KhudaBukhsh's mentorship, and our shared research interests create a harmonious synergy that I believe will propel my academic growth.

Secondly, I have collaborated extensively with his students—a dynamic, innovative team passionately dedicated to addressing AI for Good challenges. The collaborative spirit and supportive environment within Dr. KhudaBukhsh's research group make it an ideal haven for burgeoning researchers like myself.

Thirdly, I am well-versed in Dr. KhudaBukhsh's collaborations with esteemed Professor Christopher Homan. The complementary nature of their research programs intrigues me, and I eagerly anticipate the opportunity to engage in projects co-investigated by them.

Lastly, RIT's commitment to diversity and inclusion, coupled with its standing as a premier institution for computer science, promises a vibrant student community that will offer rich opportunities for learning and growth alongside fellow scholars. I am excited at the prospect of immersing myself in this thriving academic environment.
