Academic Explorations - Theoretical: My scholastic odyssey commenced during my sophomore year at university, as I embarked on an internship with Microsoft as a Data Science Intern. Collaborating with Microsoft Research in both Redmond and China, I initiated foundational work for the team by delving into underutilized clusters within Azure. These largely unused clusters were not only a drain on the company's resources but also a significant financial burden.

During my tenure, I engaged in data mining and constructed interpretable and transparent classifiers to decipher why these nodes remained underutilized - a problem akin to bin packing, where the objective is to optimally fill the clusters. The experience was enriching due to the independence and accountability afforded in my role, as well as the cooperative environment and supportive infrastructure provided. My project yielded substantial benefits for the corporation, saving over $200 million and positively impacting the wider community.

However, the experience also ignited some profound questions about my future research interests.

I subsequently joined the Reinforcement Learning and Artificial Intelligence lab and the Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute under professors Martha White and Adam White (DeepMind), where I worked on two projects. The first project looked at understanding the critical differences between Kernelizing Bayesian Linear Regression (BLR) versus using Gaussian Processes Regression (GPR). They generalize differently because GPR uses the kernel implicitly, and in BLR, we compute the kernel explicitly; hence we are trying to reason through the different representability powers of these two methods using functional analysis and understanding the RKHSs associated with them. One of the primary theorems we established was that there is no regularizer of a function in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space associated with the explicit kernel that yields a close approximation of the implicit kernel space’s function. I worked towards implementing the entire codebase, studying various theoretical properties and the paper’s write-up, which is in progress to be submitted to TMLR. In addition to learning effective habits of theoretical collaboration over many hours of discussion, I found my passion for ML theory through this project. The second project was the optimization of the MinAtar codebase (which is inspired by the Arcade Learning Environment but simplifies the games to make experimentation with the environments more accessible and efficient) and producing standard benchmarks comparing various algorithms, such as comparing Actor-Critic Algorithms, Deep - Q Networks using a new proposed hyperparameter tuning approach. I was the sole researcher on the project. I produced results where the training time of the algorithms decreased by almost 50% across all environments by including Just in Time compilation and increased the efficiency of algorithms and other PyTorch optimizations. The codebase has been released as open source and is now a testbed for researchers to test their RL and AI agents.

In my pursuit of delving deeper into Theoretical Machine Learning, I've recently joined Professor Anima Anandkumar's renowned lab at Caltech as a research intern, under the banner of Nvidia. Our current quest revolves around unraveling the enigma of Neural Networks' exceptional ability to generalize data, approached through the lens of Fourier space, encompassing an exploration of the intricate relationship between the Implicit Bias of gradient descent and the Spectral Bias displayed by Neural Networks.

Our focus lies on Fourier Neural Operators, a groundbreaking tool employed for solving Partial Differential Equations (PDEs), which have garnered widespread acclaim due to their discretization invariance. However, the selection of an optimal number of frequency modes and training resolutions for varying PDEs continues to pose a challenge. Insufficient frequency modes and low-resolution data hinder generalization, while an abundance of frequency modes and high-resolution data are computationally costly and promote overfitting.

Innovatively, we've proposed the Incremental Fourier Neural Operator, a novel approach that incrementally enhances both frequency modes and data resolution during training, an idea that was well-received at the NeurIPS 2022 AI for Science Workshop. As part of my role, I spearheaded all the experiments, honed the efficiency of these models, analyzed their results, delved into other theoretical aspects of FNO, and contributed significantly to our accepted work. Currently, we are extending our findings to encompass a broader range of PDE solvers and Deep Learning models, aiming to deepen our understanding of this complex relationship – a journey that remains ongoing.
