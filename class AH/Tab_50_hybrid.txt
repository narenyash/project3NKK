Unveiling the Intricacies of Low-Resource Languages: My Journey in Artificial Intelligence Research

My insatiable curiosity about bridging the gap between artificial intelligence and human cognition propelled me towards joining the CUET NLP Lab. This journey led me to delve into the challenging yet captivating realm of low-resource languages, with a particular focus on Bangla. My research endeavors were centered around building extensive corpora and designing innovative language frameworks aimed at driving advancements in this area.

My efforts culminated in a comparative analysis of 16 POS tagging models for Bangla, which resulted in a publication at ICO 2020. In my undergraduate thesis, I constructed a hierarchical classification system for Bangla news using an MLP-based framework. This endeavor yielded impressive accuracy rates (98.18% and 90.63% for primary and subcategories, respectively) and the creation of a dataset consisting of 76,343 news articles. Partial findings from this research were shared at ICO 2021.

Motivated by these findings, I expanded my dataset and immersed myself in exploring state-of-the-art algorithms. The results confirmed that ensembles of BERT models excel in performance within the context of low-resource languages. One of our key discoveries is that monolingual models outperform multilingual ones by adapting to the unique intricacies and complexities of a specific language, such as Bangla. Interestingly, we found that the optimal ensemble varies by task, implying that there is no one-size-fits-all model and that task-specific customization is essential for achieving outstanding results. Our work is currently under review for submission to an esteemed international journal.

Expanding Horizons: Venturing into Multimodal Learning. In 2020, driven by my interest in multimodal learning, I worked with one of my classmates on developing a sequential multimodal framework for real-time sign language detection for Bangla. We constructed a comprehensive dataset comprising 12.5k images spanning 49 classes and manually annotated the frames (images and videos). We utilized YOLOv4 to develop a real-time object detection model and a multimodal framework. I concentrated on two key aspects: firstly, the development of a comprehensive dataset, and secondly, the implementation and integration of language and speech components with the visual data. This involved transforming a series of images or video streams into coherent words and sentences. To enhance the clarity and structure of the generated sentences, we proposed three distinct signs to represent punctuation. The project, due to its novelty, ranked as one of the top 25 projects in AI for Bangla 1.0, a competition organized by the Bangladesh Computer Council.

In the realm where academia intertwines with industry, my tenure at Workera.ai provided a unique platform to delve into the integration of Language Learning Models (LLMs) within our assessment pipeline. This immersion underscored the critical necessity for language models that are not only robust and precise but also devoid of hallucinations, capable of crafting objective, analytical test questions devoid of creative chaos yet maintaining exactitude. The encounter with existing models revealed recurring patterns, an unfortunate lack of adherence to instructions, and a glaring imbalance between creativity and precision. This eye-opening experience not only broadened my comprehension of the intricate subtleties governing language model performance but also amplified my passion for developing adaptable, context-aware, and trustworthy language models that can deliver credible and faithful outputs.

Advancing Multimodal and Multilingual Understanding: My Journey with Vision Language Models. Being awarded the Fatima Al-Fihri Predoctoral Fellowship, I am collaborating with the UC Santa Barbara NLP Group. Inspired by the binding capabilities of text-to-image (T2I) models, my research focuses on multilingual concept coverage in T2I models. Our work has identified a crucial gap in existing evaluation methods, which often fail to capture the intricacies and potential inaccuracies yielding inconsistent or unfaithful scores and are thus questionable. To address this, we have introduced a metametric to evaluate existing evaluation metrics and a meta-benchmark, T2IScoreScore, containing natural and synthetic images generated using prompts with varying faithfulness. The work aims to develop a consistent and faithful metric for evaluating action and attribute binding in synthetic images.
