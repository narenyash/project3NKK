In the captivating realm of RuPaul's Drag Race, a world of sequins, silk, and showmanship, I was instantly enthralled by the allure of glittering heels, opulent garments, and a cascade of luxurious eyelashes gracing the legendary runway. For ten vibrant seasons, I indulged in fantasies of strutting triumphantly, clutching a crown and scepter, basking in the limelight. As I reflect on my ambitions, I am certain that I have devised a strategy to claim the coveted title. Though my acting prowess might not be polished enough to conquer the Snatch Game with ease, my unwavering cultural pride and political engagement promise to propel me towards the pinnacle of RuPaul's Drag Race royalty.

Amidst the dazzling spectacle of RuPaul's Drag Race, iconic performances often serve as tributes to the rich tapestry of drag culture. The crowning glory of Season 13, Symone, captivated audiences with her resplendent, floor-length durag on the main stage, a look that was not only technically breathtaking but also a testament to her deep appreciation for her African American heritage. Growing up in a vibrant, predominantly Latinx city, I have always taken immense pride in my Latino roots, yet I have often grappled with my identity as a Cuban-Honduran-American. My estrangement from my Cuban family has left me feeling disconnected from my Cuban culture, and although I reside in a city that largely celebrates Cuban traditions, I find myself somewhat detached from my Honduran heritage. However, rather than being confined by these labels, I am a captivating fusion of cultures, a mosaic of various influences that I plan to wield to claim the coveted title. On the runway, I would gracefully strut in an elegant bata cubana adorned with the vivid hues of the Honduran flag, symbolizing my dual heritage. In a lip-sync battle, I wouldn't shy away from showcasing my versatile dance skills, blending traditional Honduran punta with a dash of mambo and a dose of hip-hop. My cultural complexity would be evident in my performances, from a mesmerizing Gloria Estefan impression during the Snatch Game challenge to a masterful rendition of a song verse with an extravagant use of trilled Rs that would leave the judges awestruck.

Drag is inherently political. It's a rejection of societal norms and a celebration of queer culture. Our current political discourse is dominated by straight white men, so queer voices are vital to bringing about change. As a queer person of color, I have no choice but to be involved in politics. In Hialeah—or, as I like to call it, "mini Cuba"—politics are taboo, and young people like myself are often discouraged from getting a political education. Fortunately, I had the legacies of tenacious queer people like Marsha P. Johnson and Stormé DeLarverie to teach me what it takes to be a staunch advocate for civil rights. RuPaul once said, "Every time I bat my false eyelashes, it's a political statement." In the rare event that I would have to lipsync for my life, I'd turn any song into a political act. A performance to Beyonce's "Freedom" would become an evocative call for the liberation of people of color, while Christina Aguilera's "Fighter" becomes an uplifting message for the thousands of immigrants risking their lives to cross the U.S. border every year. On an unconventional materials challenge, I'd create a captivating gown out of fake money and wire to speak up against the privatization of prisons and the cycle of incarceration. My unique ability to turn anything into a political statement will carry me to the finish line on Drag Race.

In reality, I'm a long way from competing on Drag Race. I'm not the best at sewing, and my makeup still needs practice, but the show continues to inspire me to be outspoken about my culture, beliefs, and identity. For now, I will continue to slay lip syncs to Britney Spears' songs in my bedroom, and I hope that one day I can join the queen of queens on the judge's table of RuPaul's Drag Race.
