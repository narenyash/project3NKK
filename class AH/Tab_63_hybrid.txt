My doctoral objectives are twofold: First, to theorize how platform workers establish their reliable algorithmic knowledge systems; second, to develop publicly accessible algorithm intervention toolkits by building upon this proposed research. These toolkits are designed to enable a bottom-up, interventionist approach, empowering platform workers to face algorithmic systems more autonomously. My aspiration is to mitigate algorithmic discrimination, labor exploitation, and opacity within platform labor, with the goal of increasing inclusivity and well-being in platform work.

In the dynamic realm of platform work, algorithms serve as the invisible yet omnipresent regulators, governing service supply and demand, and shaping evaluation systems. For platform workers, these elusive algorithms are not absent from their lives; rather, they embark on a complex odyssey, akin to navigating through enigmatic phantoms within the digital platforms. This necessity to decipher these algorithmic specters necessitates a unique form of 'folk wisdom' that workers rely upon to enhance their work conditions.

Academia has delved into this subject from various angles, investigating how workers perceive algorithms (Bucher, 2016; Huang et al., 2022; Karizat et al., 2021), resist them (Bonini & Treré, 2024), and even manipulate them for their benefit (Cotter, 2018; Zhang et al., 2022). Shapiro (2017) posits that as workers grow more adept with platform technologies, they gradually assert autonomy, while traditional expertise wanes in significance.

An intriguing example is the evolving reliance of taxi drivers on digital navigation systems over their own street knowledge (Chen, 2017), underscoring the paramount importance of algorithmic understanding for maintaining autonomy at work. I argue that this area requires further profound theoretical examination.

In the realm of digital labor, there exists a fascinating and largely unexplored terrain: the genesis, propagation, and verification of the nuanced algorithmic wisdom that platform workers accumulate throughout their tenure. Unlike casual users, these workers engage in a continuous process of evaluating the credibility of their algorithmic understanding, for their employment status hinges directly upon the intricate web of algorithms governing the platforms they work on. As a result, effective strategies are assimilated into their daily routines, while those proven ineffective are discarded. This dynamic approach transcends mere comprehension of algorithms; it molds the very way workers engage with the platform, significantly impacting their job performance and shaping the contours of digital labor itself. My preference for the term "algorithmic wisdom" over "folk theories" or "algorithmic imagination" stems from this understanding that platform workers are not mere consumers but active agents who critically assess and manipulate these complex systems to survive and thrive within their digital workspaces.

In my current research, I analyze how dedicated fans decipher and influence social media algorithms, a topic I've presented at the Fan Studies Network North America (FSNNA) Annual conference. I interviewed 43 Chinese fan opinion leaders and discovered how key fans decode algorithms and persuade and teach ordinary fans to manipulate these algorithms. This behavior significantly disrupted the recommendation algorithm in China. In response to concerns about fan-driven algorithm manipulation, Weibo launched a separate entertainment trending section in December 2020. However, this measure had a minimal effect, which in turn demonstrates the significant impact and success of fan manipulation of the recommendation algorithm. I describe fans’ algorithmic work as an “empirical dance of understanding and manipulating algorithms,” since key fans must observe the real-time results and visibility of the algorithmic efforts by ordinary fans, verify the effectiveness of their strategies, and continuously refine them. This project showcases the full process in which a few users decode the algorithm, persuade a large number of other users to participate in algorithmic labor, and ultimately develop a significant algorithmic civil movement.

The reason I bring up this project is that it exemplifies how ordinary users disseminate reliable algorithmic knowledge, impart practical and potent algorithmic manipulation practices, foster collective action within a super-community, and intervene in recommendation algorithms. It shows significant potential to develop users’ algorithmic practices. In my capacity as a qualitative scholar, I have extensively researched platform workers' interactions with digital platforms. Several of my papers have been revised and resubmitted to esteemed journals including Feminist Criminology, Feminist Media Studies, and the Made in China journal. Additionally, my work on Chinese fandom and social platforms was featured in the December 2023 issue of Transformative Works and Cultures.

Connie Yuan from Cornell University on a comprehensive study investigating the intricate process of knowledge transfer within multinational corporations operating in Africa. This collaboration has expanded my comprehension of knowledge creation and dissemination dynamics within management science and beyond, enhancing my research in platform work and algorithmic knowledge studies.

My exploration into knowledge management delves into understanding how knowledge is crafted and exchanged between organizations and nations, elucidating the factors that foster or impede such exchanges. By analyzing these scholarly works, I aim to identify elements instrumental in shaping the diverse capacities of workers in acquiring, embracing, sharing, and employing algorithmic knowledge.

For instance, numerous gig economy platforms across various countries have roots outside their borders. The interplay between cross-cultural nuances and cultural similarity significantly impacts the transmission of knowledge and learning within these platforms (Chen et al., 2010). This factor offers insights into whether individuals can swiftly comprehend and adopt algorithmic labor regulations from foreign platforms, as well as their ability to assimilate and benefit from algorithmic wisdom shared across diverse cultural backgrounds.
