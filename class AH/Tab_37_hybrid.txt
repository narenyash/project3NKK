Tell us about something significant that you have done to improve yourself, in either your professional and/or personal endeavors.

In a resplendent sunrise of azure skies and golden rays, a few years ago, as I was en route to my daily grind, the soothing tones of Billy Collins, illustrious former Poet Laureate of the United States, filled the car, reciting his poem "Introduction to Poetry." Upon concluding, the world outside momentarily ceased, and I found myself parked roadside, captivated. The impulse was inescapable; it was then that I realized, this is why I pen poetry - to inadvertently delay others from their daily pursuits, a testament to the profound impact words can wield.

Much of my early life was spent battling a frustratingly severe stutter. A typical morning roll-call at school went like this: "...Roll number 21?" "Present!" "22?" "Present, miss!" "23?" "P...p...p..." "...24?"

Throughout my schooling, I was roll number 23. My speech impediment made everyday activities staggeringly difficult: answering a question in class was terror, having a conversation was inconceivable.

My journey to overcome stuttering began when I was introduced to poetry as part of the school curriculum. In poetry, I found the courage and inspiration that changed my life. P.K. Page's poem "This Heavy Craft" still takes me back to the early days of my struggle: "I, Icarus, though grounded / in my flesh / have one bright section in me / where a bird / night after starry night / while I'm asleep / unfolds its phantom wings / and practices." In Icarus' struggle, I saw my own.

Captivated by its melodic flow, I began to recite poetry aloud before the reflective surface of my mirror, each word echoing through the room as I meticulously molded my speech. The rhythmic cadence of verse not only compelled me to enunciate with clarity but also allowed me to pinpoint the letters that once seemed an insurmountable obstacle in my battle against stuttering. By keeping a list of trigger words, I was able to isolate and scrutinize the challenging sounds – the sibilant hiss of "s," the sharp bite of "p," the gentle murmur of "n," and the abrupt pop of "d." Through disciplined practice, I discovered that controlling my breath and overcoming my anxiety when confronted with these troublesome phonemes significantly diminished my instances of disfluency.

In the initial stages of my academic journey, I found solace in isolation, nestled within the safe confines of my own company. However, as I matured and my self-assurance blossomed, I recognized the necessity of stepping beyond this sanctuary and immersing myself fully in the classroom environment. The transition was initially daunting, often punctuated by moments of public embarrassment, but each small victory – a five-minute exchange within a compact study group, a recited stanza that echoed through a lecture hall filled with fifty expectant faces – served as a powerful catalyst, propelling me forward with renewed determination.

Having recited poetry for so long, composing it was the natural next step. Unwilling to self-isolate again, I pushed myself to engage local writing communities wherever I went. For the last eight years, I've managed an online poetry club I started at IIT-Bombay. In Austin, I founded the city's first poetry club, grew it from five to over 100 members in the one year I managed it, and organized monthly poetry-readings at coffee-shops and book-clubs. Today, the club is flourishing.

While reading poetry in public improved my speech, working constructively with other writers made my own poetry sparkle. In 2009, I won a National Poetry Award in India – a confirmation not only of my craft but also of my relentless pursuit of self-improvement and my resilience in the face of years of self-doubt.

In the pulsating heart of the auditorium, as I ascended the stage to recite my poem, an electrifying surge coursed through me, causing my hands to tremble with anticipation. The specter of derision or failure no longer loomed over me; instead, I was emboldened, ready to soar like Icarus with feathers unfurled. For a moment, the cacophony of 500 expectant eyes fell silent, transporting me back to my childhood sanctuary – the mirror in my bedroom. There, I stood once more as a vulnerable child, poised to embark on a journey of self-discovery and artistic expression. A deep, fortifying breath filled my lungs, and with determination in my heart, I began.
