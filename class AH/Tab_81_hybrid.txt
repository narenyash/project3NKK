**Systems Research:**
I got an opportunity to do research with Prof. Haryadi Gunawi from the University of Chicago to investigate the latency predictability of unpredictable flash storage. Researchers have been devoted to inventing “white-box” and “gray-box” solutions but can’t be simply deployed because it depends on the vendors’ willingness to adjust the device interface. There are also “black-box” approaches but must pay the cost of extra I/Os. Our group built a new technique: let the device be the device (black box) and do not remodel the file systems or applications, but learn the device pattern. Our approach (LinnOS) has the ability of learning and inferring per I/O speed with high accuracy and minimal overhead using a lightweight neural network.

Within the LinnOS project, I assumed a pivotal role, designing a sophisticated neural network model to forecast the incoming I/O speed for SSD applications. The task proved formidable; precision was paramount, as any miscalculation could potentially delay critical operations. Simultaneously, the model had to operate swiftly and economically, a feat achieved through meticulous input preprocessing, careful layer structuring, managing neuron complexity, and optimizing feature representation. Deeper models with an abundance of features can enhance accuracy, yet they also tend to slow down inference latency and incur significant costs unsuitable for application within the I/O layer.

Amidst an intricate dance of design revisions, I crafted a three-layered light neural network, meticulously selecting only vital input features. To my surprise, conventional wisdom regarding essential features such as block offsets, read/write flags, and lengthy write histories proved to be inconsequential in the grand scheme. Instead, the critical determinants were the latencies of a select few recently concluded I/O operations and the count of outstanding I/Os when those previous operations collided with the current, yet-to-be-deciphered I/O. This model, boasting a minimal inference cost of mere single-digit microseconds, outperformed the latest speculative tactics employed by leading tech giants in terms of both efficiency and accuracy.

My project, LinnOS, reduced average I/O latencies by an impressive 9.6% to 79.6%, maintaining a remarkable 87-97% accuracy and incurring a manageable 4-6 microsecond overhead for each I/O operation. Our work garnered recognition as a cutting-edge method, ultimately earning publication at OSDI 2020. The resounding success of this endeavor ignited my passion to delve deeper into the AI realm within Computer Science research.

**NLP Research:**
I continued my research journey with Prof. Derry Wijaya from Boston University. I participated in building a Web-based system, called OpenFraming, for analyzing and classifying news frames in text documents. The main goal was to make it accessible to researchers with or without computational backgrounds from a diverse group of disciplines. I greatly enjoyed building this system demonstration. For future projects, I plan to create online demonstrations for selected research outcomes so everyone can play around with them. This demo was published on EMNLP 2021.

Next, I worked on a multimodal project by utilizing BERT and ResNet to predict the news frames from a Gun Violence dataset. Our work is the first to conduct computational multimodal framing analysis. First, I trained and tested the ResNet-50 model utilizing only images from our dataset to predict the frames. The model's prediction was initially unfavorable since it only obtained 42.8% accuracy. Then, I extracted information from the images as background knowledge by using Google Visual API and combined the extracted features with fully connected layers from ResNet. The combined model outperformed the ResNet model by having an 87% accuracy on articles with relevant images. This indicates that adding background knowledge represented by Google Visual API tags contains useful information that is discriminative of the article’s frame. From this experiment, I figured out that Multimodal Learning is a crucial topic in order to build smarter AI agents. This work was accepted at EMNLP 2021. After having some research on different CS topics, I realized I prefer Systems to AI research.
