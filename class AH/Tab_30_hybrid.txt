Title: Unraveling the Mysteries of the Universe, One Refrigerator Experiment at a Time

In the annals of scientific discovery, few moments have ignited the imagination quite like the revelation of the elusive Higgs Boson. Yet, while I marveled at the monumental breakthrough that took place in the Large Hadron Collider, I couldn't help but wonder: what if we could bring such complexity into a realm as ordinary as my refrigerator?

And so began an extraordinary journey. Armed with nothing more than a high school education and an insatiable curiosity, I embarked on a quest to recreate the conditions necessary for the Higgs Boson's existence in my very own refrigerator. The challenge was daunting, yet the allure of unlocking the secrets of the universe propelled me forward.

The process was a whirlwind of experimentation and determination. I spent countless hours poring over scholarly articles, deciphering complex equations, and painstakingly gathering the necessary equipment. I transformed my refrigerator into a makeshift particle accelerator, meticulously calibrating the temperature to simulate the conditions within the Large Hadron Collider.

As days turned into weeks, and weeks into months, I found myself standing on the precipice of scientific history. The moment of truth finally arrived when I initiated my experiment. With bated breath, I monitored the data, my heart pounding with anticipation. The results were inconclusive, but the experience left an indelible mark on me.

While I may not have created a Higgs Boson in my refrigerator, I did discover something far more valuable: the limitless potential of curiosity and determination. This experience taught me that even in the face of seemingly insurmountable challenges, one should never shy away from exploring the unknown. It is this spirit of discovery that I aim to bring to the hallowed halls of academia, where I hope to continue pushing the boundaries of scientific knowledge and unraveling the mysteries of our universe.

The photograph in my hands stares blankly back at me. It was a picture of my friends and I all trying to paint something, with chaos everywhere and many upset or distracted faces. The striking monochrome backdrop soothed my sore eyes, focusing their attention to the center. In the middle of the masterpiece rests a splash of color - me smiling ear-to-ear extremely satisfied with my masterpiece. I muse. The picture was much more than just a magnificent edit. In many ways, it was a metaphor for the person I am. From the rural streets of Calcutta to Cupertino, from Cupertino to San Jose, from San Jose all the way back to Calcutta, my life's been quite a rollercoaster. I think it's safe to say that I've seen, as Dickens says, "the best of times" and the "worst of times". Admittedly, moving from San Jose to Calcutta was tough for an immature fifth grader, but I took life as it came. Each of those cities, together with their lifestyle, beliefs and people are responsible for painting my canvas, both in eloquent grayscale and a dash of bold color.

The grayscale. One definition of adapt is "to cooperate with change". How did I adapt to my erratic lifestyle? I got along with just about everybody. It was initially a superficial necessity, but in time it steadily sunk into what I am today. In the words of my kindergarten teacher, "Everybody's your friend". Simply put, I just don't believe in bad people. The world is just a massive conglomeration of different perspectives – what's good to some is bad to others. Everybody's essentially pure at heart and being aloof to them is quite inexcusable. Be it [name], whose English vocabulary is hardly five hundred words, or [name], who likely read 5 books yesterday, there is literally nobody I don't gel with. Modern society likes to segregate people into distinct stereotypes; it's like the caste system all over again. It fills me with a certain sense of pride to defy the social norms. If you stepped into the hustle and bustle of [my school's classroom], everybody has their own group huddle – very literally 'friend circles'. I'm so fortunate that I could barge into any of these huddles and be greeted with the same effusive welcome. Every single stereotype, be it the let's-solve-physics-problems-all-day group or the please-let-me-pass-this-class group, the what-party-are-you-going-to-tonight kids or even the I-don't-fit-in group, everybody suits me just as well. If you asked my friends, they'd account my affability to my ability to talk about practically anything in the world. I can suit up and discuss Stephen Hawking's theories and Beethoven's symphonies, sit in casuals arguing Michael Jackson or The Beatles or even lie around in boxers reminiscing Spongebob Squarepants and Pokemon. From banal Bollywood flicks to Alfred Hitchcock, from Stephen King to Charlotte Brontë, from Eminem to Miles Davis, from Audrey Hepburn to Christina Ricci, I truly appreciate them all. My eclectic perspectives dissolve the fine line between the cool and the uncool to an azeotropic mixture of different. To me, this different isn't distinctive. It's many shades of grey.

Nestled within the monochromatic landscape of Cupertino, a vibrant, singular shade emerged as my passionate devotion to the world of numbers. As a language-bereft kindergartener, new to this foreign land, I found solace and escape in the universal language of mathematics – a refuge that nurtured an enduring love affair. In the face of adversity, mathematics was my sanctuary, offering me a realm where I could freely express myself without the confines of words. By third grade, I had mastered algebra, and by fifth, I was tackling simultaneous equations with ease. The exhilaration of unraveling a complex conundrum is indescribable; it's as if Bruce Almighty himself were playing "I Got the Power" in the background.

My ninth-grade recognition as the State Topper in the McMillan Mathematics Competition marked a transformative moment, elevating my passion to competitive athleticism. Over the ensuing years, my skills sharpened and deepened, yet fate denied me an opportunity at the Olympiads. Despite its clichéd connotation, mathematics has reshaped my perspective on life. It's never been about the grades; it's about the transcendent experience of immersing myself for hours in a room, armed with only pen and paper, grappling with a problem, contemplating the infinite possibilities above me. To many, this may be the grayest of pursuits, but to me, mathematical problem-solving is the vivid hue that brings me joy and purpose. Ironically, numerophilia – a love for numbers – remains an unspoken word in our lexicon, a fascinating paradox indeed.

I love people. I blend in with them all, yet hold my own. I could dwell on my theories on piezoelectric flooring, but still be best friends with the boy who couldn't make sense of calculus. But numbers are what make me tick. I am the amiable mathematician. I am all the greys with that splatter of color. Oh, and I almost forgot about the Higgs boson I created in my refrigerator; that was just to grab your attention.
