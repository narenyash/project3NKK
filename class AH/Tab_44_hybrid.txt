Breathe in, breathe out. I felt my chest rising and falling, my heart beating steadily. Then her voice returned, “Clear your mind. Only listen to the sound of my voice as you sink deeper and deeper into a state of hypnosis. Your eyelids feel heavy and you feel warmth spreading throughout your body…” I concentrated. I could do this. To clear my mind I focused on my heart beat. Thump.…thump… I envisioned my heart pounding, pumping blood, spreading warmth; the oxygen-rich blood leaving my left atrium and entering my left ventricle to be pumped out via the aorta to the rest of my body. No. Now was not the time for technicalities. I needed to focus on clearing my mind again. I took some deep, cleansing breaths, but my eyelids fluttered open. I found myself staring at the serene face of my grandmother. My grandmother who was just like any other grandmother – who gave me baths in tomato juice when I had chicken pox, who taught me her secret recipe for spaghetti sauce, who, as cliché as it sounds, baked me cookies to cheer me up. But when is anything in life so simple? My grandmother was also the one who made her living as a professional psychic. I heard myself say, “I’m sorry grandma; maybe we can do a tarot card reading later?” She nodded and dejectedly exited the room.

In the twilight's fading hue, I watched her depart, burdened by the familiar weight of disappointment etched across her features. Once more, I had failed to succumb to her hypnotic spell. The room, now cloaked in the fragrant veil of incense, became a sanctuary for my introspection as I perused the well-thumbed volumes that adorned the bookcase: tomes on psychics, meditation, and chakras; treatises on diverse faiths such as Taoism, Buddhism, and Christianity; and works delving into alternative healing and herbs. These books, their covers weathered by my endless hours of study, whispered tales of a realm beyond the reach of science - a realm that ignited my curiosity like no other.

The spiritual world eluded my comprehension through empirical means, and my quest for understanding only intensified over time. Yet, I discovered an underlying motivation for my fascination with my grandmother's practice: to fathom her. Reaching out, I picked up a relatively new book on the shelf - one that promised to unveil the healing powers of gemstones. But could any of this be substantiated scientifically? I pondered as I gently placed it back on the shelf and instead embraced the solidity of my calculus book, its equations offering a balancing counterpoint to the enigmatic world I had just left behind. A sigh escaped me as I closed the book, the numbers providing a comforting anchor in a sea of uncertainty.

In my childhood, I embraced my grandmother's tales of mysticism and psychic powers with wide-eyed wonder, diligently recording my dreams for analysis and joining her in the garden for meditative sessions to seek tranquility. Her narratives of helping people through unseen forces captivated me, immersing me in a realm where the extraordinary was commonplace. However, as the years unfolded, the veil of my childhood innocence started to dissipate, and my mind became clouded with doubt. The fantastical stories I once believed wholeheartedly began to lose their luster, replaced by an insatiable curiosity for the scientific world. My requests for fairy books on my birthday were gradually swapped for microscopes, symbolizing a shift from the mystic arts to cellular structures and chemical reactions. The notion of psychic powers started to seem implausible in my rational mind, while religion began to present itself as less tangible, casting a lingering ambiguity over both. I found myself caught between two realms - science, which my intellect favored, and the spirituality that resonated with my heart, embodied by my beloved grandmother.

Science had taught me the power of observation and the importance of analyzing the past. My grandmother had taught me to look to the future. I fit somewhere in the middle – in the present. I picked up the book about gem stones and set it next to the calculus book on my lap. The pressure of the two large volumes equally weighed on me. I had the yin and yang of the intellectual world, the extremely objective and uttermost subjective of texts, balancing on my legs. What a paradigm. I recognized the close-mindedness of my attempts to separate these two driving forces in my life. I should be open-minded enough to transcend such conventional and secular boundaries, for it is through the subjective that we learn more about the objective, and vice versa. As a scientist I also know the importance of balance, and my grandmother had gifted me with the perfect counterweight to my logical nature. Suddenly I realized that analytical works and spiritual theologies, these two seemingly irreconcilable doctrines, could both have a place on my lap, in my mind, in my world.

Breathe in, breathe out. I calmed myself and set aside the books. I was going to spend time with my grandmother. I walked up to her and asked, “Grandma, can we make some cookies?”
