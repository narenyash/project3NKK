"...a Seattle teenager managed to compile coronavirus data faster than the agency itself..." said a mortified CDC staffer - The New York Times.

If you had told me a year ago that Dr. Fauci would be presenting me a Webby Award for coding one of the most significant websites of the year, I never would have believed you. In fact, I didn't even know who Dr. Fauci was.

I got my first computer when I was 6. Within a year, I could already type faster than my dad. By middle school, I coded my own video games and sold them online, driven by the way I could share my games with the entire world. I branched out from just games to being paid for creating mobile apps, websites, and computer software by early high school. As a 10th grader and the youngest competitor, I won $30,000 in a major hackathon where I developed software for the US government.

In the crisp winter of January 2020, an unsettling message from a distant friend in China jolted my tranquility. His city, he warned, was bracing for a lockdown due to a mysterious, virulent pathogen.

The world, as I knew it, seemed to shift subtly that very moment, a precursor to the seismic changes that would soon follow.

Nurtured in a household where science reigned supreme, as the offspring of a devoted physician and an accomplished biochemist, my curiosity was ignited at an early age by the intricate dance of microscopic molecules and their transformative potential. Engaging in spirited discussions with my parents, I discerned their apprehension about the uncharted territory that beckoned me, yet found no echoes of this fascination within the circles of my peers. My pursuit for knowledge led me to Chinese government websites, a veritable treasure trove, and outdated news articles peppered with ads - a far cry from the cutting-edge resources I sought.

In the throes of the escalating pandemic, I recognized an urgent need for accessible and timely health data, prompting me to design and develop a dynamic website. This innovative platform, engineered to extract real-time information from Chinese health departments, presented vital data in an intuitive, user-friendly dashboard. As the virus's reach expanded globally, my web scrapers followed suit, meticulously scouring health departments worldwide for pertinent updates. Initially conceived as a captivating side project, it soon evolved into a relentless vigil, necessitating constant code updates to maintain my site's veracity and relevance. The escalating demands of this endeavor eventually compelled me to prioritize, leading to my temporary departure from academic pursuits.

In the past year, while many have found solace in idleness amidst the global pandemic, my life has been a dynamic maelstrom. Far from the monotony of stagnation, I've navigated a storm of sleepless nights, international speaking engagements, and early-morning video conferences with distinguished entities such as the United Nations. This tumultuous journey has seen me standing on global stages, my voice echoing through virtual halls, advocating for change and fostering dialogue amidst the chaos of a world in transition.

Within a few months of launching the site, the virus had become a global pandemic, and I was on CNN (in my pajamas) talking to millions of people. My site now had more than 30 million visitors a day, mostly from people who had no idea it was being operated solely by a teenager from Seattle.

Reflecting upon the past, the relentless pressure and countless sleepless nights have proven invaluable, for they culminated in a personal transformation that has fortified my resolve and instilled in me an unwavering confidence. This metamorphosis, born from the realization of my capacity to influence positively as an individual, has propelled me towards even more ambitious undertakings. I am perpetually on the hunt for opportunities to shape creations that not only inspire but also serve humanity's needs. My thoughts are incessantly preoccupied with exploring ways cybernetics might one day enable paralyzed individuals to walk again, or how vertical farming could potentially mitigate the escalating climate crisis our planet currently faces.

In college, I want to develop the skills I need to turn these theoretical ideas into reality. I will be an active member of the university community and look forward to contributing and collaborating. Being surrounded by a diverse and interesting cast of people will teach me new ways of thinking and challenge me to more effective solutions. The future will be fascinating. Perhaps also terrifying. But it will be built by people like me and my cohort, exploring the art of the possible, and pushing past it.

Steve Jobs, my inspiration, said, "The people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world, are the ones who do." I know that my high school transcripts are not ideal, but I hope that my track record is crazy enough for your university to take a gamble on changing the world with me.
