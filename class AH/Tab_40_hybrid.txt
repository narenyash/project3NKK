Computer science graduate studies at Stanford would allow me to focus on my two passions: computer vision and entrepreneurship. My goal is to contribute to cutting-edge research in computer vision, enabling robots and computers to understand their surroundings. At the same time, I want to acquire the toolset to found my own venture after my graduate studies. To further these endeavors and to foster the cultural exchange between Germany and the US, the German Fulbright Commission has awarded me one of 75 Fulbright grants.

Nestled within the intellectual heartbeat of Munich, I am poised to complete my Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering at the prestigious Technical University of Munich (TUM), where I consistently rank among the top 5% of my class, comprising 258 diligent peers. Simultaneously, I am enriched by a parallel education in technology management at the Center for Digital Technology and Management (CDTM), an innovative partnership between TUM and MIT that selects only 25 students out of over 300 applicants from various disciplines each term to immerse themselves in projects spanning research, industry, and entrepreneurship. This dual education, forged at the intersection of engineering and business, promises a unique, well-rounded understanding necessary for navigating tomorrow's technological landscape.

During my exchange at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, I participated in courses and projects in robotics, bio-engineering, and multimedia informatics. I was introduced to the challenges of computer vision and was fascinated by their complexity and real-world impact. Back in Munich, I followed this interest in a project at the Fraunhofer Institute. Using OpenCV compiled for Fraunhofer’s “Intelligent Camera”, I developed the prototype of a computer-vision driven customer analytics tool that counted incoming customers and identified their paths through the store. This project confirmed my interest in computer vision and led to an academic focus on image and video processing, machine learning, and the theoretical foundations of computer vision.

At present, I am immersed in my bachelor's thesis at TUM's Computer Vision Group, under the guidance of esteemed Professor Daniel Cremers. My primary passion lies in the cutting-edge Large-Scale Direct Monocular Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (LSD-SLAM) algorithm, a groundbreaking tool that facilitates real-time odometry and SLAM from a single RGB video stream.

My current project involves the creation of an innovative algorithm designed to pinpoint the predominant plane within the SLAM point cloud. This is achieved through a two-step process: first, utilizing the robust Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC) algorithm to identify the most probable plane model; second, refining this model via Principal Component Analysis and optimizing it further with subsequent frames.

Looking ahead, my ambition is to expand this algorithm's capabilities to recognize multiple planes within a given scene. By doing so, I aim to facilitate simple object detection and scene segmentation, contributing significantly to the advancement of computer vision technology.

In the realm of computer vision, I am captivated by the synergistic processing of color and three-dimensional data – a field that I believe holds immense promise for overcoming current challenges. My fascination with this subject has been fueled by groundbreaking studies at Stanford, such as "Understanding the 3D Layout of a Cluttered Room From Multiple Images" (Bao S.Y., Furlan A., Fei-Fei L., & Savarese S., 2014) and "Convolutional-Recursive Deep Learning for 3D Object Classification" (Socher R., Huval B., Bath B., Manning C.D., & Ng A., 2012). In my graduate pursuits, I aspire to leverage my expertise in point-cloud processing, image and video analysis, and machine learning to explore how simultaneous depth and color data can be utilized for scene segmentation and object recognition. An area of particular interest is the employment of deep-learning neural networks on RGB-D datasets, a concept pioneered by Prof. Ng's research group. Courses like "Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition" and "Computer vision: from 3D reconstruction to recognition" would provide me with a solid theoretical foundation, while participating in ongoing projects at labs such as the Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and the Computational Vision and Geometry Laboratory would offer opportunities to contribute to state-of-the-art research.

Algorithms such as the LSD-SLAM will make 3D information available on mobile consumer devices in the near future - algorithms that can make use of this information are thus also relevant from a business perspective. Via programs such as StartX or the DFJ Entrepreneurial Leaders Fellowship, Stanford has given rise to hundreds of startups. My recent project with the Fraunhofer Institute strongly resembled the product development process in a startup; this and other projects at the CDTM have strengthened my wish to establish my own company. Participating in Stanford’s entrepreneurship programs would allow me to learn what makes a successful entrepreneur and equip me with the means to found my own computer-vision driven venture in the future.

During my studies at the CDTM and in Hong Kong as well as during my internship in a Chinese company, I thoroughly enjoyed the work with international students and co-workers. I learned that different cultures have different approaches to overcome challenges, and I believe that fostering intercultural collaboration can strongly impact a project’s success. Since my first term, I have been studying Chinese in addition to French and English, and I have recently begun Spanish. I would like to contribute my experience and language skills to furthering the intercultural collaboration of students at Stanford.

Pursuing a Master's Degree in Computer Science at Stanford presents an unparalleled opportunity for me to immerse myself in groundbreaking research within the dynamic field of computer vision. This prestigious education would not only propel me significantly closer to realizing my ambition of establishing my own innovative company, but also afford me the privilege of bridging cultural gaps and championing international collaboration between the United States and Europe.
