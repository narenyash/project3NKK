In college, I worked on distributed system design, and system optimizations for federated learning and streaming systems, while now, as a part of Adobe Research, I am currently working on ensuring the reliability of cloud microservices by analyzing system data, focusing on automatically localizing faults and predicting outages in large-scale microservice deployments. Explaining the root cause of a fault is challenging because of the dynamic inter-module correlation. A growing body of work aims to detect causal relationships among multiple performance metrics of the entire microservice to recover the architecture of the underlying system and then use various graph-based algorithms to locate the root cause. However, prior approaches for discovering these causal dependencies between the performance metrics either overlook the deployment strategy of microservices, which spawns multiple instances/pods for reliability and load-balancing purposes, or average the metric data monitored for each such instance, resulting in a loss of information. We address this gap through "CausIL" [1], which utilizes (i) metrics variations for each instance deployed per service by considering these instances as conditionally independent of other instances, and (ii) domain knowledge generic to any microservice system to identify the causal relationships. An added advantage is its capability to cope with instances’ distinct and transitional nature, usually observed in a microservice deployment due to autoscaler configuration. The above two properties improve "CausIL" over prior works in identifying the causal structure of the system.

In stark contrast, while our innovative algorithm, CausIL, demonstrates superior performance compared to previous works, it encounters potential hurdles due to the absence of domain-specific knowledge. Traditional causal structure detection algorithms also struggle with scalability and rely on parametric assumptions. To tackle these challenges, I collaborated with Professors Saurabh Bagchi and Murat Kocaoglu from Purdue University, proposing a novel hierarchical and localized causal algorithm for identifying root causes of system failures without relying on domain knowledge. Our groundbreaking work was accepted at NeurIPS 2022 [2]. The core concept was to view faults as interventions within the system, thereby harnessing causal methodologies to pinpoint intervention targets and ultimately uncover root causes by incorporating an auxiliary node in the causal graph. Our solution offers scalability, as it doesn't require comprehensive causal identification of the entire system architecture for predicting intervention targets.

My research interest in improving system performance predates Adobe Research. My undergraduate thesis at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Kharagpur, advised by Prof. Sandip Chakraborty, focused on building an adaptive 360-degree video streaming system to reduce the bandwidth consumption for streaming. We observed that streaming the entire frame of a 360-degree video wastes an unnecessary amount of bandwidth since the user’s gaze (viewport) is confined to some specific regions. This observation led us to design "PARIMA" [3], a novel low-latency intelligent streaming system that predicts viewports in advance via online regression on the viewer’s localized region of interest and video characteristics. In addition, we allocated higher bandwidth to only the predicted viewport regions without compromising the user’s QoE. The work led to my first publication at WWW 2021, which boosted my confidence and enthused me to pursue further research on designing intelligent systems to solve real issues.

My master's thesis, under the guidance of Prof. Sandip Chakraborty, centered around the innovative application of smart contracts to facilitate Federated Learning (FL), a development that deepened my passion for system design. Our groundbreaking project sought to optimize machine learning model training across data silos using federated networks and blockchain interoperability. This undertaking was driven by the vision that cross-silo federated networks could empower enterprises to collaboratively train superior models without compromising sensitive data.

Our solution, published in ICBC 2022 [4], introduced an indigenous relay mechanism within individual federated networks, facilitating secure model exchange after rigorous authentication checks. The heart of our system was a scalable and flexible federated architecture that catered to heterogeneous clients, with Apache Kafka serving as the robust communication network between the server and clients.

Designing this end-to-end system not only yielded a published full paper but also ignited in me an unwavering commitment to delve deeper into optimized system design research.
