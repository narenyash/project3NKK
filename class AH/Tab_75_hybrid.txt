I want to pursue a Ph.D. in Computer Science at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill to conduct research in label-efficient deep learning methods for computer vision problems. While semi/self-supervised learning paradigms have shown great promise at classification tasks, their application to other vision tasks has been limited, especially where acquiring ground truths is arduous and demands expertise, such as video understanding, action recognition and multi-modal learning. This forms my primary research motivation, following my background in learning with less supervision.

I am pursuing a Bachelor’s degree program (B.E.) majoring in Information Technology from Jadavpur University, a premier educational institution in India. I am indebted to the same for providing me with a foundation that imbibes a positive attitude and work ethics to take on research statements. Excelling in courses such as Data Structures, Algorithms and Object-Oriented Programming cemented my Computer Science fundamentals. Additionally, I have taken online courses offered by various universities on machine learning, image processing and computer vision, which helped me to develop a fundamental understanding of these topics, providing with the base to conduct research.

In my sophomore year, under the tutelage of Professors Ram Sarkar and Pawan Kumar Singh, I embarked on a research odyssey exploring deep learning and evolutionary optimization, with a particular focus on medical imaging – specifically lung X-rays for pneumonia detection. My project involved devising an innovative local search-embedded hybrid meta-heuristic scheme to optimize deep features extracted from these images. This experience served as a profound catalyst, illuminating the transformative potential of deep learning in addressing real-world issues. Our groundbreaking findings were published in the esteemed International Journal of Intelligent Systems by Wiley (IF: 8.993), marking a significant milestone in my academic journey and fueling my unwavering passion for research in this field.

Over the scorching summer of 2021, I immersed myself as a research assistant within SketchX Lab at the University of Surrey, under the astute guidance of Prof. Yi-Zhe Song. My assignment was an intriguing endeavor focused on fine-grained sketch-based image retrieval, a vision project that pushed me to grapple with the laborious demands of annotation – each image required a corresponding, painstakingly detailed hand-drawn sketch as a partner. This exploration exposed the vast annotation requirements of supervised models as a major obstacle in contemporary deep learning, a fact that had previously eluded my understanding.

Fueled by this revelation, I embarked on a research journey delving into learning paradigms with limited labels, specifically investigating semi/self-supervision and weak supervision methodologies. This quest led me to plunge headfirst into the realm of classical self-supervised algorithms and their role in enabling automatic representation learning from unlabeled data.

Initially, I was captivated by the potential of self-supervision in bolstering representation learning for supervised models. In tandem with Prof. Singh, we engineered a hybrid framework incorporating an image inpainting module alongside a traditional supervised pipeline for a histopathological screening task. Our innovative model surpassed state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods by considerable margins, and through rigorous ablation studies, we convincingly demonstrated the performance enhancement resulting from the additional self-supervision signal.

Our groundbreaking work was eventually accepted for publication at the prestigious International Conference on Machine Intelligence and Signal Processing (MISP '22), earning us the coveted "Best Paper Award" (Track: Deep Learning) in the event.

Commencing Winter 2021, I embarked upon a research internship at the CVPR Unit of the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, under the guidance of Professors Umapada Pal and Saumik Bhattacharya from ISI Kolkata and IIT Kharagpur respectively. My mission was to create self-supervised models for writer-independent offline signature verification, a task that drew parallels with my prior experience in working with sparse sketches, given the fine-grained nature of handwritten signatures. This led us to concentrate on local, region-specific feature learning.

In my inaugural project, we employed a 2D patch-wise attention mechanism for the reconstruction of signature images from their individual patches, using this as a self-supervision pretext task prior to fine-tuning a pre-trained model with a dual metric learning objective. Our work was subsequently published at the prestigious International Conference on Pattern Recognition (ICPR '22). In another project, we developed a non-contrastive self-supervised framework that learned decorrelated stroke representations of signature images by maximizing information among patch-level features. This work was accepted as an oral presentation at the esteemed International Conference on Image Processing (ICIP '22).

Throughout the course of my engaging projects, I've gleaned invaluable insights that have significantly shaped my academic journey. Initially, I immersed myself in a thorough exploration and evaluation of existing research, particularly focusing on self-supervised learning. This process enabled me to devise innovative strategies for addressing the stated challenges.

Moreover, these projects honed my ability to strategically design experiments that are not only feasible but also accommodate tight deadlines – a crucial skill when dealing with conference submissions, which often have shorter and more stringent timelines than journal publications.

However, what truly energized me was the hands-on experience I gained in self-supervision. This direct exposure sparked an unparalleled motivation within me to develop efficient annotation frameworks. My enthusiasm for this field has grown exponentially, compelling me to delve deeper into its theoretical and practical dimensions.

Currently, our collaboration continues as we work on two distinct projects. First, we're developing a similarity weighting mechanism using temperature variation in contrastive learning for class imbalanced datasets. Second, we're creating an unsupervised image decomposition framework that encompasses various types of image restoration (such as denoising and dehazing). Our goal is to present these groundbreaking findings at an upcoming prestigious conference.

In Summer ‘22, I was selected as a research intern as a part of the Mitacs Globalink Programme. I worked with Prof. Jose Dolz at Ecole de technologie superieure(ETS), Montreal, on unpaired multi-modal medical image segmentation. Our project aimed at circumventing the registration step for multi-modal learning from CT and MR volumes and improving segmentation performance in each modality by fusing cross-modal information from the unpaired medical scan volumes. This posed an opportunity for me to extend my medical imaging experience to a more practical and challenging setup involving raw medical volumes instead of carefully curated 2D slices. It took me a while to get acquainted with handling 3D medical scans, especially their pre-processing, sampling and visualization. Following the paper “Unpaired Multi-modal Segmentation via Knowledge Distillation”, we implemented a baseline framework comprising modality-specific normalization layers and knowledge distillation for cross-modal information fusion. The 3-month experience at Montreal was a delightful one, as it not only led to insightful discussions with several senior PhD students and their doctoral journeys but also gave me a taste of how research gets conducted at different labs across the world. Furthermore, it instilled in me the confidence to be able to collaborate with international students in a diverse lab environment – something very much required to succeed in a Ph.D. program.

Currently, Prof. Jose and I are working on extending the above project to a semi-supervised setting via an uncertainty-based approach. Ours would be one of the first to tackle unpaired multi-modal segmentation with limited annotations. We aim to publish this project soon to an upcoming venue.
