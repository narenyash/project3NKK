Tell us about something significant that you have done to improve yourself, in either your professional and/or personal endeavors.

Under the radiant embrace of a golden sunrise, several years past, as I traversed the familiar route to my workplace, the soothing cadence of Billy Collins, esteemed former Poet Laureate of the United States, filled the car with his captivating recitation of "Introduction to Poetry." Upon the poem's conclusion, I found myself compelled to halt my journey, drawing to a stop on the curbside. The compelling allure of the verse left me no choice but to pause and absorb its resonance. That moment encapsulated the essence of why I write poetry: to evoke in others the same inexplicable delay that I experienced, transforming them, if only for a fleeting instant, into fellow travelers on life's poetic journey.

Much of my early life was spent battling a frustratingly severe stutter. A typical morning roll-call at school went like this: "...Roll number 21?" "Present!" "22?" "Present, miss!" "23?" "P...p...p..." "...24?"

Throughout my schooling, I was roll number 23. My speech impediment made everyday activities staggeringly difficult: answering a question in class was terror, having a conversation was inconceivable.

My journey to overcome stuttering began when I was introduced to poetry as part of the school curriculum. In poetry, I found the courage and inspiration that changed my life. P.K. Page's poem "This Heavy Craft" still takes me back to the early days of my struggle: "I, Icarus, though grounded / in my flesh / have one bright section in me / where a bird / night after starry night / while I'm asleep / unfolds its phantom wings / and practices." In Icarus' struggle, I saw my own.

Engulfed by the fluid cadence of verse, I found solace in reciting poetry to my mirror-bound reflection. The measured, rhythmic flow of poetic language compelled me to articulate each word precisely, helping me decipher the letters that once eluded me due to my stutter. I compiled a list of trigger words, meticulously dissecting the troublesome sounds that plagued me - the labyrinthine "p," the thundering "d," the elusive "n" and the whispering "s." Through diligent practice, I discovered that regulating my breath and quelling my anxiety when confronted by these challenging words significantly diminished my instances of disfluency.

As my confidence grew, I forced myself to break out of my solitary comfort-zone and participate in class. It was humiliating at first, often publicly, but even my little successes – a five-minute conversation in a small group, a stanza recited to a classroom of 50 – kept me going.

Having recited poetry for so long, composing it was the natural next step. Unwilling to self-isolate again, I pushed myself to engage local writing communities wherever I went. For the last eight years, I've managed an online poetry club I started at IIT-Bombay. In Austin, I founded the city's first poetry club, grew it from five to over 100 members in the one year I managed it, and organized monthly poetry-readings at coffee-shops and book-clubs. Today, the club is flourishing.

In the pulsating heart of literary gatherings, reciting poetry aloud ignited a transformative metamorphosis within me, polishing my oratory skills to an iridescent sheen. Yet, it was the collaborative endeavors with fellow wordsmiths that truly kindled the dormant embers of my creativity, illuminating my own verse with a radiant luminosity. The year 2009 marked a significant milestone in this journey when I emerged victorious in India's esteemed National Poetry Competition. This triumph served not only as a testament to my honed abilities but also as a beacon of resilience, having weathered decades-long storms of self-doubt and emerging stronger than ever before.

Stepping onto the stage, bathed in spotlights, I felt an electrifying surge coursing through me as I prepared to recite my poem. The anxiety that once gripped me – fear of mockery or falling short – had dissipated, leaving only a renewed sense of confidence and determination. As the crowd of 500 fell silent, I was transported back to my childhood sanctuary, standing before the mirror in my bedroom. In that moment, I saw myself transformed into Icarus, plumes of imaginary feathers spreading behind me as I prepared for a daring flight. With a deep, steadying breath, I began, my voice resonating through the hushed auditorium, carrying with it the echoes of a story yet to be told.
