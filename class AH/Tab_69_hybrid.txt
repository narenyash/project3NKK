In the crucible of my third year at IIT Delhi, my odyssey into machine learning research commenced under the tutelage of esteemed scholars Dr. Mausam and Dr. Parag Singla, with a project centered around Generalized Planning. Our mission: to extract valuable insights from minuscule training datasets of Relational Markov Decision Processes (RMDPs) within specific domains, and extrapolate these findings to larger, more complex instances.

An RMDP is a sophisticated first-order depiction of a versatile family of Markov Decision Processes, each characterized by a varying number of objects yet sharing a common transition and reward function. Our primary objective was the development of a universal policy capable of delivering optimal rewards across all iterations of the RMDP.

The SymNet architecture, a precursor innovation, employed a Graph Neural Network (GNN) to cultivate a policy on an instance graph—a graphical representation of an MDP itself. This design allowed for the policy to be agnostic to the size of the problem at hand, as it was defined by parameters within the GNN.

When I began working on SymNet, I noticed that training using Reinforcement Learning was unstable which made it hard to make any improvements to the underlying architecture. Pivoting to a more stable Imitation Learning approach unearthed a treasure trove of tractable problems. Representational inadequacy of the SymNet graph formulation was clear from our experiments. We decided to fix these by creating a new method of converting a relational MDP to a graph which alleviated the lack of representability in SymNet, and we came with the second version. Our experiments surprisingly revealed that SymNet2.0, unlike SymNet was able to beat its teacher on larger instances. This work got published in UAI 2022 [Sharma et al., 2022].

However, SymNet2.0’s architecture was severely limited by the depth of the GNN used for learning. However, there existed a large class of problems which required “long-range dependencies”. The notion of distance in a relational MDP wasn’t well defined at that time. Coming up with a useful definition using Dynamic Bayesian Networks, we decided to implement a novel GNN architecture called SymNet3.0 which could incorporate these distances using intermediate GNN layers having all-pair attention using distances as edge features. In addition to our empirically grounded work, I was able to get theoretical guarantees on SymNet3.0 which added to its utility. This work got published at UAI 2023, [Sharma et al., 2023] with me as a co-first author.

Research in Reasoning and Planning using LLMs: After my stint with Generalized planning, I decided to diversify. The field of “generative AI” was booming with GPT-3.5 coming out to the general public, and its inability to plan piqued my interest. I collaborated with Dr. Subbarao Kambhampati from ASU on this subject. I was curious if LLMs could even simulate a PDDL domain, let alone plan. My intuition was that maybe LLMs can plan if fine-tuned, unlike previous works which had tried prompting LLMs to generate plans. My experience with a small LM (GPT-2) revealed that even with fine-tuning, LMs hallucinate and generate invalid actions. How could we improve plans without resorting to external feedback? This was a question which bothered me and for this, I came up with a simple solution of learning verifiers from the same fine-tuning data on which the plan generation is trained by sampling negative data by choosing random actions. Surprisingly, this simple idea worked very well in practice, and we published a workshop paper [Arora and Kambhampati, 2023] in the ICML Workshop on Knowledge and Logical Reasoning in the Era of Data-driven Learning.

Simultaneously, my curiosity extended beyond overt problem-solving techniques to the more covert skills inherent in mathematics and engineering. The JEE Advanced examination presented itself as an ideal platform to assess the capabilities of GPT-4 in this realm. To our delight, initial test results indicated that GPT-4's performance was far from trivial. Seizing this opportunity, I proposed this research project to Dr. Mausam, curating a dataset named JEEBench, comprising questions from the past eight years of JEE examination papers. We then evaluated various language models on this dataset. I was convinced that the dataset itself would be valuable, but the insights gleaned from it would prove invaluable to the research community.

Extensive human evaluation efforts were undertaken to mine these actionable insights, both qualitative and quantitative. We developed a straightforward method to optimize language models under the uncertainty posed by thresholding on confidence levels derived from self-consistent responses. In our work, we may have been the first to disprove the notion that language models can autonomously improve, a finding corroborated by numerous subsequent studies. Our research was accepted at EMNLP 2023 [Arora et al., 2023a] and received commendable reviews from experts in the field, with coverage in national newspapers such as Hindustan Times.

Research in Information Retrieval: At Microsoft Research, I’m currently working on an experimental approach of utilizing feedback from a retrieval system’s result to improve retrieval quality. In our recent work, with my mentor Nagarajan Natarajan we proposed a approach which merges GAR (Generation Augmented Retrieval) and RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) [Arora et al., 2023b]. We utilize an LLM as a meta-controller of a retriever which attempts to rewrite queries in order to adapt it to the retriever’s strengths.
