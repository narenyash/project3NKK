My goal as a researcher is to improve our ability to prevent errors in programs. I would like to pursue a PhD in computer science to continue making progress towards this objective. The approach I have focused on in my research is providing static guarantees for programs by 1) describing programs through type systems, and 2) formally proving properties about these programs. In the rest of this statement, I share the experiences that have shaped my approach as well as directed my interests towards programming language (PL) theory, type theory, and proof assistants.

In the vibrant realm of spring 2023, my fascination was ignited by dependently typed programming languages, specifically Agda—a language that allows for the creation of types that are intrinsically linked to programs. This captivation stemmed from their ability to offer enhanced static assurances for software and serve as a platform for constructing rigorous proofs. In my quest to master Agda as a powerful proof assistant, I immersed myself in the challenging yet rewarding textbook "Programming Languages Foundations in Agda," diligently studying it in my personal hours. Penned in Agda with a vision to make complex concepts more approachable for students, this resource served as my guide on an enlightening journey.

Eager to share my newfound knowledge, I embarked on the creation of an online tutorial focusing on normalization-by-evaluation (NbE), an algorithm pivotal in implementing a type checker for languages equipped with dependent types. In this comprehensive guide, I meticulously detail the process of implementing NbE and substantiate its accuracy through rigorous mathematical proofs. My tutorial, inspired by Andreas Abel’s habilitation thesis, underwent a transformation as part of an advanced graduate course. The process of validating the correctness of NbE introduced me to logical relations—a captivating area I continued to explore under the tutelage of Prof. Stephanie Weirich.

Making Logical Relations More Relatable (Summer 2023) Logical relations are a versatile tool for proving properties about programs, such as the static guarantee that a well-typed program is terminating. Although the use of proof assistants to teach topics in PL has become increasingly common, there are not enough mechanized tutorials on logical relations. Prof. Weirich and I conjecture this is because proofs by logical relations are known to be especially tedious to mechanize in a proof assistant. In a mechanization, the many additional lemmas required can easily obscure the technique. Although several tools have been developed to aid PL researchers in mechanizing proofs by logical relations, these are aimed towards researchers already familiar with the technique. This motivated us to develop a proof that can be used to introduce logical relations in a mechanized setting. We show that if a term in the lambda calculus can be assigned a type, its evaluation is well-defined. This is a well-known result; what is notable about our proof is that it avoids reasoning about substitutions. Substitution lemmas are often the most tedious part of mechanizing a proof by logical relations. Without substitution lemmas, our proof can be mechanized while keeping logical relations as the main emphasis. I was the first author of a paper we wrote presenting the proof in Agda. It serves as a mechanized tutorial on logical relations; we plan to submit it to ICFP 2024 as a functional pearl. In the future, I would like to continue studying logical relations and their applications in proving static guarantees for programs. I worked on one example of such a proof for an extension to call-by-push-value (CBPV).

In Fall 2023, I immersed myself in the intricate world of Call-By-Push-Value (CBPV) computational modeling, collaborating with Professor Weirich and fellow researchers to explore its potential beyond mere program behavior description, such as printing. Our collective findings revealed that CBPV is equally suited for delving into coeffects, a less-discussed yet crucial aspect of programming—the environmental demands a program imposes, such as resource usage. Coeffects, being the polar counterparts to effects, seemed ripe for exploration within the realm of CBPV, and this theme formed the backbone of our OOPSLA 2024-submitted paper.

The manuscript offers an innovative type-and-effect system designed specifically for CBPV, meticulously tracking the impact of computations at a static level. It also features an operational semantics enhanced with effects to ensure a precise representation of runtime behavior. Among our groundbreaking results is effect soundness: our type-and-effect system accurately bounds the real-time effects of any program.

Our work on the effects system provided valuable motivation for our coeffects exploration, and we present corresponding findings in parallel. My primary contribution to this paper was the development of the effects portion, while I also Agda-mechanized all results related to our effects system.
