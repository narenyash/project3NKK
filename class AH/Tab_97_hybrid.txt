In the realm of Neuro-Symbolic Artificial Intelligence, my thesis project has been an exhilarating journey under the tutelage of Prof. Mausam and Prof. Parag Singla. Our collective ambition: to seamlessly merge combinatorial solvers within deep learning architectures, thereby bestowing neural networks with enhanced reasoning and strategic capabilities.

Over a series of brainstorming sessions with my esteemed advisors, a graduate student collaborator, and extensive literature delving, we identified gaps in a state-of-the-art technique for training neural networks, specifically the incorporation of an Integer Linear Programming (ILP) layer. Inspired by the geometric insight that an ILP instance represents a polytope in high-dimensional space, I proposed a groundbreaking margin-based loss function for training hybrid neural-ILP systems.

This innovative approach circumvents the necessity of an ILP solver during the forward pass, thereby addressing the primary bottleneck present in prior work. The core concept involves transmuting the combinatorial search problem of ILP into a binary classification dilemma, capitalizing on training guidance. Our framework demonstrates superior performance across various tasks compared to all baselines, including achieving 98% accuracy for visual sudoku, surpassing the neural-only baseline's 71%, and outshining the top neuro-symbolic baseline's 18%.

Our work received a warm welcome at NeurIPS 2022 [3].

Research in Distributed Systems. My first research project was on verifying distributed programs for communication deadlocks, with Prof. Subodh Sharma. While the deadlock detection problem is NP-hard in general, real-world programs exhibit redundancies which can be exploited for faster verification. I developed a two-fold framework to (i) decompose the program into independently-verifiable communication epochs and (ii) prune away symmetric parts of the search space when looking for deadlocks (via a SAT solver). I proved soundness and completeness results, establishing the correctness of the approach. We obtained orders-of-magnitude reduction in verification times and were able to scale to larger programs and higher degrees of parallelism than prior art. This work was accepted at ASE 2022 [4], a top venue in software engineering. Earlier, I presented it at the IARCS SAT+SMT Workshop 2020.
